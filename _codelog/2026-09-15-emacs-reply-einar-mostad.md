Hello Einar,

I am writing this as a public letter in response to the blog post you
published on 2026-09-13 with the title [An apology to Protesilaos
Stavrou and David Wilson](https://einar.codeberg.page/an-apology-to-david-wilson-and-protesilaos-stavrou.html).

I accept the apology, though I think there is no need for one. I would
personally not delete anything. Instead, I would add a link on top to
point to the newer publication, plus some explanation. This teaches
each of us to not take everything too seriously and to not pull the
trigger before considering the case.

At any rate, I am fine with your choice.

Text is an especially difficult medium of communication because we do
not exchange non-verbal cues. It is thus easy to imagine something
which is not there and to then attribute bad intent to its author. For
example, I find myself laughing at the way I formulated a certain
phrase, though I can never communicate as much. Even if I write how I
feel and/or what I am doing in the moment, it does not have the same
effect.

Moreover, we may write something whose context is fully in our head
yet is not known to the reader. Misunderstandings may thus arise.

To your point, I agree with you and have stated as much many times in
video and in writing. To me, the source of truth for learning Emacs is
the manual. Whatever users do to discover what Emacs has to offer, the
manual remains the definitive resource.

I consider my publications complementary material, to the extent that
they are informative. They effectively are a demonstration or reminder
of what can be done. Though they are neither exhaustive at the time
they are published nor current for every future version of Emacs. The
viewer must thus be mindful to consult the official documentation.

Furthermore, Emacs is bigger than me. Even when I present my packages
and/or ELisp code I have developed, I am always building on top of a
corpus of work that far exceeds what I could produce in a lifetime. I
mention this sometimes, but I cannot repeat it for everything I do
because then I will become tiresome. Everyone should remember what is
our common good in this community: _Emacs_.

Finally, I have warned at length against the attitude of taking
shortcuts in [Reply to @linkarzu about 'What I Need From Emacs'](https://protesilaos.com/codelog/2026-07-31-re-linkarzu-what-i-need-from-emacs/)
(2026-07-31). What I wrote on this topic is in relation to Emacs
though applies to life in general. In short, we arrive quickly where
we want only when we move slowly.

All the best,  
Protesilaos (or simply "Prot")
