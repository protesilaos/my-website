---
title: "Games: Bravely Default II"
excerpt: "A Japanese Role Playing Game with great gameplay."
---

_Bravely Default II_ is a modern Japanese Role Playing Game that pays
tribute to the artstyle of JRPGs of yore (**spoilers throughout**). As
is the case with practically every entry in this genre, you are in
control of a bunch of kids, or nerds, or nerdy kids, or you get what I
am saying, who have no business being stalwart warriors yet somehow
are the world's only hope of survival.

The _Bravely Default II_ characters are colourful and the land looks
as pretty as a watercolour painting. Good guys talk with a British
accent, while even the bad ones are masters of etiquette. I am
surprised there was no 5 o'clock kap-à-tee at any point in the
adventure. Or, if there was, I missed it because us Greek savages only
understand café frappé and have no sensitivity for that milky tea
gimmick. And, of course, the dragons have Welsh-sounding names. This
is the most believable part. Wales is truly inhabited by dragons. I
know from experience. Just tell them they are English and see them
breath fire!

It looks adorable. Yet underneath that thin layer of kawaii lies a
primordial evil bent to subsume the entirety of existence. This is the
final boss. It is the generic "darkness" that has no personality or
backstory whatsoever. Sure, we must drive away the darkness with
extreme prejudice because I need plenty of sunshine over here to take
irresistibly attractive selfies. But games, especially of such high
technical acumen, can do better. Compare this to _Tales of Berseria_,
which has a nuanced antagonist and an overall interesting dynamic
between the main characters: <https://protesilaos.com/games/2026-06-11-tales-of-berseria/>.

Generally, _Bravely Default II_ is not strong on characterisation.
Where it excels is in its gameplay, specifically, the battle system.
Fights are turn-based, as is typically the case with JRPGs. The
titular _Bravely_ and _Default_ mechanics form a credit system where
you can play future turns in present time (Bravely) or defend and
stock up your turns to spend them at a later point (Default). The
credit system works on a per-character basis. One character can, for
example, perform an attack three times at once and then just hang
around doing nothing for another three turns while the rest are
holding on to dear life on their normal one-action-per-turn basis.

It is simple and super effective! In the common case, this system lets
you blitz through random enemy encounters. Go full _Bravely_ on the
monsters and you will most probably consign them to oblivion before
they have a chance to fight back. A single turn is enough to win the
battle, especially once your party grows in skill.

The _Bravely_ and _Default_ mechanics get much more interesting once
you invest the time to specialise in the various "jobs" on offer. Each
job allows your characters to learn skills they can bring to the
battle arena. Many jobs complement each other nicely and can affect
how turns are played out. You can, for example, have two fighters
maximise the damage output with _Bravely_ and then two spellcasters
render everyone immune to all types of attack for a few turns. As
such, you do not have to suffer during that downtime. Do it right and
even the most fearsome boss will not stand a chance against you.

In addition to its fun and strategic gameplay, _Bravely Default II_
also has settings to make fights more tolerable. You can control how
often they occur and how fast they are played out. I like this because
most fights require virtually no skill to be overcome and there is
nothing interesting happening in them.

By contrast, the average JRPG opts for realism on this front:
everything is a grindfest. There are random enemy encounters
everywhere that take forever to overcome, the overworld has as much
depth as a theme park, cities give the illusion of choice while your
life still is on rails, and nobody is interested in doing that inane
fetch quest they have for you. _Bravely Default II_ understands it is
an otherwise shallow game, so it has the decency to provide options to
cut through the nonsense.

And, to be fair, the story is okay overall. There are nice parts to it
and high points that can keep you invested until the end. I enjoyed
it, while recognising that it is nothing groundbreaking either.

In conclusion, I have to highlight the exceptional soundtrack composed
by _Revo_. Combined with the visuals and the underlying mechanics,
they make _Bravely Default II_ a piece of fine craftspersonship all
around. Do not listen to the soundtrack if you have not played the
game though. Do it after the fact to relive memorable moments, such as
the intensity you felt while powering through the phase when the
_Urgent Tidings_ piece was playing.
