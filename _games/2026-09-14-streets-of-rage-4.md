---
title: "Game: Streets of Rage 4"
excerpt: "A fine entry in the beat-em-up genre."
---

_Streets of Rage 4_ is a game that hates you (**spoilers below**). I
do not mean the general "you", the one that keeps your defences down
because of how impersonal it is. No! I mean you in particular who is
reading this. I know your name, where you live, and have already
talked to your parents about this. But before I explain why this game
treats you scornfully, let me give you some context to better
appreciate what the fuss is about.

Before gaming at home became the norm, and before 1GB of RAM had the
price of a heart transplant, video games were played at arcades on
machines that were operated with coins. You would insert a coin and
get a go at the game. When you would lose or complete the game, the
machine would ask for another coin. Simple and super effective! At the
end of your session, you had the chance to input three characters,
representing your name, which would be displayed on a leaderboard
screen alongside your total points. Those waiting in line could thus
learn who holds the high score.

Inputting those characters was an adventure unto itself. Unlike our
fancy modern gadgets with full-sized keyboards, key chords timed in
milliseconds, and layered combos, the arcade machines expected you to
cycle from A to Z by tapping a button that was already hard to press.
Picking a letter would lock it in and move you to the next selection.
A mistake could not be undone. In a civilised world, that would not be
a problem. You would take your sweet time to cycle all the way back
from, say, R to P and then ever so carefully repeat the steps to
inscribe "PRT" or something. But ours is a barbaric world, my friend!

The arcade is a social place. You are there together with a bunch of
ne'er-do-wells . There are no gaming chairs to make you feel
comfortable nor hidden lights distracting you from your solitude. You
are typically standing close to the machine you intend you use,
playing mind games on the guy whose turn it is to make an attempt at
reaching the top of the leaderboard.

If your friend gets the high score, you do what besties are there for:
punch the bastard ever so gently to make him mistype his name, of
course. We do not want him to become overconfident, do we now? The
Greeks have warned us of hubris and all that. But we also do not need
to be savages, so we let him have it sometimes, i.e., if he is below
our score.

Many of the games at the arcade were in the fighting or beat-em-up
genres. _Streets of Rage 4_ pays homage to the latter. In terms of
gameplay, it does an excellent job. For a while I felt that Capcom's
_Cadillacs and Dinosaurs_ got a remake. Though playing the game at
home will never give you the same vibes as what you would experience
at the arcade. Which is why this game hates you, amigo.

_Streets of Rage 4_ reminds you that you are at home. That is already
a terrible fate. Then, you realise that you cannot punch your friend
anymore. What a sad world we live in! It also features prominently a
four-player co-op mode. No, not to make you invite people over for a
game or two. Don't be silly! It does it to highlight the fact that you
have no friends.

Beside that, you will soon notice that everybody on the titular
streets is maxed out in all possible ways. They have more muscles than
those known to anatomists and more curves than those anticipated by
that whole "curvature of spacetime" mumbo-jumbo that physicists dabble
in.

Not only are they all cooler than you, they also have more free time
than you ever will. You expend your vitality doing some unfulfilling
job while they get to chill on the streets---and they even have
coin-ops there to recreate the arcade experience!

Okay, I am not being serious. This is a fine game. I had a good time.
As for the plot, it is a generic cartoon villain story of the bad guys
wanting to control the world. The good guys are unmemorable
vigilantes. I already forgot how they even look, which is fantastic.

For me, this is a strength of gaming. I do not want profound
characterisation and interwoven backstories when the premise is that I
run around kicking people in the stomach. This is the same point I
made about _Borderlands 3_, namely, I like it when games do not take
themselves too seriously. It makes for a fun and easygoing life.
