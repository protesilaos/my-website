In times of instability, one seeks safety. In times of stability, one
yearns for adventure. In times of plenty, one tolerates a lot as they
think of all the opportunities available to them. In times of
scarcity, one becomes suspicious of what they cannot control.

It is how humanity oscillates between extremes, committing mistakes of
past generations while also learning something in the process, which
it incorporates in philosophical inquiry, religious doctrine, artistic
expression, and jurisprudence.

Material conditions and expectations derived therefrom help explain
how political movements develop their libertarian and securitarian
propensities. Cultural trends exhibit the same tendencies, from an
explosion of experimentation and defiance to a want for purity and the
concomitant distillation of the essentials.

"Libertarian" here refers to an outlook rather than a specific school
of thought which may identify by the same name. It is the view of the
world where the individual is above the collective. From there follows
the notion of inalienable rights, i.e. those which no group, no body,
no authority, can take away from the person.

This is the principle, at least, because in practice there is no such
thing as an inalienable right: it is an instituted reality that can
always be interpreted anew, given the appropriate triggers or
prevailing conditions.

"Securitarian" is the attitude of prioritising the collective over the
individual. It consists in the desire for restraint in order to
control the factors that contribute to the individual as well as the
social experience. At one level this is expressed as a check on the
individual's own patterns of behaviour: to conform to guidelines that
are pro-social. At the collective level, it acknowledges the
importance of hierarchy, which is how individuals learn to expend
their vitality without destroying themselves and their society in the
process.

## The drift towards self-contradiction

Both libertarianism and securitarianism have manifestations of
self-contradiction that results in tyranny.

- Extreme libertarianism will be pushing against its own notion of
  inalienable rights, because it would be asking for freedom at
  increasingly fractional scales to the point where no authority can
  ever be conceived as superior to the individual and thus no right
  may be instituted as such. Institution presupposes at least some
  degree of collectivist ethos: to agree, at the very least, on the
  shared values and on the convention that some rights---and which
  ones---are inalienable and by whom may they be safeguarded and
  enforced.
- Extreme securitarianism exhibits the same phenomenon of
  self-contradiction. Too much control turns into insecurity already
  at the individual level, where the person is afraid to experience
  something different out of fear of losing the world they think they
  have. Collectively, too much security grows so suspicious of the
  indeterminate that it begins to prosecute people over increasingly
  trivial things. The analogous of an auto-immune disease.

A common mistake politically minded people make is to think that the
"bad ones" coalesce in one specific ideology or spectrum of views. For
example, leftists think that only the far-right is the bane of
society. Those on the far-right, in turn, will demonise leftists. And
so on. The truth is that every view can be taken to its extreme, at
which point it degenerates into the opposite of its stated ambition.

This is not a matter of politics alone. It is a fact of life in
general, which the Greeks understood as the world of admixture, of
there being no pure good or evil but latent expressions of both on a
spectrum of degree. Water is the source of life. Drink moderate
amounts and you are healthy, ceteris paribus. Drink ridiculous
quantities and you will die of poisoning. It is not water that is
deleterious though, but the degree of consumption. The same is true
for everything we do: too much or too little will be harmful.

Degeneracy, then, and by extension the drift towards tyranny is a
matter of degree else application, not category. Too much freedom
results in non-freedom the same way too little freedom does. Too much
security engenders insecurity exactly how the absense of security
does.

## A demanding work-in-progress

The other common mistake is to believe that insitutions are inherently
robust. A well-defined insittutional arrangement, the thinking goes,
will keep the society going along those lines. Having a strong
constitutional order which safeguards representative democracy, for
example, will supposedly guarantee democratic norms even while people
are apolitical and largely ignorant of affairs beyond their particular
milieu.

Erosion will thus be ignored or underestimated. It will happen slowly
until it accelerates all of a sudden.

No matter the setup, institutions are upheld on a continuous basis by
people. They exist and they perform their tutelary function for as
long as there is a critical mass of agents of action working in their
favour. Put differently, they are true if we believe them to be true
and if we behave in accordance with that belief. Else they are nothing
but empty promises, waiting to be instrumentalised by ruthless
aparatchiks and aspiring overlords.

This is, after all, how the aforementioned degeneracy develops. It is
expressed as a zeal for ideological correctness, which becomes a
pattern of decision-making that errs on the side of over-correction
instead of caution.

Though even with prudent guidance, the fact of the matter remains that
the order is a work-in-progress. There will be trials and there will
be errors. There will be cases of too little and others of too much.
What matters is that the people prioritise common sense over
ideological purity.

Once ideology becomes its own goal, it leads to the destruction of
what it purports to serve. Ideologies are frameworks to focus minds on
a given project, which works towards the preservation or instantiation
and subsequent protection of one or more values. It thus is a tool in
the hands of a political movement. Absent the values or measurable
progress towards that end, which we can understand as the lived
experience of people, the tool itself is not worthwhile.

## The degeneracy of the opportunist

Among those who commit to overcorrection are the purists of the
ideology as well as the neophytes who join ranks on some opportunist
premise. Both can push towards extremism. The latter group is
especially prone to exaggerate because it does not have the requisite
acculturation to appreciate nuances in the views it has espoused. A
zealous and capable student will learn all the rules and how they
apply, though it is only the master who has an intuition of when to
suspend the application of those rules.

To be concrete in the interest of explaining this concept. For roughly
the last ten years, the Western world experienced an overcorrection
towards libertarianism, of the sort here considered, which was largely
carried out by opportunists and liars. It now moves in the opposite
extreme of securitarianism.

One can attest to the times when they met, for example, some
heterosexual person cosplaying as LGBT+ under the guise of a bisexual
identity. That alone is fine: it can make people feel accepted and
thus be comfortable in their reality. Though it would not stop there.
Those same pretenders would be vociferous about how strongly they
protect their LGBT+ friends and would become intolerant of even the
most innocuous hesitation and call for careful deliberation. Same for
all-white, upper class liberals pretending to defend minorites in
abject poverty, while they themselves were enjoying another lavish
meal.

Pretenders and wannabees also exist in conservative groups, where some
new convert to a religion, for example, is eager for a holy war and
has an unchecked propensity for fundamentalism.

## The importance of leadership

In all cases, the guardrails come in the form of strong leadership.
This is a matter of attitude among those who occupy positions of
authority within the given group. One must be able to say "no" and to
push back forcefully against the drift towards extremism, in the same
way a parent has to be decisive in order to keep their child's desire
for endless sweets in check.

Leadership consists in courage and competence. Competence absent
courage is the psychosynthesis of the arivist: the person who will not
stand for any principle and who will only tend to their own interest.
Courage absent competence devolves into trigger-happiness.

Leaders start from the family and extend outward. If, as a parent, you
cannot call out your child's bullshit, if you give them everything
they want and always yield to their capricious tricks, then at the
social level we have a situation where an entire generation of
entitled people has not developed an intuition of where the boundaries
are. It lacks emotional maturity and is incapable of reasonableness.
Same if you are not correcting the tendency for bullying in your
child. And so on.

Same goes for teachers, football coaches, priests, and all tutelary
figures. If you yourself are not embodying the values you represent
or, at the very least, are making real effort towards that end, then
you are setting a negative example. You are part of the problem.

You cannot be a health professional who is a smoker and a drunkard,
for example. Your deeds contradict your claims. All you communicate is
corruption and its attendant unreliability. You cannot be a spiritual
leader who advocates frugality yet succumbs to the greed of the
moneyman. And so on.

Society will degenerate once its tutelary figures become feeble in
their character and ineffectual in their practices. It is a matter of
personal responsibility to be excellent and to have a sense of duty
towards the commons. Commitment to the highest standard at the level
of the singular person is how society as a whole protects against its
own degenerative tendencies.

A crisis, then, of institutions falling part and the civilisation
declining, necessarily is a crisis of values. The society loses sense
of what the common good is and how it may be safeguarded. It is then
when ideology takes on a life of its own, to serve its own telos, to
create a class of pretenders or professional ideologues, who exploit
their respective movement as a vehicle for their own aggrandisement.

"Crisis" comes from the Greek word for "judgement". It is a moment of
discerning what is worthwhile and where one has made mistakes. Such is
the time to set aside petty sentimentalities to sternly assess one's
prevailing conditions. Always with the understanding that whatever
course of action they commit to, they will have to remain vigilant to
course-correct when the prevailing material conditions change without
overdoing it. This is the mark of prudence, of common sense in practice.
