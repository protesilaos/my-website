I recently spoke at a philosophical symposium that was organised by an
acquaintance in my region. This was a small circle of people whom I
know. Other than me, there were 9 men and 5 women present. 3 people
were of Balkan background, which I will keep generic, and the rest
were Greek Cypriots.

What follows is a reformulation and combination of comments I made
over the course of five hours on the wider theme of values in the
context of social dynamics and historical trends.

The comments here are broad strokes which necessarily take a
bigger-picture view of historical phenomena. They are theories of
event sequences rather than descriptions of individual cases.

I did not even bother to record a video of my comments because I know
that what I cover is sensitive. Many people are not prepared to learn
how their world actually is. They will call me "cynical", which means
"descriptive, indeed, but I do not like the truth you are describing,
so I call you names".

I am increasingly more cynical in this way because I am concerned that
niceties will not take us anywhere and we are running out of time.

## The social and ethnic underpinnings of early Christianity

Early Christianity spread out of the wider region that encompasses
Palestine and Syria. I am calling this region "broader Syria", without
implying however that everybody was Syrian in the modern sense. I
could have used "broader Palestine", "Western Asia", "Near East", and
the like, but you get the idea. This is just in the interest of
brevity.

Broader Syria was a place that was dominated by foreign empires in the
centuries prior, first by the Persians, then by Alexander (so-called
"Great"), and then by the Romans.

Every empire creates its own petty bourgeois class. It consists of
those who are beneficiaries of the regime. The most successful among
them get close to the locus of power and so the rest have the wish to
climb the social ladder accordingly. They live in urban centres and
their world-view is defined by eclecticism, namely, the cherry-picking
of profoundly different beliefs without committing to any of the
corresponding worldviews.

The modern equivalent, if you need an analogy, is the New Age
spiritual person who likes Christ but detests Christianity, does a
little bit of yoga and knows about the chakra, admires Sufi poetry, is
inspired by the vitality of the Greek gods, talks about the conscience
of Gaia, engages in mindfulness meditation like the Buddhists, delves
into psychedelics, will visit some Ayahuasca resort, i.e. yet another
theme park, to cosplay for a few days as an indigenous person of the
Andes, and does all that in the backdrop of an unmistakably
consumerist ethos of capitalism.

This bourgeois figure is the kind of person that gave us the travesty
of Greco-Buddhism in the region that is modern-day Pakistan and
Afghanistan. The idea is that "basically", the Olympic spirit of
struggle through vigour, symbolised by the Olympic Games, and Buddhist
monasticism are the same. Sure, we can always find similarities and
interesting points, but flattening the differences is how we end up
with New-Age-y bullshit. You want monasticism, go become a Buddhist.
You want to power through this life, then do what the Greeks do, and
so on for all other beliefs.

It is also how Rome developed its Pantheon. The Romans would absorb
religious narratives from the places they would conquer in order to
appropriate and control them. This was their way to legitimise their
rule but also to dictate the norms of future religiosity. In so doing,
they were necessarily debasing their own elegance of thought in favour
of a hogde-podge of increasingly meaningless spirituality.

The destiny of the petty bourgeois world is that of rampant corruption
through materialism. This is because it lacks purity of values. Theirs
is an "all numbers win" sort of outlook which is underpinned by the
illusion of permanent affluence. It thus does not have strictness and
discipline embedded in its quotidian affairs, which make it prone to
the short-term impulses of instant gratification.

[ In the talk I elaborated on "pornification", but I will not cover it
  here because I have a complete essay on it: [About structure, the little things, and critical thinking](https://protesilaos.com/commentary/2026-10-01-structure-little-things-critical-thinking/) (2026-10-01). ]

From there we get moral decadence. The high society, which is already
defined by its wealth, will be caught up in intrigues over power and
sex diplomacy (e.g. think of Cleopatra). It will become increasingly
materialistic since one rich person can only out-compete others of the
same class through more money and power. This is the precondition for
greed, which engenders the worship of the money-god. From the ruling
elite, the worship of the money-god trickles down to the petty
bourgeois, until it becomes the dominant de facto religion.

Much of this describes the modern Western world, by the way. But I
will let you connect those dots yourselves as I continue with early
Christianity.

Against this backdrop of excess, the natives of broader Syria were
predominantly simple country folk. Jesus was speaking to shepherds and
fishers exactly because those are not fancy jobs that the parvenu are
interested in.

Early Christianity gave the ordinary man, including the slave, a
vision for his class-based emancipation from the tyranny of the Romans
and their petty bourgeois wannabees. It also equipped broader Syria
with a powerful ideological weapon against the wishy-washy worldview
of the Roman elite.

We can thus think of the spread of early Christianity as the effective
cultural shift of the Roman Empire from the West in Rome to the East
in Antioch. Parallel to that ethnic change we have the rise of the
lower classes. Christianity was a decisively political project.

The emperors understood they were losing their grip on power, so they
decided to use the good old tool of cultural appropriation. Only now
they knew that the Pantheon was obsolete. Through ecumenical synods,
emperors started to take control of the emerging dogma that they
helped forge. The centre of the empire also moved from Rome to ancient
Byzantium, then Constantinople (modern Istanbul), as a means of
exerting closer control over broader Syria.

The part of the world stretching from Egypt to western Iran is very
rich and densely populated. Look up the term "fertile crescent".
Compare that to the rocky and in many places barren Greek heartlands
and much of Balkans, for example. The Western empire could only be
rich by controlling this part of the world. And to do that
effectively, you need to also control the Mediterranean, else your
trade routes are in jeopardy. That was true then and remains the case
today.

Christianity became state dogma and official religion. One may think
this means that broader Syria won the battle it initiated. But they
would be mistaken. What actually happened is that Christianity started
becoming the religion of the elite and then their petty bourgeois
copycats, concentrated in the large cities of that era.

It is no coincidence that the simple folk of the officially Christian
Rome on the European continent, who were referred to with the
derogatory term "paganus", literally "villager", were those who held
on to the ancient polytheistic rites. Hence "pagan" became an alias
for polytheist, while "Hellenes" (Έλληνες), the ethnonym of the
Greeks, was meant as a religious characterisation for Greek polytheist
practitioners.

The pagans, literally the villagers, were not following the trends,
they were not up to speed with the latest fashions, and instead kept
their culture pure through the aeons the way most villages do. They
did not have any of the excesses of the petty bourgeois. The Romans
never bothered with the remote settlements because there was no money
to be made there, no loot to be extracted.

The obsolescence of the Roman Pantheon had to lead to the eradication
of paganism, now polytheism, in order to keep the entire project of
Christian officialdom under control. Otherwise, the empire would then
have to deal with the potential rise of the polytheists, ethnically
defined as Greeks, for example, and risk another culture war.

The forced Christianisation of the pagans in Greece under Theodosius
and then Justinian was not a mere change of faith through imperial
edict. It was also the subjugation of the rural populations to the
dominion of the bourgeois.

I see the completion of this process as the effective renewal of the
Roman Empire from the decadent Pantheon to a more pure religion. This
gave it a new lease of life and invigorated its want for absolute
control. Though Christianity could not arrest the greed of the empire
and thus could not stop the descent into materialism. Its original
revolutionary spirit was contained by virtue of becoming the religion
of the elites who imposed their worldview through fire and steel.

## Islam as a renewal of the same demands

Functionally, Islam can be understood as the purification of ideas
that lots of people already have. The Romans took monotheism and
turned it into clumsy polytheism through the ever-problematic doctrine
of the trinity.

Islam rejects this in favour of one absolute God (though Islam still
has elements of Muhammad as a special prophet, but we will not go
there). It also rejects Judaism on the premise that God is for
everyone, not some special tribe. Lots of technicalities follow from
there.

At its core, however, Islam is the same old demand of broader Syria to
assert itself in juxtaposition to the West. It also is class-based in
terms of giving a place for a decent life to the ordinary person.

The class-based emancipatory power of Islam is better understood in
the east, where it took hold in the Indian subcontinent among those at
the bottom of the caste system.

Modern India has close to 200 million Muslims. Add Pakistan and
Bangladesh for similar numbers each to complete the picture. Let me
remind you that the Greeks are 10 million and rapidly shrinking
because we do not have children anymore. I will return to this theme
later.

In those countries, Islam gave a place to the ordinary person in the
same way that early Christianity did to Roman slaves.

The decline of Islam in places it once controlled is analogous to what
I have already described. For example, you can look up the term
"odelisque". Those are Western paintings of concubines or whores, if
you prefer, in the Ottoman Sultan's palace.

Western people, which you can already imagine as bourgeoisie in their
usual eclectic mode, were fascinated by the Near Orient and in their
mind fancied those women as something to be celebrated. Kind of how
the modern girlboss on some porn platform is not a whore to be derided
but a businesswoman to be glorified. Of course, this speaks to Western
corruption then and now, though it also shows us that the Sultan was
no longer a figure of Islamic authority but the effective equivalent
of a decadent Roman leader.

The collapse of the Ottoman Empire is consistent with this trend of
affluence eroding the cultural guardrails of a society. Interestingly,
the Greeks who fought for independence were those who earlier eras
would have described as paganus. They lived in abject poverty on rocky
terrain. They were literally stealing from the landed gentry to
survive, hence the term Klephts (literally "thieves").

There were no Klephts in parts of the Ottoman lands that were wealthy.
There was no Klepht movement in Istanbul and the Western coast of
modern Turkey, for example, which at the time had plenty of Greeks.
The Pontic Greeks in the Black Sea were also not particularly active
because they were fundamentally having a relatively good time with the
regime.

We see again class playing a key role in rebellion. Where class finds
an ethnic basis, it leads to a sustained revolution. Class alone is
not enough because there is no organic solidarity between people on
the basis of mere ideology. They have to speak the same language and
have the same values.

The nationalism of the 18th century can thus be understood as a modern
secular theology that galvanised the enslaved masses in the same way
Christianity and Islam did under their own prevailing conditions.
Though later nationalism turned into the iron fist of capitalism, in
the form of fascism at its extreme end, but also of picking so-called
"national champions" and of engaging in protectionist practices in the
service of vested interests.

The other lesson to be learnt here is that Islam may be austere in
principle but Muslims are not immune to corruption. Empires that are
nominally Islamic will have the same fate as all other empires,
namely, an inexorable turn to materialism. At a smaller scale, this is
true for extreme wealth that is concentrated in few hands. Look at the
economic model that Qatar was promoting prior to the current war in
Iran, for example. Every so-called "influencer" from the West, which I
would describe more appropriately as a whore, male of female, was
going there to live the materially affluent yet morally bankrupt life.

## Orthodoxy, Catholicism, and Protestantism

I think that Greece historically does not belong to the West, but for
our purposes here I will not bombard you with extra concepts. Consider
Greeks to be Western throughout.

Let me now turn to the Western world to comment on similar trends
therein. In the Easternmost areas we have Orthodoxy which is quite
conservative in its doctrine. However, the Church has historically
played a key political role in empires as the enforcer of the elite's
will, including during the time of the Ottomans, though it also is a
big business nowadays. Still, Orthodoxy is conservative in principle
and so could be used as the basis of a fundamentalist reformation.

The Catholics have a more liberal conduct. Their practice is that "all
the good ones fit in". Their various orders emphasise different parts
of the faith. In some ways they are all united, but you can always
think of different emphases as facilitating distinct modes of living.

You see the "all the good ones fit in" in how Catholicism incorporated
the art of the Renaissance. To the untrained eye, the Renaissance is a
return to the discipline of the athletic body, as epitomised by the
ancient Greek world. But upon closer inspection you will notice boys
whose pose is pleasing only to the eyes of a pederast, meaning that
they typically expose their ass, and, generally, adult bodies that
have no vigour in their stance. They look like objects.

So Catholic Europe took the awe out of Greek aesthetics. Awe, to be
clear, is the combination of admiration and fear. You should admire an
athletic body, for example, because it is not easy to build it up. And
you should also fear it because it is supremely capable and thus
potentially extremely dangerous. You cannot have it otherwise. When
this is combined with strong ethics you have the perfect match, of
course.

Catholic Europe became so corrupt at the ecclesiastic level that
Protestantism emerged as a necessary corrective to its practices. In
its core idea, Protestantism is a call for purity of varying degrees,
of a return to basics, of a rejection of the notion that "all the good
ones fit in" so liberally espoused by the Catholics.

Protestantism, however, combined its puritan ethos with dominant
elements of individualism. It thus created the conditions for the
modern Western brands of individualism, all of which have the same
effect of eroding the extended family and the family altogether,
undermining the moral guardrails, and promoting materialism.

[ Again, I explain this in the talk in my allusions to "structure".
  See the essay I linked to earlier which covers this theme at greater
  length. ]

Protestantism cannot be the force of renewal that the Christian world
may benefit from because individualism is the bane of society
long-term. I am of the view that Catholicism is a lost cause as well,
unless we are talking about a new order within it that is puritan in
spirit. Orthodoxy is the best placed to take on that mantle from a
doctrinal point of view. Though we have a practical problem of
demographics and overall influence. The Orthodox countries have small
populations and relatively weak economies, while Russia is a pariah in
the Western world. As such, Orthodoxy will remain limited in its
influence.

The next 30 years will have a profound effect on our future. I say "30
years" because I am thinking of demographics. If women who today are
young adults do not take motherhood seriously, then the ethnic
backbone of our societies is dead. We have an expiry date. I am not
even considering the women already in their 30s, even though they are
fertile, because ideologically they still live in a fantasy land where
they can be princesses without giving anything back to the world.

## Extended families and the petty bourgeois

Which brings me to the big topic of the extended family. The extended
family consists of parents, children, grandparents, and many relatives
living close to each other as a community. I call it "socialism at
home", which warms my leftist heart, because it is defined by
interpersonal and intergenerational solidarity. It also is ethnic in
its character, owning to blood ties and shared culture.

Until the mid-20th century Greece and Cyprus that I know enough about
used to have extended families across the entire countryside. My own
clan is the same. This changed with urbanisation in the 70s and 80s to
bring us to the modern calamity we are confronted with.
Villagers---and again we see the traditional, ordinary folk in
action---who used to live in big families moved to the city to become
bourgeoisie themselves. There they were ridiculed by the bourgeoisie
as "Vlachs" (Βλάχοι) or Pontians (Πόντιοι), here a slur analogous to
"paganus". In the process, they grew individualistic, thus destroying
the underpinnings of the extended family and the local community.

Individuals and atomic families in cities cannot raise children. The
logistics are too demanding. Childcare costs a fortune and parenting
is a full-time job on top of the existing stressful life. Grandparents
are consigned to oblivion under the euphemism of "elderly care" and
other relatives are nowhere to be seen. In the city, people live in
fear because they do not have communities around them. They have a few
friends, at best, but they otherwise do not know their neighbours, let
alone those further away.

This material reality of modern city life has reinforced the latent
individualism of the Protestant West which has, in turn, become the
dominant worldview of all Western peoples through their gradual
Americanisation. If you live in a city long enough, you understand
that you can only rely on your own means. You become an individualist
even if ideologically you are committed to some collectivist project
like socialism.

The effect is that the average woman does not have children anymore.
Plural! This is not the fault of women as such, but a systemic
problem, of which individualism, including feminism, is an important
part.

A typical young woman nowadays, say one who has just turned 18, is
looking into several years of university studies followed by several
more years of progressing through some career. By the time she even
thinks of motherhood, she already is in her 30s. Generally, women can
still have children at that age, but they have to be in good shape.
Otherwise there are all sorts of practical challenges involved. So
chances are high that those women will not have children at all.

The alternative for such a woman is to marry upward. To find a man
with enough wealth so that they can jointly roll over the duties of
parenthood to strangers. This ties in to the lifestyle of the petty
bourgeois fellow, because now the chance to have a family is directly
linked to money and the grifter mindset. Family too becomes a
materialistic pay-to-win scheme.

Transferring parenthood duties to strangers has a longer-term impact
on upbringing. Those kids will grow up to have daddy and mommy issues.
They will also be used to being around servants, which will prime them
to aspire towards wealth, thus reinforcing the materialistic bent of
their parents.

This woman in our countries typically is a local from a petty
bourgeois family. She has lived a comfortable life. Her childhood was
dominated by fairytales of princesses and she has generally developed
an aggrandised sense of self-importance. Modern men play a big role in
this because they prioritise appearances instead of ethos, and thus
they reward the girl who is superficially attractive but totally
uninterested in the family instead of the girl who has the maturity,
leadership, and emotional intelligence to be a great mother.

There are many men, myself included, who have no chance of attracting
a woman from this socio-cultural milieu. Those women will not even
look in our general direction. And, if they do look this way, we will
not be able to have a family together because their expectations are
sky-high: meals at fancy restaurants, plenty of vacations, guaranteed
education all through university for the children, if any, connections
with prestigious people. You get the idea. How do you tell such a
woman to live a simple life in the mountains where an extended family
can flourish? You do not.

Now let me connect this with Christianity and social class. I
challenge you to go to church this Sunday and pay close attention to
the people there. It is a parade of opulence. The church itself is so
disgustingly ornate, if you notice, but I will not upset your beliefs.
Look around you. Expensive cars line up outside the temple. Everybody
is clad in luxury. You understand that those people are bourgeoisie
who compete with each other on metrics of materialism.

They are not idiots though. A marginalised person, like me, cannot
kiss up to them and fool them into thinking that we are all equal.
They have a very clear understanding of their place in society. And
they are looking to move upward. To their eyes, the poor are to be
belittled through pity and, more importantly, to be kept out of sight.
Sunday at the church is, in effect, networking for the well-off.

Sure, there is Jesus and all the rest somewhere there, but if you are
not seeing the social dynamics then you are not paying attention.

## The toxic cocktail of childless men in poverty

Now I will tell you about the new underclass that awaits for an
ideology to turn it into the scourge of god. No, I am not
exaggerating. There exist in our societies the conditions for a repeat
of history.

This underclass is the group of people who are male, single, without
connections, and poor. Right now they expend their energy on being
conformist, i.e. on kissing up in order to improve their condition.
They do so by insisting on living in urban centres, despite rent being
prohibitively expensive. So expensive, in fact, that they have no
disposable income to meaningfully commit to any of the activities that
a city provides.

Their calculus is that they will earn a better income at some point
and thus attract a wife to start a family. I think this is a failing
strategy because the wider economic trends favour the further
consolidation of wealth upward, while the kind of wife they have in
mind is the girl I mentioned earlier who is looking for something more
comfortable and/or who is not into children anyhow.

Historically, I explained how early Christianity and Islam galvanised
the underclass to fight for a different world. I think the same can
happen again.

Consider the following from the perspective of such a man. I include
myself among them, minus the part of being a conformist for reasons
you already know from my lifestyle:

- You are still strong and capable.
- You may have some resources but not enough to attract the modern
  local woman in your society; a woman who expects to be treated like
  a princess just because of how important she thinks she is.
- You want to have a family, but you know you are not getting it with
  a local woman unless you are very rich.
- The local women will not even look in your general direction, while
  your society treats you with contempt, as they do at the church.
  Kissing up to them only increases the disrespect they have for you.
  You are a loser in their eyes and you are not changing that by being
  slavish. That only makes you more of a loser.
- Muslim communities are largely pro-family.
- Muslim communities are the fastest growing ones in your country.
- Muslim communities are of immigrant background and so they have a
  better understanding of the same dynamics of marginalisation as you
  do.

Against this backdrop, my expectation is that for a lot of people of
this social profile, conversion to Islam is an appealing proposition.
Anecdotally, I already know of white men with no connection to the
Middle East whatsoever who have taken that route. It is a rejection of
the dominant group's excesses, an abolition of the need to kiss up,
and the path to a possible wife and family.

Not every convert will get this, of course, because there is more to
marriage than ideology, though the incentive is there.

You now understand how family, class, and religion coalesce. What is
missing from this equation is ethnicity. This you get once you factor
in the fact that Muslim communities in many parts are extended
families, which are always ethnically defined. So someone who marries
into such a group is effectively absorbed into the ethnicity as well.

I will close with this personal example.

Where I live there is a small village nearby. The locals are old
people. Every few weeks we learn about another death. Old people do
not do culture. Simply none. They have no interest in learning new
things. They do not have the fitness for sport. They are too tired to
listen to you, so forget about philosophical meetings either. And I
will not even mention dancing and singing... In short, a society of
old people is culturally dead even though there still are humans
around. I call this "zombification", which is where we are right at
the moment in many parts of the West, owning to depopulation and
demographic decline in general.

In this village, there are migrants who do all the work. Men tend to
the fields and women take care of the elderly. There also are refugees
from Syria, of Muslim background, who are growing in number because
every woman has multiple children. Those mothers are young, so they
may have a few more kids.

Imagine this continues for 15 years, which is not a lot of time, and
is the expected turn of events. Today's kids of Syrian descent will be
adults. The old locals will be long dead. Syrians will control the
village. The few younger locals who remain will have no option but to
convert to Islam or they will abandon their ancestral lands to be
Christians or whatever in some other country.

I have given you a very big picture view of topics that can be
discussed at great length on their own. My intention is to focus your
attention on the dynamics of the underclass versus the dominant
classes, of competing ethnic interests within empires, and of religion
as the imposition of a new normal for social affairs.

Finally, for the Greeks who are here, but also for the others because
your nations are the same, I will repeat: if those 30 years do not
bring radical change in mentality the zombification will eliminate us
culturally and biologically. Those who do not see it will have the
same fate as all other corrupt empires, namely, they will be dominated
and annihilated by the more vital and the more austere social groups.

Life is simple in this way.
