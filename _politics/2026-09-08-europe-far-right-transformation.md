Writing for _The Guardian_, Jon Henley notes that [Europe’s centre fears nationalist wave after AfD victory but only has itself to blame](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/07/afd-germany-far-right-saxony-anhalt-nationalist-wave-europe-analysis).
Jon explains how AfD's win in Saxony-Anhalt should not be overstated
yet it still is a telltale sign of things to come. The trends are
there and they are painting a clear picture.

Already in 2019, I wrote that the [extreme right is the new
right](https://protesilaos.com/politics/2019-04-23-far-right-new-right/).
Yes, I see the future in its essential form because by thinking things
through I understand the internal structure of phenomena, the
"physics" if you will, beyond the day-to-day particularities. When I
say something, I have considered it carefully. I do not do tricks and
gimmicks.

My argument then, as it is now, is that liberalism, expressed by this
political centre, effectively is a luxury that society tolerates under
conditions of abundance. It stops being appealing when the constraints
appear to be tighter, at which point some form of intolerance takes
centre stage.

Liberalism in day-to-day politics has a strong correlation with
political correctness and tokenistic virtue. It is performative
because it sounds smart and caring. The European Union, in particular,
has a clear rhetoric of moral superiority in its touting of the
so-called "European values", which are enshrined in Article 2 of the
Treaty on European Union and then elaborated in Article 3, as well as
all sorts of everyday self-righteous talk.

Virtue signalling, however, does not have strong principles as its
actors are principally concerned with staying in power. My follow-up
argument, then, which I shall elaborate at length herein, is that
those who today shed crocodile tears over the drift towards the
far-right will blithely be implementing far-right policies tomorrow,
having performed the requisite mental gymnastics.

## Short-term thinking and disconcerted action

The radicalisation we are experiencing now is the natural evolution of
the 2008+ financial crisis. We have been moving from a crisis of money
to a crisis of values. This is because the financial crisis is the
cumulative result of many smaller frailties of ethical character,
which revolve around short-term thinking with its attendant greed.

From the perspective of decision-making, short-term thinking
necessarily engenders disconcerted action. The consequences of prior
actions must be undone or otherwise revised by posterior actions. It
is the "debt" they create, which may be measured in monetary terms
though can also be understood as legacy software that was never
refactored, as policies with perverse incentives that were never
thought through, as wants that were decoupled from one's capacity to
fully realise them. This is true for individuals who act in a
haphazard fashion and are thus not in control of their workflow, as it
is for groups of people and official entities.

Concerted action consists in logically coherent and pragmatic
longer-term sequencing. One action flows from the other, creating the
benign conditions for the next, such that the flow is uninterrupted
and the ulterior end, as originally envisaged, is met.

Disconcerted action, by contrast, is reduced to opportunism. It is
about kicking the can down the road and rolling the problem over to
somebody else. This "somebody else" may be a future version of the
same agent of action or another agent of action altogether. In
dithering, the problems accumulate and the solutions to them are no
longer easy to implement without incurring painful losses. Again, true
for individuals and for groups.

Concretely, disconcerted action on the political front creates
legal-institutional arrangements that amplify tensions, turning power
impulses into systemic pressures and thus local shocks into global
failures. Put differently, there is a transmission mechanism from
elementary patterns of behaviour to broader phenomena.

Money fits into this scheme as a figment of a culture's disposition.
It is an instituted representation of its aspirations. Through the
processes of money creation, money distribution, and money
accumulation, we discern the underlying beliefs of the given society.
In light of short-term thinking, money-seeking takes the form of
individualistic profiteering, of making as much as possible right now
at the expense of others, especially "others" in the future sense. The
logic is the same for the distribution and accumulation of money,
which favours present gains no matter their potential pernicious costs.

Against this backdrop, we can understand the overarching market
incentive of short-term conduct as "you want to jump off the train
which is on a coalition course as late as possible". The reason is
that there is profit to be made in the meantime and there is no
interest in preventing the crash altogether by toning down or
otherwise redoing individual patterns of behaviour: those are not
profitable in the immediate time frame.

## The European Union's sovereignty mismatch

Which brings me to the European Union's disconcerted action in the
post-2008 world. What happened in the EU since then is a series of
half-baked reforms that did not address the design flaws of the Union
in terms of economic governance.

To this day, we have a monetary function, conferred to the European
Central Bank, without a counterparty fiscal function, which would be
given to a European Treasury under the authority of a fully fledged
ministry of finance.

By extension, we do not have a European government, but a bureaucratic
body, the European Commission, which fleshes out and implements the
guidelines that an intergovernmental institution, the European
Council, prescribes. The European Parliament is secondary in
significance and in no way capable of escaping the architectural
constraints it exists in.

These halfhearted reforms were conceived and implemented in accordance
with the historical incrementalism of the European integration
process. This is the mode of conduct whereby one problem is fixed by
creating two new ones, so that those can be addressed in turn, always
with the intent of shifting the locus of power towards the notional
European centre. The post-2008 reforms compounded what already was a
labyrinthine organisation.

In effect, the European Union is a monster of complexity. Its
processes are hard to understand and harder to follow. Through its
very constitution as a political order it obfuscates whatever answer
to the questions of "who governs?" and "where is the locus of power?".

Its decision-making effectively denies the sovereignty of peoples
because of the inherent mismatch between the scopes of application of
the Union as a whole and of national interests in isolation.

There are rules for the system as a whole without the commensurate
accountability structures. Accountability, in its expression as
representative democracy, only exists in full and in direct form along
national lines. There is no equivalent to it for the European
Commission or indeed the European Council when it is acting as a
whole.

The sovereignty mismatch means that we have governance at the EU level
without government, state sovereignty without popular sovereignty. To
have democracy, we need both. The EU as such is not a democracy, but a
technocracy with severally contributed albeit never jointly
established national democratic inputs.

## Unaccountability and the gigantism of "more Europe"

This very structure gives rise to an intellectual elite. It comprises
the diplomats and apparatchiks who understand the inner workings of
the Union and who operate behind its closed doors. The policies they
set in place function as quasi-permanent directives that no national
government can effectively deviate from and, by extension, no singular
sovereign people may contest in full.

The elites occupy specific milieux, such as Brussels' European
quarter. Because of the social bubbles they live in, they make for
natural allies of big interests, in what effectively is expressed as
an ideology of gigantism. This is summed up in the motto "more
Europe". Whatever challenge we are facing, "more Europe" is the
solution, which is about another transfer of power from the national
level to the hands of the Europeanists.

The rationale of gigantism is that bigger is always better. By
handling things directly at the European level, the thinking goes, we
avoid all the inefficiencies of having many governments do what they
are there for, namely, to decide on behalf of their people. National
will formation is thus construed in functional terms as a matter of
impracticality, decoupled from its normative components of legitimacy
and accountability.

Gigantism has its own all-consuming logic, once thought through in
full. For whatever we may now think of as a national particularity
such as, say, schooling, we can always come up with a Europeanist
interpretation of it by which the European centre is the
template-maker and enforcer of uniformity, all in the name of
"efficiency".

This is, indeed, anticipated in the ancient myth of Procrustes. The
man wants to eliminate the "inefficiency" of diversity among people.
If we are all different, then one size cannot fit all. This applies to
the clothes we wear, but is figuratively true about the activities we
do, the preferences we have, and so on. But if, Procrustes thinks,
everybody can be made the exact same size then we are working towards
"efficiency" as then one size will fit all. The myth has it that
Procrustes places each person on a bed, which serves as the frame of
reference for the target size. Those who are smaller than the bed are
stretched to fit it. And those who are bigger are mutilated accordingly.

The myth of Procrustes is an ancient insight into a perennial mistake
of the governors who think that they can control everything centrally.
It also is a story of cruelty and abuse, as that is what happens when
you dismiss normativity in pursuit of "efficiency".

## The jingoism in Eastern Europe and the militarist rhetoric

The European Union is an underachiever on the global stage relative to
its economic weight. It cannot, for example, control a small country
like Israel, to make it do what it purports to believe (because what
they claim is not what they actually want, given that actions speak
louder than "strong condemnations"). Similarly, it has no leverage in
the Middle East more broadly, so it is subject to the energy shocks
coming out of Iran and the Arab world, as well as the capricious power
plays of the American administration, owning to their strong
dependency on US energy.

Muslim sovereignty is emerging stronger than ever. Iran has
demonstrated that it can hold its own against the Western empire. The
Mecca agreement reflects the culmination of a longer process towards
self-governance of the region by the countries that compromise it. Not
only are the Americans losing their grip on power, but the Westerners
in general are adapting to the rapidly developing multipolar world.

What the EU has been trying to do in recent years is to find a niche
by positioning itself as a counterweight to Russia and then to China.
Towards the latter, it implements all sorts of impediments to trade.
As for the former, it has since Euromaidan been cultivating Russophobia
which culminates in lucrative contracts for the makers of weapons.

The jingoism in Eastern Europe draws from the historical memory of
nations bordering Russia, though uses them as a pretext to give a new
power impulse to the otherwise underperforming European industry.
Industrialists know they cannot compete in market terms, so they
decide via the aforementioned gigantism to set up trade barriers and
then to shift business to the war machine.

Ursula von der Leyen and Kaja Kalas, among others, are gung-ho about
the war in Eastern Europe, claiming that Ukraine is winning. This is
the narrative that justifies the continued transfer of treasure to the
coffers of the industrialists. Money is nominally sent to Ukraine when
in reality it is cycled back into European industry. The argument is
that "helping Ukraine" is how we let it win decisively the war it is
supposed to be winning already. How exactly a country is winning when
it is losing territory and is now set for an extremely harsh winter is
the kind of knowingly false talk that future historian will have to
reconcile with the facts.

What matters is that the EU is fully committed to the grift of
militarism which is, once again, a short-term measure to cover its
structural weaknesses.

## Preparing the far-right transformation

Due to its already turbocharged militarist movements, the gradual
transformation of EU gigantism from nominally liberal to effectively
far-right is all but inevitable.

Parties like the AfD are purportedly pro-Russian. This I consider a
tactical trick, akin to that of Georgia Meloni's, or even the
Molotov-Ribbentrop pact for an older example. The moment they are in a
position of power they will understand that their interests are those
of the industrialists and financiers which, in turn, run counter to
the agenda of the Russian elite as well as the interests of other
rising regional powers.

In light of the reemergence of Muslim sovereignty as well as Russian,
Chinese, and Indian strength, the European gigantists, now invigorated
by the far-right to drop their performative tolerance, will seek to
redraw the map.

In phase one, they will continue antagonising Russia and China,
ramping up the pressure however they can. In phase two, they shall
collaborate closely with Israel in the Middle East, as it is the
protectorate of the Western empire in the region, to provide a
counter-balance to the alliance of Muslim nations.

The historical disputes between the Greeks and the Turks on the Aegean
and the Eastern Mediterranean will be exploited in an attempt to (i)
extend the already developing influence of Israel in Cyprus and the
Balkans and (ii) draw a wedge between Northern Africa and the Middle
East, effectively bringing the Mediterranean under tighter Western
control.

Their goal is to change the momentum that is building up in the Middle
East and the Muslim world more broadly, ranging from Morocco to South
Asia. Currently the Mecca agreement covers Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and
Turkey, but there is no principal reason why it does not incorporate
other countries such as Egypt and maybe even Iran, given the requisite
political realignments. The trend is in that direction.

This is all happening because the European Union started out as a
half-done political project. It tried to introduce political union
through the back door, by instituting a purposefully incomplete
monetary union (the euro) to then create the instability that would
make "more Europe" a necessary step towards immediate relief. It now
continues as what effectively is a colonialist gambit.

The dynamic is the same. The EU is playing the militarist game to
ultimately shift even more power to the centre. A small group gains a
lot in the process, but there is no public good involved. In turn,
national sovereignty will be reduced to a shadow play of elections
that change nothing, of governments that have no power, of citizens
who are brainwashed to believe that they actually control the
apparatus that determines their collective life.

Western interests are currently using the Eastern and Southern
European periphery as colonies that provide a buffer zone as well as
the potential of becoming battering rams. The far-right in the Western
countries will only intensify this dynamic.

Anyone who paid attention to the whole PIGS fiasco of the 2010s
(Portugal, Italy, Greece, and Spain, plus the obvious Nazi-style
dehumanisation) knows that if even the so-called moderates were
outright racist against Southern Europeans you have to be naive to
think that the far-right will be any better. Additionally, you have to
be clueless about how ruthless big interests are.

They will happily bring pain to the Greeks, for example, by involving
them in some conflict on the side of Israel if that serves their
Middle Eastern ambitions. Not because they care about the Greek
national interest or, indeed, the Israeli people, but simply due to
how they can service their goals by throwing nations to the fire.

The EU has the potential to become a monster of totalitarianism, all
while paying lip service to the vaunted "European values". This is
what happens when disconcerted action becomes the norm: moral decay
follows.

The elements are there and the far-right transition is going to become
the organising principle that puts everything in place. If there is no
staunch resistance against "more Europe" from the national
governments, of which there are no signs right now, then the EU is
destined to become a beast of epic proportions and thus to usher anew
into the mainstream the brutality that characterised Western power
politics in centuries past. "More Europe" will simply develop
far-right characteristics and those who today pretend to worry about
this turn will be its infamous apologists when the time comes.
