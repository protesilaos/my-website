In light of Gloria Steinem's death, Jenny Brown writes in _Jacobin_
how [Gloria Steinem Was the Godmother of Neoliberal Feminism](https://jacobin.com/2026/09/steinem-feminism-ms-cia-socialism):

> The establishment’s favorite “radical” feminist, Steinem worked with
> the CIA to promote reactionary propaganda and helped direct the
> women’s liberation movement into a corporate-friendly,
> anti-socialist direction.

Jenny tries to emphasise the shortcomings of neoliberalism by
commenting on its feminist manifestation. She juxtaposes neoliberal
feminism with the supposedly benign form of socialist feminism. The
notion of a socialist feminism sounds appealing until we think it
through, at which point we uncover the familiar individualism that
undermines society.

## Collectivism or individualism

The essence of socialism is its collectivist ethos: the group is above
the individual. This does not mean that individuals do not matter or
that they have no rights, but only that the common good takes
precedence over the individual interest in cases where the two are in
conflict. This is the basis for arguing that, say, a billionaire
should not profit at the expense of society.

The consistent collectivist cannot subscribe to a view of the world
where the individual has inalienable rights. Such jurisprudence
necessarily reifies the singular person as the supreme good and, by
extension, does not recognise any social body as an agent of political
will.

To the collectivist, all individual rights can, in principle, be
forfeit, including one's life, if the common good demands as much.
This is because the individual is ephemeral, whereas societies---and,
by extension, cultures---subsist through intergenerational solidarity.

There will be cases where this tension is necessarily oppressive from
the perspective of the individual, should the individual be pulling
towards a certain extreme away from the midpoint. Such are the demands
for the society qua organism to survive long-term: it has to maintain
its integrity, which is a function of the roles it enforces among its
members, the rules it embeds, and the hierarchies it establishes.

An organism cannot be heteroclite. Indeed, _organism_ essentially is
the spirit that is discerned as the _organising_ principle which sets
together the otherwise disparate parts into a system. "System"
literally means "jointly standing". There is a superordinate goal of
resisting entropy for as long as possible to which everything else is
subordinate.

A society, then, forms an organism through the organising principles
it generates. The national interest, the want for energy and resources
more broadly, the cultural expression... These reveal the overarching
objectives which no longer account for the feelings or wants of
individuals. Each person is subject to the workings of the whole,
whether they like it or not.

The individualist does not recognise any body that is superior to the
individual. In its most honest expression, individualism fully rejects
collectivist thought, for it does not treat anything as a public good.
There is no group whose interests are above those of the individual.

The individualist will be in principal agreement with notions of
inalienable rights, such as human rights or a very strong conception
of property rights as the foundational codex of collocation.
Consequently, the billionaire is not a problem in principle: that is
just shrewd entrepreneurship.

A country of individualists inevitably develops into an association of
marauders and pillagers, who will seek to exploit other lands to make
up for the fact that they are living beyond their means. They do so
because they put private interest above anything else and, as such,
think only of short-term gain.

The group, then, is an aggregation of selfish opportunists. It cannot
be allowed to have a life of its own, the way an ethnic community
does, as that gives rise to the collectivist ethos. The individualist
must actively sever ties and break roots to remain individualistic.

## The common good starts with the extended family

To the collectivist, the common good is understood as concentric
circles that extend from the family, to the local community, to the
homeland, to the nation.

The collectivist will argue, for example, that someone may not cut
down the oak tree that lies within their property, since cutting down
trees will be harmful to the community long-term. Monoculture begets
disease and endangers the ecosystem, for instance, while the absence
of trees and other vegetation makes soil erosion more likely and
floods more devastating.

In this example, the individualist will cut down the trees
indiscriminately because the only relevant person and thus the supreme
good is that of oneself. Their own short-term interest reigns supreme.
The individualist cannot live in harmony with a tree's lifespan, which
often goes on for centuries. "Whatever can happen, must happen now,
and may the others be damned", is their rationale.

The common good starts with the extended family in present time as
well as intergenerationally. The elders, for example, will plant trees
whose shade they will not sit under. They do it for their
grandchildren as well as for all the children that may live in that
land. Their plans concern the long-term, which is no longer about
their individuality. They are interested in the conditions that may
favour the next several generations.

We may describe this longer-term outlook as "patriarchal" or
"matriarchal" or, simply, "responsible". Within the confines of a
single extended family, the patriarch and matriarch are the masculine
and feminine tutelary figures who together uphold and pass on the
cultural norms. They are examples to be followed. Through their
leadership, which happens in a spirit of love, they give life beyond
their own by creating the environment for social reproduction:
reproduction in biological terms within the framework of a culturally
defined society, else ethnicity.

There is no patriarchy that is opposed to matriarchy. The two
complement and invigorate each other for there to be ethnic
continuity. What the man does, the woman cannot emulate, and what the
woman does the man is incapable of doing. Both are necessary for the
collectivist ethos to be understood through one's sense of duty to the
commons. The man is a good man when he tends to his family instead of,
say, gambling away his money. Same for the woman whose attention is on
her offspring instead of various kinds of plastic.

The common good starts by sacrificing the individual interest. One's
own convenience has to be set aside for the collective to prosper. The
parent must for a while accept fewer hours of uninterrupted sleep, for
example, so that the baby may survive. The child, in turn, must learn
that not every capricious want of theirs is satisfied because those
can be self-destructive once realised. Children conform with the norms
of the society and are not allowed to do only as they wish.

Whatever good, then, may be experienced at the local level is made
possible by the good of the wider space in which locality is
understood. There has been a past for there to be a present and this
cycle continues into the future through responsibility. The
collectivist thinks about the homeland and the nation at-large, not
merely their parochial gain.

The individualist is not conditioned to serve any common good. They
will continue to pollute the environment, for example, because
fundamentally they believe that whatever happens to the planet
long-term is not their problem to deal with.

Such is the midpoint of our modern world, especially in Western
culture. Its values are individualistic, its real god or moral
lodestar is the ego, whence come all the patterns of behaviour that
are detrimental to longer-term wellness.

There cannot be a socialist feminist in earnest. There will come a
time when they have to decide whether they are socialist or
feminist, else collectivist or individualist.

## To the feminist, family is hell

Which brings me to the lie that is socialist feminism. Consider the
following statement from the 1972 publication [Socialist Feminism: A
Strategy for the Women's Movement](http://www.historyisaweapon.org/defcon1/chisocfem.html).
Emphasis is mine.

> Many analyses have identified various institutions (e.g., the family
> or sexual relations) as the crucial contradiction of sexism.
> However, these contradictions reflect the social relations of a
> sexist society, or institutions in which sexism occurs. Eliminating
> these "prime factors" would neither eliminate sexism nor necessarily
> create supportive alternatives for women. As the factory may be the
> locus for capitalist exploitation, it is not the basis of that
> exploitation. Private ownership and profit is the basis, giving rise
> to the class relations. Similarly, **the family is a crucial locus
> of sexist oppression** but it is not the basis of that exploitation.
> **Control by men over women and the relegation of women to secondary
> roles** is the basis of sexism, giving rise to a sexist society.

This is the irreducible storyline of the feminist narratives.
According to it, men are oppressive qua men, men effectively enslave
women, and the immediate domain of their tyranny is the household
where family life happens. This family life is dismissed rhetorically
as "secondary roles". It follows that the emancipation of the woman
comes at the expense of the household, familial ties, and manhood as
such.

By extension, the most free woman is the one who is not under the
control of any man and recognises no family. If the woman has
children, even if she is a single parent, she still has duty towards
them, so children will become a problem as well that needs to be
externalised for the mother, now the individual who matters above all,
to be free.

What will the woman do once she gets rid of the "secondary roles" of
the household? She will pursue the presumably primary role of the girl
boss, or socialist agitator, or, if she is so inclined, will find an
inadequate outlet for her maternal instinct in the form of some
ideology that seeks to rescue the forlorn; an ideology that will
sooner or later be her own enemy, though her bleeding heart will not
let her recognise the danger ahead of time.

Feminism is insipid poison for society. It is the ideology that seeks
to render shameful and eventually eliminate the single skill women are
peerless at: motherhood. It works to make the woman an enemy of the
man, which will not only destroy intimacy or reduce it to a ruthless
transactional exchange but also tear asunder the social fabric. Once
the ground is saturated with enough toxicity, there will be no family,
no communities, no nation, no hierarchy whatsoever.

Feminism effectively argues that the problem for women is womanhood,
which consists in the fact that they are endowed with the disposition
to raise children. Feminism treats womanhood as the core evil and
encourages the woman to effectively become a lesser man in pursuit of
some stupid "primary roles".

The feminist only thinks of children as a burden to be managed through
deferral to some impersonal "childcare", which supposedly relieves the
woman of her maternal duties although it actually encourages her not
to bother with children at all. Continuing from the same publication:

> A second problem we faced was in our understanding of our oppression
> as women. We knew that childcare was an issue for many women, but
> failed to take into consideration the problems such women face. The
> very women we hoped to involve (those with young children) were
> among the least likely to ever be active in any kind of social
> movement. They simply don't have the time (because they don't have
> childcare), are less mobile, and don't think of themselves as active
> community members. The prevailing notion that women need something
> to do after their children are in school also makes these women less
> likely to consider becoming involved.

Later we find other snippets, like:

> With material changes such as children, a mate, a home, one often
> becomes more circumspect because such a person must be able to
> provide for others (by law and social pressure).

So the women could not be activists, which presumably is the apogee of
this vaunted freedom of theirs, because they were performing the vital
function of motherhood. The publication goes on to argue for some
superficially socialist scheme by which funds are directed towards
childcare. Make no mistake though, the essence is not socialism, else
collectivist ethics, but to sever the mother from her children in
phase one, to make her detest motherhood in phase two, and to render
her a rootless individual in phase three. Socialism is just the
vehicle by which hardcore individualism proceeds.

The feminist enterprise dehumanises both men and women. To the woman,
it denies her of her nature by making her think of her potential of
motherhood as innately burdensome and of her femininity as proof of
the fact that she is not man enough. To the man, it treats him as
inherently malevolent, owning to his propensity to enslave women. The
man cannot escape his manhood to be seen as something more than a
penis with a penchant for tyranny. The composer, the poet, the
painter, the engineer, the philosopher, is still a man, after all.
Whatever intellectual qualities and sensitivities he is endowed with,
no matter how pro-social his integrity and discipline are, he still is
the primordial enemy. When it comes to manhood, the feminist tends to
interpret everything as a phallic symbol of power and control.

The feminist must have never heard about the mother-in-law, for
example, or never met one of the countless men who are wrapped around
their girlfriend's or wife's little finger. Only men do the
controlling and the abusing. They must have never heard about the
lengths men have gone to in order to be seen favourably by a woman. As
such, they have not bothered to understand the psychosynthesis of the
common man; the man who finds fulfilment only through female
validation and who lives broken by self-loathing while he is
single---not because there is anything wrong with him, but simply due
to how nature makes him seek out the female counterpart as the
ultimate end. The man who is strongly independent is a rarity in this
regard and effectively irrelevant to any discussion about numbers.

Yes, there are men who are tyrannical and abusive, just as there are
women who would rather destroy society than accept lifelong duty
towards others. But we cannot reduce everybody to a simplistic
"men/women are bad" kind of formulation.

The gist of the aforementioned is that "socialist feminism" is a
walking contradiction. Once we read past the generic socialist talking
points we find gendered individualism that teeters on sociopathy. In
present time, this gender talk also comes from the side of men, as in
the case of Andrew Tate and friends, that I have commented on recently
in [About rootlessness and broken relationships](https://protesilaos.com/politics/2026-08-21-rootlessness-broken-relationships/) (2026-08-21),
as well as to a lesser extent [On the war in Ukraine and social expectations of manhood](https://protesilaos.com/politics/2026-07-20-war-ukraine-social-expectations-manhood/) (2026-07-20).

## Family is the natural unit of solidarity

The collectivist ethos is about serving the common good. Put
differently, it is expressed as solidarity in the form of people
supporting each other.

The natural unit of solidarity is the family. Within the family exist
bonds of blood which are held together by mutual feelings of love. The
vitality of the family culminates in solidarity between generations:
the elders are in the same space as the children and all are enveloped
by a community of such extended families who know each other's
lineage. They have a defined homeland for their clan with a culture
that is preserved across the centuries. Put differently, they are not
rootless and will seek to grow roots even when forced to emigrate.
Solidarity is thus organic.

In the countryside of Cyprus where I live, there is a growing number
of people of Syrian Muslim background. They found refuge here from the
civil war of the 2010s that sent their country into disarray. There
were only a few of them back then. Now the women have five children
each and are young enough to have another five. They support each
other and live close together, from the little ones to the elderly.

These communities are the only ones which are vital in the Cypriot
hinterlands. By contrast, the locals are walking dead. Old people
whose posterity daydreams of the princely life that Hollywood promised
them away from the countryside; a posterity that has turned radically
individualistic and thus only finds excuses to not have children and
to not take on the mantle of social reproduction.

The Syrians are neither rich nor are they enjoying some legendary
"childcare" that was brought about by the socialist reconstitution of
the state. They thrive under the prevailing conditions and their
future is bright regardless. They simply understand that you have to
put the common good above the personal interest: you submit to the
authority of the collective.

Here, the Syrian man makes a basic salary by working in the
construction sector. He spends his day at the construction site. He
labours under the punishing Cypriot sun to provide for his loved ones,
which he returns to as soon as the workday is over. The woman stays at
home with the rest of the extended family, tending to matters of the
household and keeping everything together. She is the cornerstone of
the community. Her work is not "secondary" in any way. She is best
person to raise her children together with her relatives and, while
doing so, to instil in them a strong sense of honour so that they too
go on to do the same.

These people do not outsource the most important of duties to some
faceless "childcare", nor do they have faith in the abstraction of the
nanny state. They make things happen through the resilience of their
collective character. Their meagre income is enough to raise several
healthy children, all of whom have good manners and are eager to
provide a helping hand to their neighbours. By contrast, the zombie
nation of the Greek man and the Greek woman, and of the Western man
and woman in general, finds it too bothersome to even have a single
child and will complain to no end about it.

Muhammad was a supremely gifted leader because he understood the
essence of religion as a framework of rules for life on earth. Forget
about the angels: Christians have plenty of those too. Muhammad's
project aims, in practice, to have strong communities whose vitality
is intergenerational. Whereas the Christian world gradually turned
religion into a private escapist affair about one's relationship with
some effectively voiceless father in the sky. This has transmogrified
into the various brands of individualism that are ravaging our societies.

Muslims are going from strength to strength because they accept that
the collective is above the individual. They will lose their momentum
as soon as they forget what grants them vitality. Christianity, on the
other hand, is becoming the spirituality of the undead; people whose
last hope is that there exists a better afterlife, as this life they
have already forsaken.

The particularities of the Muslim quotidian paradigm need not be
emulated. The collectivist essence is what matters. I cannot cosplay
the Arab man. I can, however, seek to rekindle the essence that we had
only a few decades ago, the essence I was raised in, and which have
since been sacrificed to the altars of overabundance, of individual
profit at the expense of rapid ethnic disintegration.
