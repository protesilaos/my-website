## Patriarchy as the domain of the abusive man

A key term in feminist discourse is "patriarchy". It can mean all
sorts of things, as each thinker will fasten upon the construct their
own significations. At its core, it is the dominion of the man over
the woman.

As I also noted in my article about the lie of socialist feminism, the
argument basically is that the household is the woman's hell. There
she is ensnared by a man.

This man, we learn, is an abuser. He uses violence and mental pressure
to force the woman to do whatever he wants. In other words, the woman
is just an object; an object of pleasure as well as an instrument for
some work.

This abusive man, the story goes, is not allowed by the patriarchy,
which now represents all other men, to show emotion or to recognise
that he might not be as strong as he pretends to be. In other words,
each man is abuser and abused by himself in isolation and by all other
men in concert.

If we accept this narrative, then by definition every father out there
is a psychopath. We are dealing with monsters, my friend, fiends who
will enslave women and who have no emotion whatsoever. Those ghouls
will teach their boys to do the same, so the boys are also little
monsters. And so on.

Given this, I expect to walk into a random group of men and find
complete psychos. Are you telling me this is a representative sample
of ~50% of society? Is the surgeon who saved your life a psychopath?
Is the musician whose songs you enjoy a perennial abuser of all other
people? Do the men who hug and kiss their mother, who show love to
her, to their aunts, to their grandmothers, to their sisters and
cousins, are all those men actually hating their family? Is there no
man who just loves his girlfriend or wife?

I do not accept this premise, sorry. Yes, there are abusers and yes
those men are insecure. But there are secure men who have no need to
remind themselves how tough they are by picking on easy prey. The
secure man only wants a challenge greater than him, which is how he
contributes to civilisation. He thrives in difficulty. You know this
if you have done sport, for example, or rigorous thinking. The easy
mode is not interesting.

My position is that there is nothing inherently evil with manhood. If
there are individuals who are abusive, then we have a problem of
individuals, which we can manage collectively in the context of a
community---and we will definitely need other men who talk tough and
mean what they say, if we are to contain the bullies. Though we do not
have a problem with men as such, which is what feminists ultimately
claim.

## Patriarchy as a precondition for capitalism

Which brings us to the next part of what patriarchy does. This system
of abuse normalises exploitation at home, which then normalises
exploitation in general. Whence comes capitalism.

If this is the case, then we should expect to see less capitalism in
modern Western societies which have long now conformed with various
feminist demands. Sure, not the entire feminist programme but parts of
it. If patriarchy and capitalism are intimately linked, then reducing
the one must reduce the other. Is this happening?

What does it even mean for there to be "less capitalism"? If we define
it as exploitation, then all the single parent households, by virtue
of breaking free from the aforementioned men/psychos have cumulatively
led to less exploitation at home which must have translated into less
exploitation elsewhere. Did it? Are those single mothers better off
now?

There is, however, a more pressing concern. If patriarchy, which
basically is the household, is tied to capitalism, then we cannot have
households for as long as we are living under capitalism. Those will
always be patriarchal, else capitalist. It follows that to undo
capitalism we have to nudge all the women away from men. Once we do
that, capitalism will collapse, because the transmission mechanism
from household to the rest of social life will be interrupted.

I find that self-destructive because of the toxicity it will create in
society everywhere. In other words, it will not work.

But this transmission mechanism is problematic in a different way as
well. If men are inherently abusive and if we are here because there
have been generations of households through the millenia, it follows
that capitalism has been there all along. Capitalism now becomes this
"make your own story" kind of term, where you can use it to describe
manhood and any kind of conditions manhood creates in the world.
Capitalism becomes essentialist as inseparable from the man.

As such, the end of capitalism necessitates the end of man.

So we already learnt how feminists blame women for their womanhood and
now we have the other piece of the puzzle of them blaming men for
their manhood. The feminist must be neither of those, of course, while
the feminist thought is necessarily culminating in transhumanism at
stage one and posthumanism at stage two. All in the name of undoing
capitalism. And all this wrapped in socialist parlance. How convenient
that you do not see the trojan horse!

## The importance of hierachy

This is a point I made at length in my article about broken
relationships and rootlessness, where I also commented against the
modern antipode of feminism which is masculinism (or the "masculinity
merchants" as I have described them before). It is a general point
about a human's behaviour.

[ Also read an unrelated, non-political entry of mine [on managing curiosity](https://protesilaos.com/commentary/2026-09-05-how-to-manage-curiosity/),
  which I also published yesterday together with the piece about the
  lie of socialist feminism. ]

Every person needs structure in their life. Otherwise their potential
is wasted. They fall into self-harming habits, lose sense of their
commitments, and so on. Structure means that you are reminded of your
duties and your goals.

A disciplined person brings structure from within. "I shall do X
because I said I will do X and my word is sacrosanct". This is how
discipline is in practice. There are degrees. The most disciplined
person operates this way in every aspect of their life. They can
afford to live without much feedback because of how they already
behave within constraints. Everybody else is better off having people
around to give them guidance. But, fundamentally, every person needs
structure.

When you are studying, your teacher will point out your errors and
guide you to the correct method. If the teacher does not do that, you
will embed the errors as second nature, which makes it much harder to
change them later. As soon as you recognise the teacher's superiority
of skill, you are consenting to the emergence of a hierarchy. In its
simplest form it is you and the teacher, but there can be many levels
to it.

For every aspect of life there are such hierarchies. Families have
hierarchies as well. Grandparents, parents, uncles and aunts, elder
siblings. Depending on the specific community, there is the neighbour,
the sage, the police officer, the doctor... All those people
contribute to some form of structure around the individual.

The generic term is "hierarchy". If we are talking specifically about
a male figure because for whatever reason their masculinity is
relevant then we say "patriarchy" and if we are talking about the
female tutelary figure we have "matriarchy". There is nothing wrong
with those words. In every society we find patriarchs and matriarchs
because every society has structure throughout.

Now, feminism poisons the debate by making patriarchy a necessary
evil. It follows that a certain aspect of the social structure will
have to be dismantled. So either feminism will replace patriarchy with
some other hierarchy, such as matriarchy or the rule of the feminist,
or it will abolish structure altogether. The latter means that every
single person will be left to their own devices to make decisions they
are not prepared for. Imagine the child having to navigate the world
without any feedback from its parents or other tutelary figures. That
will be disastrous. And if it is some other hierarchy, then the
feminist has to tell us why that is superior to what is in place, to
what has stood the test of time? Is there no value in a father figure?
Does the man who has discipline and integrity not serve as a role
model for other people, including other men but also women? Do women
not admire a skillful man in whatever his area of expertise is, such
as a guitarist?

I think that hierarchy cannot be removed. What matters is to have
harmony. It is neither patriarchy nor matriarchy because it is both of
them complementing each other for different aspects of life. Sometimes
you have to give people a warm hug, while at others you have to call
out their nonsense. If all you have is warm hugs or judgement, then
you are doing them a disservice: you need both.

## Yes, some feminists are problematic but read this one instead

Once we go into the details, we end up with a common rhetorical
practice of positing a more true version of the subject matter which
has the potential to radically shift the debate. In this specific
case, we have to contend with decadent feminisms while there exists a
virtuous one. The _Jacobin_ piece I was commenting on was also
unfolding along those lines in juxtaposing neoliberal versus socialist
feminisms, with the latter being the virtuous one.

I have two principal objections here.

1. It posits a true feminism. It then needs to explain why are all the
   non-true feminists not switching to the authentic version. Are they
   traitors? Is this a psy-op of the CIA or some other shadowy forces?
   Whom shall we trust and how should we go about telling truth from
   falsehood? What means do we have to confirm that our choice of true
   feminist is the most true? Do we run each opinion piece through
   some "true feminist checker" program? Do we monitor discussions
   between people to confirm they are in line with the truth? This
   sounds silly, but I am serious: will you police all discourse?
2. If we accept that there is a more true feminism, we are going down
   the slippery slope of fundamentalism. There can always be something
   more true and more authentic than it. The history of leftism is
   full of sectarianism. The radicals will not be radical enough. This
   pits one group of people against another in a competition of
   self-righteousness. The "self" here will have to be a person in the
   end, because the generic category of "women" is the contested group
   already and also because of point 1 above. As such, fundamentalism
   leads us once again, willy-nilly, to individualism.

My commentary is about the common in the multitude. I am not trying to
find truth in the words of one person or some authoritative source. I
am examining the trends and am telling you where they are going.

The truest feminist is counterfactual. We can run the thought
experiment to indulge our curiosity, but we are no longer describing
phenomena.

My commentary on feminism concerns the irreducible part of it, which
is the victim narrative of a group of people, namely women, being
exploited by another group of people, namely men, on a binary of good
versus evil. There are layers of complexity built on top, but this
core idea persists. In my original article I explained why this leads
to individualism in how it undoes the family. I also made several
clarifications here.

The socialist cause under feminism switches from a class issue to a
gender issue and from a gender issue of women as a whole to a "me, me,
me" contest of individualism writ large.

## The programmatic agenda of socialist feminism is socialist

I was told that I did not fully get the point of the 1972 publication I was
quoting from [Socialist Feminism: A Strategy for the Women's Movement](http://www.historyisaweapon.org/defcon1/chisocfem.html).
What they want is not like what other feminists want, because they
have the socialist agenda to address problems within the household.
They are socialist throughout.

This I contest because I think through the implications of their
position. Their argument is basically this:

- There is a core problem at home.
- The problem is not solved at the individual level.
- We need collective solutions for it (i.e. socialism).

Then they elaborate on what those may be. We can even think of many
ourselves (of which we have plenty across the European Union, for
instance) in one way of another, such as public healthcare, strong
networks for childcare, longer maternity/paternity leave, and more.
Those would be in line with the socialist programme.

Now what happens if we do not have those? Absent the socialist state's
pampers, the "socialist+feminist" construct becomes just "feminist".
Which means that the household is a problem. And if the household is a
problem that cannot in the meantime be solved collectively, then it is
addressed individually. Hence the individualism I have already covered
at length.

This is exactly why I bring in the case of the Syrian refugees (they
live close to where I am, by the way, and I see them every day).
Materially they are in the same situation as me, namely, marginalised.
But they have a different mindset than our society at-large, which
allows them to endure the challenges and thrive regardless.

I recognise that the individualist/atomic-family kind of childcare is
a burden on parents. I comment more about that in the other article I
linked to which argues against Andrew Tate, among others: [About rootlessness and broken relationships](https://protesilaos.com/politics/2026-08-21-rootlessness-broken-relationships/) (2026-08-21),

What the Syrian woman has, in short, is the vaunted "socialist
childcare" at the local level: her extended family. She does not have
to wait for the reconstitution of society.

Consequently, we as a society have a problem of expectations, which I
also comment on in another thread: [Comments on material conditions, expectations, and community](https://protesilaos.com/politics/2026-09-06-comments-material-conditions-expectations-community/) (2026-09-06).

My view about communities can be summed up as this: "I can have
socialism at the local level together with my extended family and
neighbours". It has solidarity, it has resilience, it works for the
Syrian refugees, it has worked for millennia.
