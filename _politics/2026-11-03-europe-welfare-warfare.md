Europe is experiencing a cost-of-living crisis. Everything is
expensive and there is not enough money even for the basics. More and
more people are coming to the realisation that our society has become
a grand pay-to-win scheme in which the many are becoming increasingly
marginalised as they are getting priced out of housing and sociability
altogether. Meanwhile, European policy-makers keep furnishing billions
for their proxy war with Russia. Ukrainians do the fighting so that
Western elites may profit.

It is, nonetheless, a mistake to think that the ruling class is
decoupled from the rest of society. All exist on a continuum of want,
else differences in degree, underpinned by shared values. Those are
made manifest as expectations of what amounts to the desirable life.
The average European wants the comforts of the city, amusements
aplenty, just-in-time consumption of anything their heart desires,
plenty of vacation, travels around the world, and a robust welfare
state. The question now is "what does it take to finance that sort of
lifestyle?" and the answer is not pretty.

## The continuum of trade and conquest

International trade and imperialism differ only in means. Both have
the same end, which is to collect resources from different parts of
the planet and make possible goods that are otherwise not available in
conditions of autarky (self-sufficiency). The piecing together of
resources is what makes people more affluent.

Consider a simple scenario where Europeans make trade with the
Chinese. Both sides are benevolent and eager to help each other
thrive. And both work towards their own enrichment. The routes between
Europe and China are inhabited by other peoples who will eventually
want a piece of that delicious pie. This they will get either through
piracy or by trying to get involved in the trade agreements. Raiding
and negotiating are themselves on a continuum: you attack so that you
can sit at the table and you sit at the table because you can attack.
But them getting involved means that the share of wealth eventually
diminishes, relative to how it was before.

A saturated market has much tighter margins for profit. Eventually the
original actors will be dismayed. They will have the memory of the
good old days when money was plentiful and will seek to change things.
This they can do by redefining the rules of the agreement with the
consent of those involved, or by imposing their will through brute
force. Again, the same continuum of negotiation and aggression is in
effect. Consent means concessions though, which result in less profit.

What then tilts the balance between peaceful cooperation and violent
confrontation is the cost-benefit calculation.

The same dynamics exist within each region. The European who is not
involved in business sees the entrepreneur next door live the opulent
life and now wants some of it as well. Thus arises the social demand
for spreading the wealth around. Again, this diminishes the surplus
per person.

Against this backdrop, there is the trade-off between giving and
taking. It applies to the interior and between states. Giving means
that surpluses are eventually distributed so widely until there is
nothing more to give. While taking involves increasing levels of
aggression, in the form of social stratification and the subjugation
of other peoples. At any rate, there is no distinction between what is
happening in the notional inside and in the outside: it is the same
propensity for giving or taking; the same peremptory rules of life.

To be more concrete now. The international trade we had following the
collapse of the Soviet Union coincided with the supremacy of American
power. The so-called "international community" was what America wanted
it to be. Economic and political "freedom" was the flip-side of
"security" with strong American military presence practically
everywhere around the globe. To speak of international trade is to
describe the American empire, and vice versa.

This is not a new phenomenon. All political arrangements have this
dual nature. Pax Romana, for example, was a time of prosperity which
was underpinned by the iron fist of the Roman war machine. The golden
ages of European nations after the Middle Ages coincided with their
rise as colonial powers. Even the most abstract market depends on the
consistent application of norms, tacit or explicit, which are
necessarily underpinned by the potential for corrective action.

## The latent violence of safety

Which brings me to the potential for violence. Every order is
constituted against disorder, every safe space is carved out of the
wilderness, every given plan consists as a diminution of complete
openendedness.

Think of the common symbol of love, which is the mother holding her
baby. Love is made possible by all that is non-love, such as ruthless
opposition to all that represents a threat to the baby's wellness. The
mother keeps the baby protected from, say, poisonous spiders, rats,
vipers, creepy strangers, and so on. Failure to do so results in the
near-certain death of the child. Her love towards the baby is made
possible by her willingness to actively avoid or, if necessary,
displace and otherwise control all those other life forms. Not because
she harbours some inherent hatred, but as a matter of showing love.
Such is the drawing of boundaries, which is supported by a
determination to stand and fight where the dividing line is.

The same principle is true for a house. The interior is comfortable
because it leaves out the wilderness. If there are bats, for example,
they carry with them the disease of rabies: a bat resting on your face
while you are asleep will transmit a horrible and incurable virus to
you through saliva. So bats must be kept outside. Again, not because
one actively loathes them, but as a necessary condition for survival.
Extend this idea to the grass, the vegetation all around, the tiniest
of species, which also bring all sorts of challenges and threats.

Those who romanticise nature have not lived outside their comfortable
milieu: nature destroys anyone who is not paying attention to their
surroundings.

Same for the society, which rests on institutions. Members of the
social whole agree on shared values either outright or through
acculturation, which they may codify as law. The common good they
share is made possible by the delineation of the space within which it
can be made manifest. The realm of inclusivity necessarily stands as
exclusivity to whatever cannot be a party to it. If, for example, the
society institutes theft as undesirable, then it must contain,
constrain, dissuade, deter, or otherwise eliminate thieves. Else the
thieves will undo that which the society considers its midpoint,
namely, the order where theft does not exist.

Concretely, this is backed by force and the attendant readiness to
employ it. Your favourite cafeteria is not overrun by beasts because
the built-up area it exists in forces all other life forms away, from
the grass, to the insects, the snakes, the jackals, and so on. Force
is ultimately expressed as the potential for violence, which is
typically actualised when the easier pathways to the given objective
are exhausted.

At this point, naive people will respond with emotionally charged
commentary: "oh, so you believe violence is good, you monster!" No,
silly. Whether this is "good" or "bad" is irrelevant. Mine is a
description, not a prescription. I explain how the world is. Else we
live in the fantasy land of ideology from where we cannot enact
thoroughgoing reform.

Bringing the realities of living into the discussion of international
affairs, we appreciate how trade and war are on a continuum of latent
violence. They both share the goal of piecing together resources from
different parts, such that they expand the pool of wealth. We choose
to trade because the alternative is to kill each other. And,
sometimes, that alternative is more appealing, hence warfare. War may
be destructive over the short-to-medium term, but it still has the
potential to be greatly profitable long-term, such as in the case of
colonisation.

## A crisis of attitude

The cost-of-living crisis in Europe is fundamentally a question of the
average character. Europeans do not know what they actually want. They
lack the maturity to set priorities and the wherewithal to deal with
the concomitant trade-offs.

Europeans want to remain self-righteous about their democracy and
fundamental rights, call out the Russians, antagonise the Chinese,
control the Arabs and the Africans, make fun of the Americans, and
still enjoy the opulent life.

Consider the cost of energy. Lowering it will make everything else
more affordable. Though there still are great costs involved. Europe
is strongly dependent on imports from America right now, which means
that it needs to diversify.

- Europe could be importing cheap oil from Russia, but that would bring
  billions of fresh cash into the coffers of Putin's war machine,
  which he would definitely use to wipe Ukraine and maybe even the
  Baltic nations off the map. Here is already one trade-off.
- Europeans want cheap renewable energy, but this means that they must
  collaborate with the Chinese who have all the expertise and relevant
  resources for panels, batteries, inverters, et cetera. The Chinese
  will ask for concessions, such as to eliminate all tariffs, provide
  unencumbered access to ports, and so on. Importing cheap goods from
  China effectively forces domestic businesses into bankruptcy. Again,
  this has far-reaching implications.
- Another option is to approach the Arabs and the Iranians. Europeans
  can get energy from them in exchange for something they want. For
  example, Europeans will have to accept the legitimacy of the Iranian
  regime, they will have to withdraw all military presence from the
  Middle East, stop arming Israel, and so on. As always, there are
  consequences that have to be considered very carefully. What, for
  example, prevents China or Russia from filling in the vacuum left
  behind by Westerners? "Nothing" is the answer.

There are other neighbouring considerations which pertain to matters
of lifestyle. The average person does not think about the energy
profile of their appliances. They do not know how demanding and
energy-intensive it is to have heating, a hair-drier, and a toaster
all working at once, while the washing machine is running, the
computer is active, and all the lights are switched on. This is the
typical household. None of it is "cheap" anymore: it can be made
cheaper through shrewd and perhaps unscrupulous international
agreements and/or outright pillaging of other people's resources.

Renewables are not a panacea either. They cannot address the
insatiable desire for energy that an energivorous lifestyle demands.
Put differently, people must understand that their own consumption
habits have a cumulative effect on the world they live in and the
political realities they enable. Put differently, it is far more
revealing to check how people vote with their wallets than to listen
to what they signal as a virtue.

The easiest way to understand the crisis of attitude in Europe is to
shift the discussion away from ideological purity into matters of
governance. There is where things get real. Ask somebody what exactly
will they do first day at the office. They have the keys to Europe and
they can act as they see fit. You call up Putin? He is like "okay, you
want energy but what do I get in return?". Russia will make its
demands very clear. Whatever concessions you make do not merely have
an expression in present time: they also create the conditions for
future bargaining. Every decision has ramifications. For example, if
Russia controls the Black Sea as well as the Baltics, it can get a
better deal out of its trade with Germany and France. Similarly, the
Franco-Germans being in charge of that space gives them more leverage.

Extend this principle to every transaction and instance of trade,
always being mindful of the aforementioned continuum. If you are
honest, you will always reach the same trade-offs; trade-offs that the
average citizen has not bothered to think through and thus blithely
purports to be righteous.

Fundamentally, the Europeans do not understand how entitled they are.
They fail to realise that the good life they used to have was built on
the legacy of colonialism. If the average citizen still wants to have
all the comforts they are effectively advocating for a new
imperialism, even if they have not connected the dots themselves.

Europeans, and Canadians for that matter, have long now been shielded
from the realities of the bonanza they were partaking in. It was
principally the Americans who were doing the dirty work, but in a
world that has shifted towards multipolarity all the Western nations
are faced with the reality of how to remain relevant.

"Relevance" in this world is a polite way of describing the efforts
that culminate in dominance. Europeans will know this the moment they
take a cursory view of their history as well as a cool look at the
rigours of governance. Though the political correctness of recent
times has distracted them from the facts. Hence their crisis.
