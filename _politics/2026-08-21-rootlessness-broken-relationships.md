Reading the leftist _Jacobin_ magazine article with the title [Toward Simp Liberation?](https://jacobin.com/2026/08/tate-simps-incels-male-competition-misogyny)
led me to the remarkable investigative journalism piece from _The Cut_
by Ezra Marcus titled [Revenge of the Simps](https://www.thecut.com/article/cam-girls-webcam-pimp.html).
Its subtitle is _how a community of porn addicts tried to rescue the
teenage camgirls they worshipped from the clutches of a violent webcam
pimp_.

This crime story is a microcosm of the modern Western world. It has
everything from getting rich quick, putting money over people, selling
an exaggerated version of oneself through social media personas,
producing explicit sexual content of oneself, being a ruthless
entrepreneur, understanding male worth as a function of personal
wealth and status, thinking of female worth as a matter of looks,
seeing women as objects of entertainment, propping up masculinity as a
facade for sociopathy, all against the backdrop of broken
relationships with their rootless individuals.

In this world, there is a level of analysis in which there are
perpetrators and victims. Though at another level all participants
share a common set of values. The men qua traffickers who measure
their worth by their business acumen are on a continuum with the women
who are perfectly fine, at least initially, with selling their body
for some easy money, where "modelling" is just an acceptable form of
porn. And they are all in principal agreement with the simps who
otherwise consume the pornographic material.

## From victims to perpetrarors

In both of the aforementioned links there are many references to
Andrew Tate, even though he is not involved in the criminal case at
hand. Andrew is the embodiment of a wider phenomenon as bully and
bullied himself: the victim of dysfunctional parenthood and hostile
urban living conditions who becomes the means through which abuse is
normalised, propagated, and valorised.

Andrew recycles age-old ideas of manhood while continuing the
capitalist practice of commodifying every facet of life. His message
internalises the social structures he was raised in and suffered from,
blending them with the materialist mindset of incessant grinding for
enrichment and upward social mobility at the expense of all intangible
values.

As such, Andrew necessarily communicates something that is truthful
about his society. Though he does it only as a way of private gain,
hence his masculinity merchant gimmick. Fundamentally, he is not
interested in institutional reform. Rather than abolish slavery, he is
content with being the master of slaves and a god in their eyes. To
this end, Andrew fulfils the destiny of the bullied to become a bully
as soon as the conditions are favourable.

It is, nonetheless, an analytical error to blame Andrew Tate for what
is happening in this case and others. Even if he himself is
contributing to phenomena, he is but an instance of wider trends.
Communities are broken not because of Andrew's meme-grade rhetoric but
due to the very structure of modern economic organisation.

## Cities breed distrust

The vast majority of people in industrialised countries live in big
cities as individuals or atomic families, where they are expected to
be away from home most of the day while they do some kind of work. At
their workplace they are all treated as expendable "labour force" or
"human capital" and are thrown under the bus whenever it is expedient
for their employer to do so. There is no mutual sense of togetherness.
Their everyday experience is thus one of distrust. This is the
baseline normality of the modern world.

The perennially jaded individual does not have the time or energy to
commit to family affairs, no matter how kind or conscientious they
are. Even in two parent households, children do not spend enough
quality time with family. More so in single parent families. The lack
of quality time with loved ones forces young people to seek connection
in imaginary friends, with whom they develop parasocial relationships.
Their role models are not actual, flawed individuals, whom they
experience day-to-day, but exaggerated figures of seemingly impeccable
quality on magazine pages, TV shows, and Internet sites.

The city is an inherently dangerous environment for the vulnerable. In
the common scenaria, this includes children, women, and the elderly.
Though gangs bearing weapons appear with increased regularity in many
countries, meaning that even the strongest man is ultimately just as
easy of a prey. No place is safe for kids to play care-free and for
parents to get a moment of rest, all while the elderly are effectively
rendered irrelevant to social reproduction by being left to wither
away in some facility among strangers. Neighbours come and go, as they
are typically renting the place they live in. So there is no sense of
trusting those around one's own place either. By extension, one does
not know who they are dealing with: everyone blends in with the
anonymous crowd and is a potential vector of attack.

Consequently, there always lurks some danger right around the corner,
which makes everybody more tense. Parents do not let their children go
out while they themselves suffer from chronic stress due to the
rigours of work, the disproportionate burden of parenthood placed on
the atomic family, and the pervasive sense of threat in their milieu.

Kids staying in closed spaces all day is an understandable protection
mechanism, though it still is detrimental for their longer-term
adjustment in society. They are not socialised properly, not with
friends their age and not with elders in their community. They are
trapped in a tiny physical space, which becomes a restrictive
mindscape that is an extension of their own fears and overthinking.
They are eventually get sucked into the fabricated world of
parasociability and concomitant distorted images, whether those
originate in legacy publications or online media, and seek happiness
in what effectively is some form of pernicious escapism. Furthermore,
they have unrealistic expectations about their own self and their
relationship with others, which has far-reaching implications for
every aspect of social life, including romantic experiences which are
pertinent to the criminal case presented in Ezra Marcus' journalism.

It is not only the vulnerable though. Cities are meat-grinders that
make no exceptions. Such is the pace of life they dictate, such is the
environment they necessarily create, that everyone eventually falters.

## Anonymity and the lack of honour

At its core, the big city is akin to the jungle, for it engenders a
default combative behaviour of the individual or tiny group against
the rest of the world. The city cannot instil a communitarian,
collectivist ethos because there are no such social bonds to be
experienced on a quotidian basis. Those bonds occur naturally in the
extended family, which incorporates the neighbourhood and the entire
village into a singular unit of shared trust and intergenerational
solidarity.

Cities do, of course, offer plenty of opportunity. This is the
byproduct of the anonymity they enable: you can essentially be whoever
you want and connect with those you like, which sounds liberating
until you realise that the same is true for everybody else who will
now seek to exploit you with impunity. Those who play fair get abused
by those who have no such restraint. Which brings us back to the
baseline of distrust.

Anonymity makes it so that it is impossible to have an honour-based
moral code. Only the law can dissuade people, though the law cannot be
enforced to the same effect as a local community singling out one of
its members. The law is less efficient because its locus of authority
is away from the fray of the action. To put it simply, the police
officer is not your relative or immediate neighbour most of the time.
The law, in this regard, can only be close to events when the
political order is totalitarian (which then creates conditions for
ceentralised abuse on a monumental scale).

The lack of honour is the environment in which the vicious moneyman
thrives and where the sociopath emerges as the master grifter. There
is no incorporation of accountability in one-to-one interactions,
which means that the most unscrupulous rise to the top. They are even
celebrated for their success in doing so, since this is the ordinary
operation of the system---the rules of the game, if you will.

## Systemic problems and the importance of hierarchy

For as long as we are seeing the degradation of relationships as a
"manosphere problem", an "Andrew Tate problem", or in other contexts a
"modern women problem", we are not being thorough in our investigation.
There are system-wide forces at play that need to be understood as
such and be addressed in a root-and-branch fashion.

In the _Jacobin_ piece I mentioned in the introduction, Evelina
Johansson Wilén and Meagan Day discuss events through the lens of
feminism. While both of them are insightful, I think feminism cannot
appreciate that itself is a product of this structurally
individualistic world. In the feminist worldview at-large, hierarchy
as such is what oppresses people in general and women in particular,
which inevitably leads to individualism even when the discussion is
along the lines of building social movements. Why? Because if your
extended family, which is dismissed a priori as "patriarchy", has no
power over you, then nobody should: you are a rootless person who only
understands the irresponsible logic of "my body; my rules" in all of
its manifestations.

Hierarchy, which in a local community that spans generations always
becomes patriarchy and matriarchy as one, is how an individual's
innate freedom is harnessed for the greater good. The harnessing is
key. In the absence of hierarchy, each person has to rely on their own
devices. Political correctness demands that we echo the notion that
all people are equally competent at controlling themselves, but
experience tells us that some individuals are fundamentally their own
worst enemy: give them too much freedom and they will ruin their life
together with that of others. The honest person who undestands their
propensity for self-harm admits that they are empowered when framed by
others. Structure is always---always---what people need: it either
comes from within, with unflinching discipline, or in community with
other people. The latter is the only reliable arrangement from the
perspective of social organisation.

Hierarchy is what holds individuals accountable. If this is not
enforced by the extended family and immediate community, then it must
be conferred as supreme authority to some nanny state or not done at
all, which is where we are nowadays. The social fabric is torn asunder
because at the micro level there is nobody to keep individuals in
check, even when those individuals are religious or, anyhow, have a
clear idea of what their society's concept of righteousness is.
America is a country of deeply religious people, for example, yet here
we are commenting on its rampant porn-defined substrate. The issue is
more basic, then, and has to do with the defective setup of
face-to-face accountability.

In a vacuum of hierarchy, there is no collective power apparatus in
place to guard against predators from a position of caring. The drug
lord has easy access to your son. The pimp is one social media post
away from your daughter. And so on. With the tutelary
figures---hierarchy!---gone, what remains is a war of all against all,
whose repulsive face we keep witnessing on a daily basis.

## A crisis of values

Moving to the text of Ezra Marcus' reportage, I find that the
following quote reveals something more than the details of a human
trafficking crime:

> Another young woman, Sarah, moved into the lake house a week after
> her 18th birthday. She’d found the job through a Snapchat ad, which
> had promised $8,000 a month for bikini photos. She was pressured by
> Muhammad to quickly sign a contract. After a month streaming naked
> on Chaturbate, working 11-hour shifts, she had earned only $1,000;
> Connor told her the shortfall was her fault. One day, a fan sent
> $32,000 directly to her PayPal. She didn’t tell her bosses, only her
> boyfriend, Kaiden Kingsley, a recruiter who lived in the house. But
> word got back to Tyukalo, who ordered Muhammad to take her phone.
> When she got it back the next morning, her bank account was drained
> and the contacts for her family and friends had been blocked.

What we read here is descriptive of modern society at-large. Consider
these general observations:

- Get rich quick? Check.
- Find a shortcut to the good life? Check.
- The good life is measured in material possessions and luxury? Check.
- An exploitative contract from your boss? Check.
- Blaming the employee for the lack of business profits? Check.
- No tutelary figure to hold teenagers accountable? Check.
- The girl boss who sells her body because those are her rules? Check.

What we read here is specific to organised crime, yes, and I do not
mean to trivialise it, though we have to learn from the particular in
order to appreciate the general. As soon as we abstract away the
details of this specific case we realise that what is happening, in
terms of values, is business as usual.

The polite society, the one which can no longer understand its
political correctness as the shadow play it actually is, will, of
course, condemn the crime. Though in reality its moral standing is
suspect for as long as it normalises all of the above in its routine
workings.

In this specific story, the simps end up helping the victims. Though
their capacity as simps is itself part of the structural problem. If
you are demanding pornography as a consumer, you are necessarily
contributing to the formation of the supply chain for pornography
through the incentives you reinforce. And with that comes rampant
cruelty.

In this twisted world, where porn is mainstream entertainment and
girls are encouraged to "liberate" themselves, your demand for
pornographic content means that you have no principled objection to
your child, sibling, relative, being on camera. And if you think that
you are okay when strangers do it but not your loved ones, then you
are echoing the individualist mindset with its attendant disregard for
solidarity. This is the crisis of values that brings us here.

Contra feminism as hinted at in the _Jacobin_ publication, I view
modernity's social woes as products of too little hierarchy. Andrew
Tate and friends make a mockery out of patriarchy and manhood in
general because they are not interested in the pro-social, responsible
project of building communities; a project that always happens with
women as equal contributors. They only care about maximising their
earnings at the expense of everyone else. As such, they leverage
parasociability, turbocharge the mindset of victimhood, amplify the
latent distrust in society, and encourage people to fight each other
as rootless individuals. Put differently they do business as usual
minus the politeness.
