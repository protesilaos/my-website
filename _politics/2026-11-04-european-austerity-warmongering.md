## From the Euro to the Eurocrisis

Imagine a simple world in the 1980s where West Germany and Greece are
in an economic union. In the beginning, each country has its own
currency. West Germany is an economic powerhouse that is driven by its
coal and steel industries, so its government can issue debt at very
favourable interest rates. By contrast, Greece does not have strong
economic fundamentals and its borrowing costs are higher as a result.

The Cold War ends and the Soviet Union collapses. What once was West
Germany can now unify with East Germany. But politicians around
Europe, are concerned that German reunification will make Germany far
too powerful for others to control. Policy-makers are nervous. They
cannot be in an economic union with Germany, given that the imbalance
in power will inevitably turn into a hegemonic relationship.

The solution is the creation of a new currency to replace national
currencies: the Euro. This new currency is a quid pro quo: "offer us
your strong currency and the attendant financial benefits, and we will
continue to do trade with you". The Euro was, in effect, a leash to
keep Germany under control and to boost the mutual confidence of
everyone involved that the Germans will not attempt to take over
Europe once they become too strong. If the trust is not there, the
economic union will not work in practice.

The Euro is introduced at the turn of the century. Germany and Greece
now have a single monetary policy, entrusted with the European Central
Bank in Frankfurt. The markets estimate that German and Greek
sovereign debts are basically equally valuable. So Greece starts
borrowing at very low rates. Money thus flows from German savings into
Greek credit-driven consumption.

The bonanza is great for German industry. Car manufacturers can sell
their products to the parvenu Greeks. The latter enjoy the affluent
life that cheap credit guarantees, which makes them strongly
pro-European and fully committed to the project. Germany is not only
larger now, it also has access to another market with temporarily high
purchasing power.

The German industrialists and bankers benefit greatly from this
expanded market. If the money had stayed in Germany in the form of
savings, those plutocrats would have difficulty accessing it and
getting a large share out of it. The money would have to go into
infrastructure projects and/or to increase worker salaries, which is
not immediately profitable for the rich. But because it got converted
into credit abroad through the capital flows in the Euro Area
periphery, this money could be recycled as a wave of pure profits
without changing realities on the ground in Germany.

In the meantime, Greek public and private debt rises while investment
spending in Germany remains weak. The average German worker does not
benefit from this arrangement: their wages are practically frozen and
their infrastructure is not getting any better. It is the ruling
elites who are having a good time with the Euro.

The indebtedness of the Greeks has to be understood as the
indebtedness of the Germans. The reason is that the Germans are denied
of what they could have done with that money and of the infrastructure
they could have had. Long-term, the lack of investment means that they
are worse-off than past generations.

The influx of cheap money in Greece creates strong inflationary
pressures. Everything triples in cost overnight in the conversion from
the Drachma to Euro. Investment funds are buying up properties. Mass
tourism makes all sorts of areas, products, and services inaccessible
to the ordinary person. In other words, the distribution of wealth is
uneven and life as a whole becomes an egregious pay-to-win scheme.

The 2008+ financial crisis hits. The Euro Area is a fairweather
construction that is not prepared for the coming storm. There is no
mechanism for fiscal transfers in Europe and, more importantly, there
is no democratic accountability+legitimacy at the EU level that would
give the power to an elected government to actually do what is good
for the entirety of the system.

The only real solutions to the Eurocrisis are the outright
federalisation of the Union or the withdrawal to national borders.
Instead, the EU remained a bureaucratic entity that retained all of
the original architectural flaws. Its solution was to codify austerity
into its core legal framework and, thus, to consign an entire
continent to atrophy. The European Central Bank pumped trillions of
inflationary money into the system to paper over the cracks. This went
into the pockets of those who are at the top of the so-called
"transmission mechanism", i.e. bankers and friends, who then put the
money into housing, among other rentier projects, further pricing out
of the market the average person. This is where we are now.

"Austerity" in this context effectively means that public spending
does not go towards welfare and infrastructure that would benefit the
majority of people. Governments keep their spending tight yet still
find billions in cash for projects that benefit the rich. More on that
below.

Back to the Eurocrisis days, the Greek government is now bankrupt
because the cheap credit is over and it has no means of supporting its
artificially enlarged economy. Everything was a bubble. The German
government is also in an extremely difficult situation. German banks
took the German savings and converted them into cheap credit to the
Greeks. That money is now gone, so the banks will go bankrupt and the
Germans will lose their savings. How do you tell the German people
that their plutocrats messed it up?

The German government thus has to give money to the Greek government
in order for the latter to pay the German banks. But this is hugely
problematic. If the German government was honest, it would have to
admit that the money that could have been spent on productive projects
within Germany was actually channelled into frivolous spending by
greedy bankers, with corrupt politicians in the mix. So the German
establishment has to spin a racist narrative of lazy Greeks who cannot
control their finances and must be saved from their own vices by the
righteous Germans.

The German government is now prepared to bail out the Greek government
in order to save the German banks. To do this right, it must force
austerity onto the Greek people, otherwise the German citizens will
feel they are getting robbed. Well, they did get robbed by their
ruling elite, but this way the problem appears to be only about the
"lazy Greeks": there are no systemic forces at play.

The German government was already imposing austerity through the
backdoor on the German people---remember that money was leaving the
country to be converted into cheap credit elsewhere. It simply
intensified those efforts during the financial crisis. Again, this is
where we are today.

In turn, the Greek government could tell its own people that "we all
had a piece of the pie" and so we were all responsible for our own
impoverishment. Austerity was thus the easy way out for both
establishments.

This is a politically expedient way to avoid the realities of the
situation:

- the Euro is a project that benefits the ruling class;
- austerity is hardcoded in the Economic and Monetary Union;
- the European Central Bank does not have a counterparty treasury;
- the European Union is not a democracy.

All the above continue to be the case.

What I have referred to here as "Germany" can be extended to a core of
European countries, such as France and the Benelux nations. "Greece"
is a proxy for Southern Europe and beyond.

The important part here is to understand how debt and credit actually
work against the peoples on both sides and that austerity applies to
both equally. The other important lesson is that racism is socialism
for dummies: the ruling elites are telling you how others are the
problem so that you punch down instead of looking up. This, too, is
what is happening right now with the rise of the far-right in Europe;
a far-right that will blithely align itself with the EU establishment,
even though it purports to be against the EU.

## Austerity leads to atrophy

Back to our simplified version of Euro Area dynamics. This politically
expedient solution has disastrous consequences for the economy. If the
average Greek and the average German do not have enough money to buy
cars, what will the industrialists do with their products? They could
export to China, but China has now become so effective at producing
its own high-end goods that there is no reason for the Chinese to buy
German brands.

In other words, German cars are no longer competitive. The
industrialists can boost their competitiveness by bringing down their
costs and by finding new markets. To bring down their costs, they have
to secure cheaper energy sources. Europe is especially weak on this
front. It can import energy from Russia or the Middle East, but is
fundamentally not in a strong bargaining position relative to those
countries.

Finding new markets without bringing down costs is not going to work
for global trade. The reason is that Chinese cars will be more
appealing to those same international markets.

Against this backdrop, we can understand the push for (i) the eastward
enlargement of the European Union, (ii) the EU becoming the de facto
political arm of NATO in all of Europe, and (iii) the centrality of
the Ukraine project.

To put it simply, German industry needs the eastward expansion to
boost its competitiveness. By controlling the countries to its east,
Germany has powerful leverage in its deals with Russia. The same
principle is true for relations with the Middle East. Now you know why
the establishment supports Israel: it puts pressure on the countries
in that part of the world to do business in terms that are favourable
to the West. The average Israeli person is a pawn the way Greeks and
Germans are. Nothing new here.

Both Ukraine and Israel perform the same function. To be a battering
ram in their region and to work as the bridge that connects
energy/trade routes to the European centre.

Austerity discourages productive investments. Industrialists and their
friends in finance have the two-fold motivation to (i) convert their
money into financial assets with which to extract rent instead of
building productive capacity and (ii) gear their production towards
something that governments would be willing to buy, given citizens
cannot afford much. For the former, money goes into funds that, inter
alia, buy houses and appartment blocks at scale, hence the ongoing
accommodation crisis which is a problem of concentrated wealth. And
for the latter, they want war to create the necessity for the
production of relevant hardware as well as to further improve their
bargaining position with regard to energy/trade routes.

War makes perfect business sense for those vested interests. In the
short-term, they get to sell arms. In the long-term, they can benefit
from potentially advantageous geopolitical realignments. All the
billions that are sent to Ukraine are recycled back into those same
business interests. In effect, the European taxpayer is bailing out
the European oligarchs.

## Who controls Ukraine controls Central Asia

This is between the Europeans and the Russians. Reading this heading
will make you think that I do not know geography. That is because you
are not paying attention to the maps. Ukraine connects to the Asian
heartlands through the Eurasian steppe. This land corridor is vital.
It provides direct or relatively easy access to the Black Sea, the
Caspian Sea, and the Far East, with further connections to be had from
there.

If the EU/NATO controls Ukraine, it can make a stronger case to the
Caucasus nations to also join the EU/NATO architecture. From there,
the West can reach all the way to Iran via Azerbaijan, also putting
pressure on Turkey, to remind the Turks not to stray away from Western
interests. The Azeri and the Israeli establishments are best buddies,
by the way, so factor that in as well when you broaden your view of
how the fate of East Europe is inextricably bound up together with the
Middle East.

Now add the Uyghur element as well to understand why the West, which
is highly suspicious of Muslim and Chinese people somehow adores the
Chinese Muslims. Uyghur lands neighbour the Central Asian nation
states. The West having a foothold there can create all sorts of
problems for the rest of China and beyond.

Access to the Caspian Sea connects the EU with Kazakhstan,
Turkmenistan, and others. The pressure on Russia is thus immense and
China will start to feel the heat as well. Should the Russians lose
ground in Ukraine they run the risk of withdrawing further north with
a long-term possibility of losing large parts of their own country.

Behind the scenes, you can be sure that Western spies will be all over
Chechnya and other republics to reignite and/or outright arm
secessionist/pro-Western movements. Once you understand the logic of
energy and trade routes with regard to competitiveness and business
interests, you know that it cannot stop in Ukraine. Only fools think
that Ukraine is about "democracy" and other such fancies.

Now you know why Russia did not respond with force to the eastward EU
enlargement towards the Baltics but it drew a red line on Ukraine and
Crimea. Estonia and friends being part of the EU does not
fundamentally change the realities for Russia. But Ukraine is
different because of the aforementioned ramifications.

None of this started in the 2020s or even the 2010s. It goes back
decades. The unification of Germany, the collapse of the Soviet Union,
and the enlargement of EU/NATO eastward have to be understood in the
context of these trends.

While all these are unfolding, there is another set of incentives that
grows stronger in the so-called "Brussels bubble". People who work for
the EU institutions, think tanks, and related, all have a personal
interest to keep this business going. A stronger EU translates into
better career opportunities for them. Money is plentiful, job security
is guaranteed, prestige is a given.

These are the elements that easily corrupt a person's heart. Combine
it with the elitist mentality of top-tier education and you get the
picture. They can feel like demigods, they operate within their own
little world with other such people, and eventually they lose touch
with the prevailing conditions of everyday life.

I am not describing politicians here, though the incentives are the
same for them too. Think of all the assistants, functionaries,
advisers, and lobbyists. I am writing about all the people whose names
you do not know yet whose cumulative impact on policy is massive.

Those people are decoupled from the consequences of their actions,
which is why they are not trustworthy. If there is war in Ukraine,
they are not the ones involved in the fighting. If there is inflation
that forces all of us home all day, because we cannot afford anything
beyond the basics, they do not experience hardship. Fundamentally,
they form an apparatus that has a life of its own. We are not a factor
in their thinking beside, of course, virtue signalling about "the
people".

The drive for "more Europe" nowadays means more efforts being put into
the war machine and into the institutional connections that will
forever ask for more war, exactly how it is in the United States with
the military-industrial-financial complex.

This "more Europe" with a militarist spin is the new bonanza for the

intensifying.
