In these "interpretations" I typically pick a Greek song, translate
its lyrics, and then do the philosophy I would have done anyway. Put
differently, I am using music as a starting point for a comment that
might turn into a discussion... A failing tactic, for sure, but I
digress.

The reason I pick Greek songs is to introduce this type of music to an
international audience. Though you should not assume that I have a
vested interest in it. I care about art, which knows no borders.

So here are 10 songs covering a variety of styles that I enjoy. I keep
it to 10 because I do not have enough lives to appreciate in full
everything people do. Sorted alphabetically:

The gist of all this is that each person is multifaceted. Sometimes I
do not want to listen to music at all. I enjoy the birds and the
ambience of the mountains. At others I am keen on something more
contemplative and at others still I will put on a dance song. As a
matter of fact, while I am typing this out, _Coco Jambo_ by _Mr.
President_ is playing from my archive, which is one helluva blast from
the past!

In embracing our multifacetedness we free ourselves from the
absurdities of ideological purity. Take any benign idea---love,
beauty, peace---to an extreme and it becomes so inwardly corrupted it
turns into its opposite.

In the expression of multifacetedness there is common sense. You allow
each facet of yourself to thrive, because you are taking care to water
all the plants. As such, you are creating an internal polyculture,
which is resilient to disease. Diversity in harmony produces balance
of the sort that is sustainable.

Balance can also be enforced through uniformity,, adherence to dogma,
and brute force. This is the singlefacetedness of monoculture that
delivers short-term results and longer-term malaise. In terms of
everyday behaviour it runs counter to our nature at times, even when
it is done in the name of it, and thus ends up being pretentious in
substance and performative in style.

The human condition is diverse throughout. The individual is, in this
regard, both mirror and amplifier of group dynamics, of social trends,
and historical trajectories. By exposing ourselves to others, we allow
our own self to flourish and to become more rounded.

How can you know, for example, if you actually like dancing if you
have never done it in earnest? Maybe what you are missing is exposure
to that person and/or situation that will tease out whatever remains
hidden deep inside of you.

Intellectually, I am a philosopher. Though a more generic term is
explorer. I will go anywhere the wind sails because (i) I am keen on
learning as much as I can, (ii) I recognise I am not in complete
control of the world and so accept my fate, and (iii) I have the
courage to deal with whatever danger awaits.

The danger with the explorer's conduct and with diversity more broadly
is that it can become wasteful of its own vitality if it is stretched
too thin. Concretely, if I explore too much without integrating any
findings into my life, then I reach a point where I am pulled in all
directions, unable to actually do anything from start to finish.

Similarly, diversity for the sake of diversity does not produce any
good results. Polyculture at its extreme is so competitive for each
plant that there can be no one or few species that yield enough fruit.

It again comes down to balance and common sense, to avoiding the
absurdities of the extremes.
