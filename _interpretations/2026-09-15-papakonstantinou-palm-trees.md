For the present article, I have picked a seemingly bizarre song from
the vast corpus of Vasilis Papakonstantinou: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wv28XE7tGJo>.

_Palm trees_ is written and composed by Apostolos Boulasikis. All of
the songs I have heard from Apostolos are surreal in the images they
evoke and profound in the interpretations they withstand. This one is
no exception.

Below are the lyrics, my faithful translation of them, and some
philosophy.

The titular palm trees symbolise the end of a journey. They represent
the exotic life one finds away from their homeland or the realm of the
knowable more broadly. "Exotic" in its root as "exo" (έξω), which is
outside, thus strictly foreign, unknown yet conceivable as unknown,
otherworldly.
 
The song speaks of flight and makes an exhortation to that end. To
leave one's place, in the hope of finding the altogether different
world that lies beyond their current reach. As such, _Palm trees_
invites one to embark on an open-ended adventure in pursuit of an
elusive goal.

This is not necessarily a boat trip to the other side of the ocean.
One can perform such a journey while physically staying where they
are. What matters is that they open their mind to fresh ideas and new
possibilities. Practically speaking, though, one needs to abandon
their immediate locale to undergo such a transformation. The reason is
that people eventually stick to what they used to do because it grants
some benefits within the given society.

Someone may be, for example, the cool edgy kid in high school. They
gain a reputation to that end, which itself carries expectations.
Others now see and want edginess. This person then has the incentive
to continue to be edgy, to effectively play the role, rather than,
say, pause and become more contemplative.

Travel will help break this loop. The person will have the freedom to
become another as their preoccupations are no longer tied to the
expectation of specific patterns and outcomes. Modern societies
understand this and is why children switch schools after each level
and eventually go to university, should they choose to. At each level
there is a chance of rediscovering oneself anew, even if only marginally.

A journey in pursuit of the exotic is neverending, because there can
be no discovery of the exotic as exotic. Once one enters the other
world, they already are conditioned accordingly. They have become
another in the process of (i) escaping from the confines of their
comfort zone and then in (ii) reaching that world. That which once was
distant has become proximate.

Such travels have no terminus. The exotic is not a specific entity,
but the notional other of one's lifeworld. If one moves from inside to
a specific outside, then that becomes their new inside as the realm of
the familiar and the proximate, which shall have its own outside, and
so on.

As people, we change over time to take on certain qualities and leave
others. What once was a defining feature now is not characteristic of
who we are. There may still be traces, but more pronounced patterns
have since taken form.

It thus does not matter where exactly one finds those palm trees, i.e.
who they become exactly. The point is to develop a certain consistency
such that they can be that person in earnest and feel they are
performing their function well. This is described at length in the
lyrics through the references to all the figures one can be. Each leaf
of the palm tree points to a persona.

To what end? There is none. The journey is not better than staying
exactly in place. Those who leave as well as those who stay still meet
the same fate. It then comes down to what feels right or what the song
describes as the path luminated by the palm trees: one can only follow
what they discern.

For instance, I am the journeyman type who leaves home in pursuit of
discovering myself by losing the self I was, as merchant on the silk
road, pirate at the Horn of Africa, sellsword in one of the many
European wars, all as the same person. I thrive in openendedness. This
is what makes me a philosopher and a polymath, after all. It is
exuberance that comes with the requisite courage to embark on the
adventure, to not be deterred by the challenge, and to not be held
back by any promise of comfort either.
