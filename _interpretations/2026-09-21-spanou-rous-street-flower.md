title: "Interpretation of “Street flower” by Maria Spanou or Giorgos Rous"
excerpt: "Translation of---and philosophical commentary on---a Greek song whose translated title is 'Street flower'."
---

For today's entry in the series I have picked _Street flower_. It is a
love song written and originally performed by Giorgos Rous (Γιώργος
Ρους). It was then remixed by Maria Spanou (Μαρία Σπανού), who gave it
a slower pace and a stronger sense of brokenheartedness. Here is the
version that Maria performs: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ztYW6hKNecs>.

And here is the original, which is more upbeat, performed by Giorgos
Rous himself: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TFSc7x91EWU>. Perhaps
it has that superficiality of exuberance because men tend to express
their grief through heightened energy. This is how they can be
destructive because they are not in control of their emotions. Though
when that pain is channelled into creativity, when the man does not
give in to the lust for mayhem, it provides the power impulse for
cultural realisation and the clarity that is necessary for greatness.

I prefer Maria's version because her emotional touch, which
communicates sadness, is closer in spirit to the lyrics. Though I
recognise that she works with the outstanding contribution of Giorgos.
This is the beauty of art, after all, but also the diversity of
humankind at-large: nobody owns inspiration, nobody owns expression.

Below are the lyrics, my translation of them, and further comments on
what I make of this song.

The song starts with the image of notes that are soaked in tears. This
makes it a description of what likely was a specific experience of the
songmaker or a generalisation that captures the truth of many such
moments. The poetic "I" has put to paper thoughts that were
troublesome and emotionally charged. In so doing they have released
the pain. They have broken free from the obsessive side of infatuation.

This process is painful in itself because it constitutes a rapture.
One part of the self is severed and effectively left to wither away,
which is what the titular street flower may represent: the innocence
of affection that is uprooted, cast aside, and thus consigned to a
swift death.

In the first stanza we learn how the poetic first person thinks that
those words that were written in tears were not about them. Though
this is admittedly a coping mechanism. An innocuous lie to distract
from the truth that those words were specifically about their author.

Creativity is an effective way to deal with challenges. It takes an
emotion, such as grief, extracts from it its energy to use it as a
resource and nullifies the parts that can be harmful. This is true for
every propensity that could cause damage, such as anger and
frustration. Though it also extends to what people consider positive
feelings like admiration and affection.

There are no purely positive or purely negative feelings. Each is both
positive and negative. What matters is the degree to which it is
expressed and the conditions in which it is made manifest. Anger can
be liberating when it engenders the violence that destroys a
corrupting influence. Love can be degenerative when it leads to the
kind of unconditional support that welcomes the wolf into the flock
or, more commonly, when it raises a child to become a spoiled and
entitled piece of shit.

The creative mind essentially is the body's harness. It functions as a
transformer of emotions into deeds with a longer-term outlook, which
in their finest form are magna opera. It is how, for example, pain
stops being painful while remaining there as pain. These are the
mechanics at play in the backdrop of the _Street flower_.

When faced with a blank canvas, those who create in earnest will see
their self reflected in their art. Write a journal and hold nothing
back: you will not be able to hide anymore. Speak through colours,
melodies, dance motions and you will learn to be truthful. This is the
artist's world. It is a feedback loop between their inner state and
the works they have put out there. It culminates in the discovery of
the self through successive reinventions of the self.

By contrast, the technically minded person is not trained to be honest
about their own condition. They are a sharp tool that is perfected for
an external application. Internally though they have a lot to learn.
To put it schematically, they know how to do a PhD but not how to
live. Their mental state is governed by hesitation, perhaps fear, in
actually figuring out who they are. In order to not enter the
liminality of this world, the space where one learns to be content
with who they are, they fall back to being pedantic. They will search
for technicalities and definitions, which are assurances of false
certitude, just so they do not proceed.

The most creative mind, then, is the one that is both technical in its
application and expressive in its constitution. It is the mind that
creates entire world of inexhaustible meaning.

Back to the lyrics of _Street flower_, it speaks of creating the
make-belief that love does not hurt, but also of pretending that the
poetic "I" does not actually fancy the poetic "you". The song suggests
that this is a fool's errand. Denying the reality of the feelings does
not take them away. It is more reliable to admit what has transpired
and treat all the thoughts for what they are. Thus the person may move
on to the next stage of their life.

Moving on is not easy because it is akin to homelessness. There is
radical uncertainty of what comes next and where to go. It is unknown
whether another love will be found and it also is unclear whether that
will last or be yet one more injurious experience. We do not have the
luxury to remain indecisive and to stand still because time does not
wait for us.

Turning the page and then proceeding through action is a form of
creativity as well. It accepts the indeterminacy of the situation, the
absence of closure, and sets the conditions for something new to take
form. This is how there even is a street flower to begin with: it can
thrive on a tiny patch of soil that accumulated at the edge of the
asphalt. Such is its resilience and want for expression.
