This is an entry from my journal. I write about current events to
ultimately make comments about duty and morality.

* * *

I am getting more involved in various communities. While I enjoy it, I
keeping pushing against the urge to disengage. Here are all my thoughts.

I attended another ethnic Greek event the other day. We do those quite
frequently and I hope we increase the regularity to at least a weekly
cadence. My involvement in this community is already noticeable. I am
participating in the rituals by reading hymns out loud---and I do
speak very loudly, probably the loudest. I will eventually start doing
3-5 minute lectures before the main event on various topics about
practicalities of living with a connection to the Greek tradition. I
have the clarity of thinking, am a good speaker, and know what I am
talking about.

This time we had plenty of people in attendance, from teenagers to
elderly folk. Perhaps the cooler weather is a factor: we are not
competing with the sunny beaches for attention. Though even with just
two people I am already content. What matters is that we remain true
to what we do.

I like that there are priests and priestesses sharing the work. This
is consistent with the ethos of recognising the equality between the
sexes in light of their inescapable differences, as symbolised in the
6+6 of the 12 Olympians. Women here are expected to assume leadership
roles and tend to the commons even in a formal capacity: a priest,
male or female, is an essential tutelary figure as the linchpin of the
community.

Apart from the locals, many of whom I did not know, we welcomed
delegations from other parts of the world, including Italy and
Argentina. They read out translations of our hymns in Italian and
Spanish. I am inspired to practice my Spanish, which is "okay", and
also to learn Italian, which I already find intelligible. But not
right now because I am still improving my French. Portuguese is
another language I am interested in, due to my past collaboration with
people from Portugal whom I really liked.

For me, knowing a language means that I can philosophise in it. My
standard is high. Though even the basics are good enough to go places
and meet people. There might come a day when I travel to Italy,
Argentina, and beyond, to do philosophy or just to contribute however
I can. This is practically impossible for the foreseeable future, as
the costs of even the typical trip are beyond my budget, but stranger
things have happened.

Beside the ethnic Greeks, my involvement in other communities
continues apace. The other day I joined a panel for EmacsConf 2026 to
talk about our connections as people in relation to software. To me,
making software free from the familiar copyright restrictions is an
expression of sociability. It is like saying "I care about others,
here is what I have discovered, you might find it useful, and you are
welcome to use it and share it with whatever modifications you wish to
make to it".

With free software, who the original author is becomes secondary in
importance over time, since the programs we share are ultimately a
vehicle for creating the commons that we all benefit from. My
computing is made possible because others offered their work under
conditions of freedom. I do the same in return. We thus have all the
resources we need to help each other. Our essence, that which is
irreducible, is our shared belief in software freedom. That is the
only view we all agree on. It may be the starting point for agreements
in other areas as well, though this is our starting point and a
possible reason to get to meet each other in person. Nothing more.

Communities develop around a common good. If that is absent, whatever
groupings will dissolve fairly easily, as is the case with fads or
cults of personality. "Wind gatherings; wind scatterings" is an
expression we have in Greek, which effectively says "easy come; easy
go". Communities stand the test of time because the common good is
greater than even their finest members. There is some initial draw
there that attracts people even as the specifics of their involvement
may change.

Communities that do get corrupted and end up disintegrating are those
which lose sight of what made them interesting in the first place. In
a sense, they forgo their essence while chasing short-term gains.
Again, wind gatherings, wind scatterings. Their opportunism is their
downfall. Their hubris is that they allow a vocal minority or
ephemeral promise of convenience to distract them from their original
mission. Like, for example, a free software community that now wants
to position itself as an activist group on every political cause
beside software freedom: that will only alienate the veterans and,
more importantly, bring in newcomers for the wrong reasons, while also
demonstrating to those involved how woefully unprepared they are for
pursuing their new lofty ambitions. People think they can affect
political change until they actually try it. Once you put things to
the test, they are not simple anymore. In the meantime, what remains
of the community is an empty shell.

To me, the fact that I am involved with the ethnic Greeks and Emacs is
not a coincidence. Both are niche. This serves as a pre-filter. I know
that a person who is passionate about Emacs, for example, is very
likely to have interests and a personality that overlap with mine. We
are diverse, though we do understand each other at some level. Whereas
joining the most popular trend and mainstream religion for that matter
makes apparent just how different I am from them. I know because I
tried it more than once.

Beside those engagements, I am scheduled to talk to a group of people
about geopolitics and matters of strategy. I am not the typical nerd
nor your average spiritualist because I am a keen political operator.
I understand people very well and I know how things actually
work---no, it is not pretty and your ideology does not make it
prettier, but such is the human condition. I will talk to them about
Cyprus, Greece, Europe, and the Middle East. It saddens me that the
European Union is a bureaucratic apparatus that is stifling our
nations and is now gearing up to become a war machine in its twisted
logic for ever-closer union at all costs. We could have had a European
democracy but we got a proto-empire instead.

At any rate, I do not know who those people are whom I will be talking
to. I am acquainted with one of them and expect to have a small
audience of young and older citizens alike. I always talk without any
prior notes. I like to stare into peoples eyes. I impose myself in the
room and command attention. This obviously is a show of dominance
though it also is an expression of sociability. I am close to them,
sitting or standing beside them, in the same situation they are in,
without obstacles in-between or intermediaries. Fundamentally,
fortitude and sociability of this sort are two sides of the same coin:
I make myself available because, at heart, I am not afraid of them.
Similarly, I can help them exactly because I have the power to do so.
Weakness is never helpful, except in people's ideological fantasyland.

I always have this feeling that I am a misfit. My defining feature is
best described as in-between-ness. I do not fully identify with any
group or activity. For example, I was not into computers until my
mid-20s and suddenly I became an Emacs developer who even contributes
to core Emacs from time to time. I am not into programmer culture yet
somehow find myself talking with programmers about all sorts of
issues. I do videos even though I barely watch YouTube and do not even
check my own recordings. Those who post comments sometimes ask me to
include timestamps, as if I care about engagement numbers. I am
uploading videos while not doing what is meant to be done for optimal
results. I am there without being there.

I do not have academic credentials beside a Bachelor's degree and yet
there are so many PhDs, professors, and principal investigators who
like to talk to me. Some have been there for years at this point. I
have long now suspended my disbelief. But there is much more. If you
pay attention to my writings and sayings, you will find that I have
nothing in common with your typical "religious person" yet I am an
active member of a religious group. I was working at the European
Parliament without ever wanting to be involved in politics: all the
people there were members of some political party or organisation,
whereas I did not belong anywhere and still do not. I cannot think of
a single group where I would be confident in saying "look at them, I
am basically the same". Not one.

When I will talk to the lads about geopolitics, I will again do it in
my personal capacity. This too is my in-between-ness in action. There
are analysts who make a career out of this subject and here I am, an
outsider, some country bumpkin, doing my own little thing again. I
have no pedigree. I do not speak on behalf of some ideology. I do not
advance any particular cause. There is no movement I consider myself a
part of. I am not into committees and networks and interpersonal
connections in pursuit of some position of authority. I am myself and
say exactly what I want regardless of the audience.

The good thing is that I am not feeling the peer pressure to fall in
line and am thus better prepared to say exactly what I think is the
case. The bad thing is that this is the initial condition of a
despotic person. If I am not listening to anybody now that I am
irrelevant, then why would I do it after I have gained power? This is
a scary prospect. I am too potent a tool to fall into the wrong hands.
If I become corrupt, that will be horrible for others. At least I am
aware of it and will hopefully always have the restraint which
characterises me, such that I am despotic in a pro-social way, yet
despotic nonetheless.

Is this not how it is with every capable life form though? The best
guard dog has the sheer strength and ferocity to fulfil its role. It
is exactly that same set of skills that is devastating in the wrong
context. The safety of a nation depends on weapons and borders which,
again, can be abused to tyrannise people. The naive answer is to not
have power, to mutilate yourself, to break the hand that can offer
goods because that same hand can deliver a swift death, and to pretend
you are morally superior even though you have also eliminated your
capacity for good.

There is no morality in fear and in the resulting enfeeblement. I
shall do the right thing exactly because I have mastery over the power
to do the wrong thing. What makes it right is my sense of duty, which
is another way of describing benevolence. Such is the in-between-ness
of the human condition, as experienced through awe, for example: the
combination of admiration and of fear. The world is awesome due to how
incredible it is and it is awesome due to how powerless we are in the
face of its vicissitudes.

Where fear talks the loudest people do stupid things. The potential
for harm is yet another challenge to be overcome, one more force to be
redirected. Such is the tragedy of life: we cannot have the good
things without accepting the bad things. One is latent in the other.
This computer uses electricity. It is the same energy that can burn me
alive yet it is here harnessed in a way that augments my capabilities.
Those who think they can have it differently have not put their ideas
to the test.

Back to my communitarian outlook, how do I even connect with people?
What do they find in me? There is nothing remarkable about my
surface-level features. In terms of appearance, I am not good-looking,
at least not in a mainstream way of broad appeal. In terms of
clothing, I only have plain clothes and generic shoes. Even my haircut
is cheap as I do it myself and you can notice the imperfections from
up close. I actually cut myself with the razor yesterday. Nothing
serious: it just shows how clumsy and unprofessional this is. My
social standing is even more unimpressive: if I were to describe
myself in those terms you would have a hard time telling the
difference between my personal account and the urban dictionary entry
for "loser". I have nothing that the average person respects in a
stranger. But here I am doing one little thing at a time and somehow
influencing others around me.

I am close to 40 years old (38) though I feel I have not even started.
People peak at different ages. Some are done after high school. They
spend the rest of their life reminiscing about the past while regaling
others with stories of the good old days. Others burst into the scene
in their 60s and everyone is like "where did that come from?". I must
be a late bloomer or, if that is not true given my output for decades
now, then I am evergreen and the most worrisome part is that I might
be becoming ever-greener. My creativity is high, my attention is
undivided, and I am as physically fit as ever.

I write "worrisome" because each deed creates far-reaching
consequences. There is a world where I am a leading figure of some
international religious community. There is another world in which I
am a politician with no-one to hold me back. There are many such
possible worlds and they all demand that I abandon my current life. Do
I even want to go anywhere else? I like it here. It is quiet and
simple. Lonely, too, though in-between-ness gives me a full dose of
that everywhere I go.

In each of those worlds I can be doing something remarkable whose
impact will be felt even after I am long gone. One way of looking at
it is that there are great adventures to be had. How can a sailor at
heart ever turn down the sirens? Potent though they are, I always feel
the urge to disengage and withdraw into my caves. For example, I had
no plan to submit a talk for EmacsConf this year. I joined because
they asked me to. I did not want to ignore them. Leave me alone and
you will learn that I drift into a parallel dimension: I focus on my
projects, do not contact anyone in the meantime, only to realise that
many years have flown by.

This want to step back is not mere hesitation but the baseline of my
behaviour. Anyone who reads my writings and understands how things
work already understands as much. I write consistently at length and
in depth. Writing is a solitary activity. Even when I am not writing,
I am thinking about something which I will eventually type out. This
means that I spend much of my time in my head. I am doing it right
now. There goes another day. I could have gone to town. I could have
talked to people. I could have done so many other activities. All I
have is this...

I explore ideas that I find interesting and, in the process, will
venture as far or dig as deep as I need to. I put my ideas to the test
in order to figure out which among my findings is viable. I discard
the rest. My lifestyle is defined thus: it is austere. It may also be
sad and miserable, though I am too used to it to notice.

I think it selfish to quit. Yes, the introvert within would be happy.
Introverts expend energy when they are involved with others. Imagine
not having to do that all the time, oh how sweet! The nerd inside is
also keen on quitting. Not having all those commitments would give him
the free time to become fully absorbed in whatever subject without
concern for the boring details of this milieu. These voices are like
children. They have no sense of responsibility. I understand there is
a reality all around me that is responsive to my deeds and, above all,
I recognise that I have talents which can be beneficial to others as
well.

Each person has something to offer. Those with an inclination to sing,
for example, will inspire all of us to sing along. Those who are
attentive to detail will become engineers, doctors, etc., and will
give us something that changes our life. Those who are carers will
raise children, support the elderly, create/maintain good conditions
for sociability within any given community, and thus empower others.
Even the smallest contribution is important because absent those there
will be no larger contributions either or they will not have as
significant an effect.

What exactly do I offer? I lack the talents of the artist, I do not
have the detail-oriented mind of an engineer, I am not doing science,
I am not a care-giver, I do not organise people for sport or any other
event, I am not involving anyone in my affairs, I do not have a
family, there is no neighbourhood or village that really needs me.
What I essentially do well is scribble thoughts; thoughts that I have
thought through carefully. I share those without knowing how they can
be of use and to whom they might be relevant.

Even where I live is a reflection of who I am. I did not plan it this
way though the world has a strange way of becoming. It is rugged and
unwelcoming here. I live outside even the closest built-up area. It
may sound fun to not have any neighbours until you actually do it. Now
you are in a remote place with nobody around to support you. In
theory, you are extremely vulnerable. This is especially noticeable at
night: you move from a well-lit place, which is the closest village,
and begin to descend into a steep valley. There is a line of sight,
extending from the artificial light, beyond which everything is dark.
The road reaches a cliff before it takes a steep turn. As you move
down the valley, darkness envelops you. You cannot see anything. What
sort of beast dares to live in such a place?

Mine is an unusual set of skills in a balanced combination: practical
thinking, the capacity for abstract reasoning, decisive action,
competitiveness, pro-social outlook, and emotional stability. I can go
as far and as deep as I want because, fundamentally, I am convinced
that I will always win in the end and that even in defeat I will make
the contest so challenging that my loss will no longer matter.

It is this latent ferocity that concerns me. Though it is exactly this
untamed force that morality is designed to harness. It is what
empowers me to give to others gifts galore; to give non-stop. To what
end? I have no full knowledge. I am just wagging my tail and will play
along with whomever is not afraid of wolves in their element.

This makes me a simple-minded creature, indeed.
