title: "The beauty of the Middle East"
excerpt: "An entry from my journal in which I comment how the Middle East is also a metaphor for admixture."

This is an excerpt from my journal.

* * *

By "beauty", I am not referring to my Apollonian looks and disarming
modesty. No full body naked selfie for you today. Sorry!

The Middle East is a place where cultures meet. Milieux which can be
understood as distinct may also be seen as blending into each other.

In Cyprus where I live, we officially are a European Union member
state. If you look at the map, you will notice that the island is
closer to Lebanon than it is to the next EU country's mainland. Yet
our political orientation is Western in its outlook and Westernising
in its function.

Part of this ideology is the historical trajectory of Cyprus as a
colony of many Western empires. Though at the level of narrative the
major source is the thought of the Enlightenment Age. It branded
Hellenic culture as Western, while also reducing the vastness of Greek
thought into a rationalist project, which was en vogue back then.

The more I reason about Greek culture, the more I realise and
integrate its holistic worldview. This is obvious already at the
artistic level, with the intuition of polytheism. Many gods represent
the many facets of our experience, as well the many aspects of the
world which are, in principle, not necessarily known to us. Yet all
gods partake of a singular godhood, which suggests that the many are
the one, the one is the many, the way a single ray of light can also
be seen as a spectrum of colour.

Any given person illustrates said multifacetedness. You are reasonable
yet also have an emotional side. Your mind grasps the transcendent
while your body confines you to the here-and-now of everyday
necessities. You represent admixture. However constituted, you emerge
as the interplay of many factors, of processes that are notionally
internal to the system you are, as well as stimuli that occur
exogenously.

You look around as your attention discerns patterns. Everything looks
different. I am not like you and you are not like a tree and this tree
is unlike that other tree. There is individuation throughout. Though
as you continue to observe and to study attentively, you stop seeing
individuality emerging from the disparate data points. You are instead
finding the similarity of things. A bird represents admixture, as does
a plant, as does a society, as does our historical trajectory, as does
this planet, and so on. At one level, these are all distinct. At
another level, they are instances of the same underlying pattern and
ultimately of pattern as such.

The Middle East is a place with an actual history and unmistakable
characteristics. Though it also is a metaphor for this commonality
through diversity. I have met people from so many different
backgrounds here. Greeks, Turks, Lebanese, Syrians, Palestinians,
Israelis, Egyptians, Iranians, Pakistanis... The list goes on. And I
have talked to even more of them through video calls.

If you are searching for reasons to differentiate people, then you can
go down to the individual. Each person has something unique to them.
And if you want to find similarities, you can easily look past
whatever differences. Analytically, both are correct. It then is a
matter of one's attitude in pursuit of a certain end.

I like to connect with people from diverse backgrounds. Unlike other
fellow leftists, however, I do not do it by denying my ethnicity. I
think that is a mistake because you then act like you do not have a
cultural background. You sit at a table with people from Northern
Europe, for example, and you immediately notice that your Eastern
Mediterranean zest does not fit right in. This is fine. My point is
that pretending it is not there is not a helpful starting point.

I acknowledge the fact of my Greekness yet do not let it become an
obstacle in the way of talking to others. This is why I mostly write
in English and why I want to be as good as I can in as many languages
as possible. Right now I am relearning French and will then brush up
on my Spanish. Long-term, I am interested in Turkish and Arabic, in no
particular order, since they are of immediate geographic relevance
where I am located.

Cyprus feels like home to me. This is where my land and my house are.
Though it also is a place that has a strong vibe of the aforementioned
admixture that the Middle East metaphorically captures. I am a country
bumpkin in these mountains, in many ways irrelevant to what is going
on around me, yet I still manage to continuously meet people from all
over the broader region and the world at-large.

It is nice when we play ball together. It is not nice when each wants
a ball to themselves.
