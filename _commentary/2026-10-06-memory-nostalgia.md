This is an entry from my journal.

* * *

Living in the present moment demands situational awareness. You pay
attention to your immediate surroundings and you notice changes in
your environment. Those you discern exactly because you have a mental
representation of the setting. Change is understood as the difference
between one representation and another.

When it rained heavily during this past Sunday's early morning hours,
the stream adjacent to my land brought down a lot of gravel and mud. I
have taken care to build infrastructure there with the intent of
making my areas less susceptible to flooding. My work has yielded
great results. I sleep peacefully now even during heavy storms.

Mountains erode naturally, even though human negligence can greatly
speed up the process. Over the course of millennia every existing
system on this planet will disintegrate, though the capacity for
integration will remain latent in its constituents.

Entropy does not undo systematisation. It does not stop the forces
that turned the originary star dust into bacteria, and dog, and plant,
and human, and Earth or, indeed, that gave that very dust its innate
pattern qua dust. Entropy is the same process of transfiguration that
engendered the forms in the first place, which remains in effect
ceaselessly, such that existing forms give way to forms-yet-to-be in
an ever-lasting flux.

It is, because it is becoming; it becomes, because it is.

## Presence with memory

Our experience is similarly one of being and becoming. There is no
fixed point to it. One may not cling on to their adolescence, for
example. What many refer to as the "good old days" are but a
representation whose traces remain imprinted in memory. It is not
their lived experience anymore as it does not form part of their
environment nor does it characterise their interpersonal relations.

Though memory is inherent to one's presence. It stays with them and it
produces consequences in their life, such as by underpinning
future-oriented projects or by determining how they choose among
mutually exclusive sets of outcomes. A memory, then, is ever-present.
What generated it may have transpired, but the memory itself persists
to condition each situation as it unfolds.

This is true for what is thinkable, such as feelings one has about
what their grandmother told them when they were little. It also is the
case for information that is encoded in the very fibre of our being,
unconscious to us yet definitive of our condition. Evolution
effectively is the compounding and subsequent reconstitution of
encoded memories.

That I am able to type this out is an indication of memory embedded in
muscle tissue. I do not actively think where to move my fingers as I
input the individual characters. While I am thinking through these
words, my focus is on their sequence and the meaning they form.
Because I have memory at the level of the muscles, I do not need to
expend my limited capacity for concentrated thought on the keyboard.
My attention remains undivided, directed at the topic I am expounding
on.

## The layering of memory

I speculate that whatever we consider as supreme skill effectively is
an extraordinary layering of memory. Talent, in this regard, is the
capacity to establish layers of automaticity which free the person's
attention from more basic tasks to then channel it towards higher
levels of complexity. When done to great effect, there is excellence.

I think as much because I know from my experience with football how
much knowledge is involved in each part of the process. Walking and
then running is already something that needs to be learnt. Keeping
your balance is a skill as well. Controlling the ball with your feet
is already a challenge unto itself. Knowing where you are on the field
is another difficult part of the sport that you have to develop. Then
figuring out where your teammates and opposition players are relative
to you and the ball. These and others are all improved through
continuous practice and the accumulation of experience.

The person with an elite talent, then, is one who incorporates inputs
so effectively that there is no waste in their efforts. They learn
quickly and integrate findings into their existing corpus of knowledge
to great effect. Furthermore, they are able to discern patterns among
the mental representations they have, such that they can better
predict outcomes. To continue with the example of football, you can
recognise a skilful player before they even touch the ball: they move
around with purpose, knowing where to be at each phase of play.

## The layering of memory down to the little things

Excellence thus manifests as situational awareness. It is about
understanding the prevailing conditions so well that you are no longer
inhibited by them. One may think of the surfer who succeeds only when
they ride the wave, but who otherwise experiences degrees of failure.
Excellence consists in one's alignment or harmonious adaptation to
their situation. The most excellent is the most aligned.

I can write regularly at length because of the aforementioned
layering. Writing is a form of collecting one's thoughts. This is the
case regardless of the topic and skill level. Every act of writing
involves focus, which only happens in a state of calm.

I have been practising all the little things for long enough that they
are embedded in my life as second nature. Each article flows naturally
and I am able to quickly put it together. This was not always the
norm. I remember when I first had to write a 2000-word essay for
college: it took me months to do it. Now I write as much practically
every day. And the quality is superior to what I could have achieved
back then. I quite literally am a different person in this regard.

The office setup is as I need it to be. The keyboard is configured to
my liking. My computing environment is optimised accordingly. My room
has plenty of light. It is quiet outside. I can listen to the birds
right now, while I can look at Olympos in the distance. There are no
notifications on my phone and I am not on social media anyway, so I do
not feel pressured to direct my focus away from the screen every few
minutes. Similarly, there is not much happening on my computer screen.
It is largely static and text-centric. All these in concert facilitate
uninterrupted creativity.

Though the tools alone do not make the artisan. There is also work to
be done at the personal level. On the technical front, this is about
practising the relevant skills, such as to read, to speak, to write,
to think things through. All have attentiveness in common. Then there
is the overall constitution of the person. Benign routines that are
stable contribute to a state of vitality.

This has been my normality for many years. It actually improves over
time in what may be likened to a process of compaction. Every single
day I wake up feeling energetic. My emotional state is stable as well.
I do not have mood swings. I do not feel demotivated one day and
excited the next. Reading something does not upset, anger, or
otherwise push me towards an extreme reaction. It might sound boring
in this regard, but I describe it as "what you see is what you get".
So this alignment I alluded to is uninterrupted all the way down to
who I am day-to-day.

It is not a matter of intelligence. Plenty of smart people feel they
are not in a position to control their attention and life in general
as much as they would like. Intelligence may affect the degree, such
as how quickly may one acquire knowledge. Though it does not remove
the need for layering. Again, I can think of the gifted kid who could
perform all sorts of tricks with a football, yet owning to the absence
of work on the fundamentals was not able to become a decent footballer.

What I describe here is about paying attention to the little things.
To take care of every part such that they all contribute to something
greater. Layering is akin to construction work. If you mess up the
foundation, then you cannot build something stable on top. It also is
like organising your room. If you misplace stuff, then whenever you
need to bring something new in you must first remove what you had
misplaced. Whereas putting everything where it ought to be saves you
from the future trouble and makes you more efficient as a result.

## From memory to nostos

This brings me to the Greek concept of "nostos" (νόστος), which is a
constituent of "nostalgia" (νοσταλγία). Nostos refers to one's longing
for home or, more generally, the want to be at the place where they
belong.

Nostos necessarily covers themes of place and of space. What qualifies
a specific location as home is that it exists in a wider space of
peace, or calm, or sustainability, or generally one or more conditions
which render it habitable, welcoming, interesting, and so on. There
can be no viable place absent factors of viability inherent to the
space.

The space that nostos describes encompasses people as well. If one is
thinking of their village, for example, they are factoring in the kind
of relations that are possible there, the familiarity with all the
locals, the attendant sense of kinship, the perception of a shared
destiny, and the resulting understanding of a common good that all
agree to and aspire towards.

The village itself is no longer home if, for instance, it is turned
into yet another theme park for tourists. There are no locals there
with whom to develop a community centred around such a common good.
The theme park attracts arrivists who come and go on the basis of
business incentives, plus those who are keen on a fleeting connection
with a simulacrum of local culture.

Wherein we discern the predictive power of memory. One may have an
intuition of home without ever experiencing it. They have a sense of
what would work for them as a space of viability and a niche carved
therein. Memory thus has a future orientation as well.

One may harbour nostos for a bygone era. They may also have nostos for
a potential state of affairs.

## Pain in general

"Nostalgia" incorporates a second Greek word as a suffix: "algos"
(άλγος). It literally means pain but can be more broadly understood as
malaise. Algos is used in medicine, such as "myalgia" (muscle +
pain-ness) and "neuralgia" (nerve + pain-ness).

Algos is thought of as something bad and it is indeed the case in the
context of medicine. Though pain as such is a messaging system, a
transmission mechanism to communicate some effect. If you are sitting
all day, for example, you may feel discomfort in your back. This can
develop into an injury, with varying levels of severity. What this is
telling you is that you need to change your ways. Do not sit for so
long. Maybe modify your desk setup. Check your physical conditioning.
Et cetera.

Pain is the messenger. If all you do is silence the pain, such as with
pain-killers, without addressing the reason it is there, then you are
shooting the messenger: the problem persists and will return to haunt
you.

In being a transmission mechanism, pain is neutral. It can still
communicate a sensation whose longer-term effects are benign, such as
the immediate discomfort of physical training which culminates in
longer-term health benefits if sustained, but which can still be
injurious if overdone.

## The pain of nostos

Nostos consists in memory of a past world or projection into a
possible future world. It can thus develop into a malaise when it
detracts or distracts from the present by means of contradicting the
prevailing conditions.

A person who is guided by nostos may feel unsettled where they are.
They may sense that the world around them is inimical to their stay
there. They may further believe that everything is falling apart and
there is no way to take control over one's milieu. Hence algos.

This is increasingly common nowadays for a variety of reasons, such as
the ongoing industrial revolution in the wake of Artificial
Intelligence. Plenty of jobs are becoming obsolete and many others are
being radically transformed. There is great uncertainty about the
nature of change and there also is concern about the pace of the
transformation. Everything seems to be breaking and it is happening
fast. A person may thus feel powerless in the face of magnitudes that
are far beyond their control.

In this light, nostalgia can turn into depression. It is the longing
for stability, which is so strong that it takes away from other
considerations, such as the need for adaptability.

Though ours is a world of admixture. Nothing is purely good or bad.
Whatever we get has elements of both. The result is thus subject to
the application and extent thereof. In other words, it is a matter of
degree. Even in this state of unrest, nostalgia in response to
apparent powerlessness can inspire a root-and-branch rethink of
priorities which can engender a bigger shift in one's values and
patterns of behaviour.

The individual may recognise the importance of community, for instance.
They understand that it is no longer viable for each individual to
live in their own filter bubble. They need to find the others and to
be collocated with them.

Pain, then, becomes the driver for thoroughgoing reform whose telos is
the cultivation of a sense of belonging, the formation of a shared
destiny with others, and the establishment of a place that has the
trappings of home.

Couched in those terms, it is a mistake to dismiss a view of the past
as a rose-tinted representation of the world, such as what we had
pre-AI. It is not about the past being faultless. Such is a strawman.
But rather to appreciate how nostos comes forth as the distillation of
the elements which are worthwhile even in the presence of the
potentiality for harm and abuse. Those are clarified as important and
then rendered in the conscience as irreducible components of a desired
configuration of factors.

Future orientation depends on the ability to target what is worth
having even in full knowledge of potential downsides inherent to it.
The person who finds negatives in everything will eventually become
nihilistic in their quest for perfection. Since nothing is ever going
to be flawless, they will end up with nothing they accept as valuable.
Nihilism become self-enfeeblement once it is totalised. It does so by
removing all motivation.

There is no perfect home. What matters is an approximation that moves
in the right direction. One understands that having a place where they
belong is better than not having any, despite what it may make
inaccessible. For example, to be home necessarily precludes the
adventure of not being home. To make that judgement call is the sign
of self-awareness, else maturity, and, more importantly, a
precondition for drawing delineations between home and all that is
non-home.
