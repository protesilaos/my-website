Think of yourself as a bundle of energy without a body. Your energy is
dispersed in all directions, like heat in the environment. It is not
directed towards a specific goal. This is mostly how you are as a
child. You are curious about everything around you. You touch it and
you want to taste it as well. Curiosity may eventually develop into a
more sophisticated form of expression. Areas of endeavour will capture
your imagination. You will want to explore the world in breadth and,
at least for a few things, also in depth.

This energy of yours will always be wasted or remain ineffective if it
is not channelled towards something in a laser-like fashion. Every
person does this to some extent. We impose our being on our immediate
environment. Our room is, at least in some way, an expression of who
we are and how we see the world. We change it to be more like us. We
impose ourselves on everything we do.

To occupy the safe space of a house, someone has necessarily displaced
other life forms from its midst. There is no human settlement that is
inclusive to all life, for that then is the wilderness. This I know
firsthand: I had to clear the wild vegetation and force rodents,
venomous snakes, spiders of all kinds further away from here.
Otherwise my room would be infested by forms of life that can kill me
either directly (e.g. a viper's bite) or indirectly (e.g. bats with
rabies).

Areas of interest are the same. We direct our energy towards them and
thus infuse them with our vitality. This topic I am expounding on, for
example, can be covered in many ways by different authors, but the
exact line of thinking herein has an unmistakable pattern that you
will discern in my other writings; a pattern of a common author; a
signature, as it were, that reads "Protesilaos".

The bundle of energy becomes capable at imposing itself when it is
given a structure, else a body. Though the body alone is not enough.
It needs to be complemented by patterns of behaviour that themselves
reinforce focus. It further has to be buttressed by relationships that
demand attention. Such is the extended structure beyond the body.

Absent the extended structure, the person becomes wasteful and
self-destructive. They will grow addicted to video games, for example,
thus leaving other parts of their life to atrophy. They will hop from
one project to the other, never accomplishing anything in the process
out of fear that such an eventuality creates commitments, and thus
they will refrain from not creating relationships that would demand
attention.

This extended structure involves other people. A person who is
solitary may seek a substitute for friendship or support in imaginary
bonds, such as intimacy with an AI bot, falling in love to a camgirl,
gambling, and the like. In so doing, they are a victim of a life
without supportive structures.

The extended structure is not limited to other people, however.
Someone who is committed to their garden, for instance, is necessarily
channelling their vitality accordingly. They have to keep doing it
indefinitely, for the garden will not sustain itself qua garden: it
will become the wilderness if left alone. Similarly, these can be
commitments to hobbies or to goods of some sort such as maintaining a
free software project.

Structure, then, consists of all the magnitudes that prevent,
dissuade, inhibit, influence, direct, frame, or otherwise condition
one's innate energy.

Every person needs structure in their life. Else they will fall into
wasteful and eventually self-destructive patterns of behaviour. For
some people this will come from their interpersonal relationships.
Their family and friends will be there to hold them accountable and to
nudge them towards benign activities. For others, this structure
largely comes from within as the austerity of discipline.

A highly sociable person will still benefit from some discipline, just
how a highly disciplined person benefits from sociability. No matter
where one is on this spectrum, they cannot avoid the mechanics of
energy dispersal and of energy direction.

## The unsustainable pace of instant gratification

A contemporary euphemism that people often admit to is "I am a
dopamine addict". What they really mean is that they are governed by
the desire for instant gratification. This is the common in the
multitude among all self-destructive activities. Think of drugs,
gambling, gluttony, and pornography, among others. This
"pornification", as I will be describing it henceforth, is edifying in
the moment at the cost of longer-term vitality.

Though instant gratification also extends to other media which have
effectively been pornified in the process: short videos that elicit an
intense emotional response, saturated flavours that bring everything
to the maximum (e.g. the common combination of grease, salt, and
sugar), constant loud noise which you get by default in cities,
perfumes and intense smells, over-the-top colouration, and even
exaggerated body parts such as muscles that require doping and boobs
that come from plastic surgery.

They all have the effect of dulling a person's response to stimuli.
The conscience of such a person is that of the artificially amplified
reality that can only be maintained by increasingly more intense
inputs. Everything is intensified and intensifying. It is louder and
faster to the point where it cannot be realistic anymore. It
effectively is the opposite of what one gets in a natural setting.

Such "dopamine addiction" is uninteresting and not gratifying
long-term owning to its shallowness and to the fact that one cannot
intensify towards infinity without suffering the consequences of poor
health, lack of concentration, and so on.

Interest that is sustained involves depth of understanding, which
presupposes focus, else structure. Such is gratifying long-term. It is
the gift that keeps giving, because there is always something to it.
It is sustainable, for its rhythm is consistent with the natural pace
of the human condition; the pace that we experience in the great
outdoors.

## Building up discipline from the little things

The disciplined person necessarily is the austere and stern person.
They have to be able to hold back and to say "no" to temptations. By
extension, they are neither a pushover nor a people-pleaser. They
cannot yield to the pressure of a group to, for example, do some drugs
together. That is not discipline anymore, but the beginning of a
descent into the slippery slope of self-harm.

Discipline starts with the admission that "no" is not bad per se even
when it runs counter to one's feelings. "No" often is the imposition
of structure, understood as restraint, against the want for energy
dispersal; a want that if left unchecked will unravel the structure
altogether.

"No" starts out as a private affair. You have to reach a point where
you are able to stop doing what you know is bad for you, bad for your
loved ones, bad for society at-large; "bad" because it wastes energy
and becomes self-destructive by extension (the ongoing decline of
Western civilisation is a direct consequences of its pornification,
for example---if you are not seeing this, either you are not paying
attention or are so deep into pornification that you cannot even
imagine a world outside of it).

This, however, cannot be done right away. One must build up the
capacity for it through tiny steps, which cumulatively create the
critical mass that produces what we describe as discipline: the
conscience of strict austerity.

In a world of persistent distractions, one begins to impose themselves
on their immediate environment through abstinence. Create short breaks
from the constant noise, such that you get used to what will initially
seem like silence and boredom but which actually is the baseline of
sustainability.

Spend 30 minutes in the morning drinking your coffee/tea/whatever or
having breakfast without engaging in any other activity. Do not use
the phone, do not play music, do not have the TV on. Make it as simple
as it gets: you and your immediate activity. This can be a social
event, though it is important to not have other intense stimuli involved.

Extend this practice to your other meals. Spend however much it takes
to eat food without any distractions. Do not eat in front of your
computer, for example. Switch off the radio. Be in a quiet place. You
get the idea.

In the beginning, those dishes will be relatively intense in their
flavours. Though you want to eventually move away from that towards
simplicity. You do not need everything to be maximally sugary, milky,
greasy, salty. If you can have a dish without extra sauce, for
example, then skip the sauce and work to uproot it from your psyche.

You can have "cheat days" in the beginning, if you think you cannot
live without them, such as a once-a-week indulgence. Though your goal
is to taper off, to gradually reduce the cheating until there is none
of it. If you can do it "cold turkey", as they say, then that is even better.

Introduce in your daily routine more such breaks from the noise. One
example is a brisk walk through a park or in the great outdoors. Walk,
do not run. Take the time to pay attention to the little things, to
the slow things, to everything that effectively represents through its
very mode of living a rejection of pornification.

Such is your new normal for a lifetime, hence austerity.

## Care and critical thinking

Structure consists in judgement. One must have a concept of the end
goal in order to pursue intermediate objectives. As such, they have
the capacity to determine what is a deviation from the goal. If the
goal is the lodestar, then we know when we are moving away from it,
given means for orientation.

Critical thinking is judgemental. This is already evident from the
word "critical", which is derived from "crisis", the Greek word for
judgement. We use "crisis" also in the case of an economic downturn or
an emergency because those too have a reflection on our moral
character. Maybe we were being greedy or careless, for example.

Crisis is a call for orientation. It is the need to find the fixed
point in the sky and proceed accordingly. By extension, critical
thinking is the skill of identifying patterns in thought that are
benign and separating them from those that are degenerative in the
consequences of their application.

The judge who upholds correctness, else the rule of law, over cancel
culture, for example, is effectively standing against the degeneracy
of indictments based on vibes. The judge has a longer-term
understanding of their thoughts because they factor in the
consequences of their conduct. Whereas the angry mob is governed by
their immediate impulses and unchecked passions, not recognising how
those engender tyranny.

Critical thinking is an indication of self-care. This is the case
since one must be able to evaluate their choices in accordance with
longer-term sustainability. The person who succumbs to the pressure of
instant gratification does not have the mindset to pause and to
consider things carefully, i.e. to judge them.

A child may exhibit some critical thinking, but is not there yet
because much of its life is being taken care of by its guardians.
Guardians in a society are the parents but also the various tutelary
figures. An adolescent who becomes an adult may still be a child well
into their 30s, for example, if most of their life is being taken care
of by some form of stewardship, such as school and college.

Such a person, now the adultchild, has not developed sufficient
capacity for critical thought, since they never had a need for it.
They never had to appraise the consequences of the dominant factors in
their life and, as such, they did not develop a keen eye for judgement.

The adultchild can only become more critical in their everyday
thinking by taking more control over their life. This starts with the
sort of small practices of self-imposition, else discipline, that I
covered above.

The adultchild is forever hampered in the rigour of their critical
thought for as long as they are operating like a child. Perhaps they
cannot have strong discipline, in which case they need robust
interpersonal relations to hold them in check and to push them towards
the essence of adulthood: duty.

## The two leashes of humanity

Pornification is an alias for recklessness. All that is pornified is
in disrespect of longer-term wellness. Exaggerated food destroys your
health. Exaggerated online content destroys your concentration.
Exaggerated consumption destroys your environment. And so on.

Historical societies had understood the pitfalls of recklessness. They
codified those as instances of hubris or sin and they organised their
social experience in a manner that kept people's self-destructive
propensities in check.

We can think of structure as a leash in the interest of simplicity.
Your dog is a good canine citizen when it is trained to not pull on
the leash. By extension, it knows not to rush towards excitement. From
there follow other markers of good training.

A person benefits from a leash in the same way. Historical societies
did so through the expectation of gendered duties. Males were on a
leash to control their innate aggression in favour of fatherhood; an
aggression that quickly degenerates into violent abuse and self-harm
if left unchecked. They were pressured to pursue activities with a
pro-social function, such as guards, priests, and coaches of various
kinds. Similarly, women were expected to concentrate on motherhood and
neighbouring activities (e.g. teachers, nurses) and to thus assume the
pro-social function of social reproduction, starting from the extended
family and extending to the community at-large.

Absent those two leashes, else the structure, society begins to
disintegrate. Its energy is dispersed because that is exactly what is
happening at the micro level of the individual. The man falls into
some sort of pornified addiction, as does the woman, as does the
child, as does the politician, as does the church at-large. From
there, they can only salvage the situation if they revert to strict
discipline. Else they are bound to be replaced by social groups that
retain their vitality, that do not disperse their energy, and that are
necessarily more austere in their lifestyle.

There are no shortcuts. Do not ignore the little things. Do not
underestimate how those activities that harm you one bit at a time are
enfeebling you and the world around you. Seek the slow-paced
experience. Learn to say "no" and be strict with yourself in order to
become a paragon of duty. Lead by example so that you may be strict
towards your peers as well. Be the change you want to see, else you
are a fraud. There you will find the personal and collective
empowerment of austerity.
