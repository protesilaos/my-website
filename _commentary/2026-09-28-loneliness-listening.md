The following is an excerpt from my journal.

* * *

I was discussing with someone how the interpersonal experience of
depth feels to me. Namely, how the deeper you delve into any given
topic, the fewer the people you will find around you who are willing
or able to follow you. And if you do find someone, their energy levels
do not match yours. This is the state of my loneliness that I have
alluded to several times before.

Unlike solitude, loneliness is not about the absence of people around
you: I do talk to others and have no issue socialising. If anything,
socialising is easy because I have diverse interests and activities I
am fairly competent at, such that I can eventually find something to
connect with others over. Besides, some people seem interested to
approach me for whatever reason, so this part is not a problem
overall.

Loneliness consists in the fact that you are alone in one important
aspect of yourself, peerless within the confines of your milieu.
Despite your best intentions and sincere efforts, you cannot bring
others into the inner sanctum of your world. Having recognised the
trend of what happens when you try, you no longer extend the
invitation. This is my intellectual side. I only have my writings to
share it with.

You know that you have become an alien in this regard. Though you also
suspect that you might have always been one: a dragon, a being that is
unmistakably recognisable yet shrouded in mystery. They can quickly
notice that you are different, though they do not know exactly what
that difference is about, nor can they fathom what it entails. You
embody the link between worlds, where the depth and the surface are
one, present in both of them though belonging to neither of them. Your
domain is that of liminality. The indeterminate does not disturb you.

This experience of loneliness may be why I have become a good listener
and, by extension, a competent conversation partner. Since I cannot
find another dragon around, I might as well give to others what I can
afford: a situation-specific peer.

Listening is an act of courage. You offer the other person that which
is most precious to you: your creative life force, your vitality. You
give them your undivided attention for an indefinite amount of time.
As such, you open your inner self to them. Though not to disclose
anything in particular but to become a receptacle for whatever they
may disseminate.

Courage is knowledge of your own strength. You know that you have the
requisite fortitude of character. Your emotional stability provides
you with a strong foundation, such that you may endure whatever comes
your way. Absent such knowledge, you are being reckless or naive as
you run the risk of getting shocked.

Courage also involves faith. You trust that the circumstances will
favour you. In this example, you believe that your interlocutor will
treat you with kindness. Or, in a different way, you wager that your
resilience will withstand their potential abuse.

Whenever I offer someone my vitality, I notice that their demeanour
changes. They are not as cautious even though we are strangers and
despite the fact that I have not shared anything meaningful about
myself. They still feel they are in a safe space. And indeed they are.
How could it be any different? As a dragon, I am the monster that all
other monsters are afraid of because their presence does not disturb
me. Is there anything more unsettling in this world than a being that
is not moved by your threats?

When I listen, I communicate calmness. I effectively say that
everything is going to be alright. I make it authoritative through my
demeanour, because I am stable.

Even within the first hour, people will switch gears from whatever
technical and impersonal issue they were expounding on to tell me
about their private affairs. I never ask such probing questions and do
not try to move the conversation in that direction. They simply switch
topics.

I think they realise that I have created a portal to some other
dimension that will not stay open for much longer. It is a rare event,
so they wish to make the most out of it, which they do by sending a
message across in the hope that something nice happens. They do not
even know they have this hope, nor are they in control of their
response.

The person being listened to feels transparent. They are not merely
seen. They are exposed completely. What remains is for them to admit
what is already available and, perhaps, to become aware of it
themselves. There is no filter that can distort their image. They
would not even want such an effect to exist. Masks are for hiding from
the many so that we can reveal ourselves to the few whom we trust.
Hiding is sometimes done through overexposure: it still presents them
with a distraction from what is kept inside.

When people tell me about themselves, I notice that they lose track of
time. This is the portal in practice: I have transported them to
another place where their worries are irrelevant. They leave them at
the door. They also lower their defences. What they tell me seems to
me like something they would not wish to share with some random guy,
though "random guy" is exactly who I am. But they still divulge
details that would be easily abused by a malevolent actor.

As such, listening takes more than personal ability. It depends on the
interlocutor's faith in your own benevolence. You reveal the dragon.
You give them the space to proceed. What follows depends on them.
