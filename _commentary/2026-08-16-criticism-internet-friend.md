This is an excerpt from my journal.

* * *

I have made a few friends online through my activities. Generally, I
care about deep connections: to talk about life and expand on shared
experiences. I usually do more of the listening, because that is who I
am, though still contribute with poignant remarks and relevant
commentary.

My interests are diverse, covering the full spectrum of human
experiences. I can connect on matters as far apart as mechanical
keyboards and dog training, physical fitness and programming, art and
farming. And because of my philosophical disposition, I always am open
to new ideas, plus I find connections in patterns that are not obvious
to others.

One time I was having a chat with someone who shared with me their
experience with psychedelics. I have never done psychedelics because I
never felt the need to. Though I am not against them in principle and
am always eager to give my undivided attention to any topic. What I am
against is abuse and recklessness: the freedom to ruin yourself is a
freedom I have no respect for. I am, if you will, opposed to
"heroin-ising the LSD in your ignorance" as Nikolas Asimos correctly
points out in one of his songs: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gW6ucfl36m4>.

As we were talking, I expounded on some of my thoughts about the
interconnectedness of the world. The person eventually exclaimed how
"I feel I am tripping right now". This is what you get when you let me
connect the dots from the most practical consideration to the greatest
abstractions. I probably never felt the need to do psychedelics
because I am like Obelix: I fell in the cauldron of Kykeon as a child,
so I have that perspective built into me ever since.

People need a safe space to speak their mind. They want to find
somebody who understands them. Open-mindedness is a good starting
point to feel the other person and relate to their lived moments. It
still takes courage to open up, as it requires the emotional maturity
to deal with feedback and potentially integrate it in your routines.

There is this common phrase nowadays that "I do not mean to criticise,
but...". It is a figment of political correctness and, when applied
consistently, a disservice to those you care about. Criticism is not
the same as shittalking or being argumentative: it is an informed
opinion that is framed in the context of your contribution. You
benefit from criticism as it helps you understand things from a
different perspective.

Indeed, "criticism" shares the same root as "crisis" and "critic",
which means "judgement". Judgement is prudential insight. As with
matters of law, a judge is someone who knows what they are talking
about. Some random fellow is no judge: they are a vociferous ignoramus
at best and when there are lots of them, you have the rule of the mob,
which is a form of tyranny and decadence (e.g. cancel culture).

Immature and mentally feeble is the person who cannot handle
criticism. Instead of listening, they argue back before you have even
completed your sentence. Instead of admitting to their shortcomings,
they double down on their incoherent thoughts. And when you ask them
to pay attention, they bring up their precious feelings.

Your vaunted feelings are your prison cell, friend. They confine you
to a tiny space that can only feed your fears. You have gotten so used
to that state of insecurity and attendant mediocrity to the point
where you consider it comfortable. You are not taking any opportunity
to grow and to become the most excellent version of yourself. Instead
of using your feelings as a hardened carapace, learn to recognise
things as they are. You do that by being attentive. This is the
prerequisite to expanding your reach. As such, there are people who
proceed through action and there are people who have feelings.

This is not about lacking emotion and about becoming this caricature
of a man with a stiff upper lip. No. It is about understanding that we
sometimes grow attached to the wrong things and that in those cases we
fail to identify the corruption that gradually occurs in our midst
like rot under the carpet.

The best kind of criticism involves emotional intelligence: you know
what to say and when to say it. Sometimes the medicine will still be
bitter. It does not matter if you dislike its taste though as it still
cures you.

If your friend is never giving you feedback that would qualify as
criticism, then they are not your friend. They may be your colleague,
your teammate, your talking buddy. Those are all fine. But they are
not friends. A friend shows love. Love makes no discounts both when it
is gentle and when it is tough.

By the way, "philia" (φιλία), the Greek word for "friendship" is a
specialised form of "agape" (αγάπη), which is why "philo-sophia"
(philosophy) is sometimes translated as "love of wisdom".

Coming back to the Internet friends, the medium is digital though the
attitude is the same. I have not met any of the friends I have online
and might never do so. Though I still treat them with care. One such
friend was defending their dissertation the other day. I attended the
video call, even though I have no face-to-face connection with this
person or any of their family. I was among the handful of friends and
relatives who were in the audience. And I did it at an hour that was
inconvenient for me. My friend told me after the fact how they drew
strength from our presence there and how that helped them do what they
ought to.

A friend sometimes says nothing. Their deeds do the talking. You know
that for as long as I can, I shall. And I will be there to inspire you
to reach your highest peak. Why? Because I care about you in particular.
