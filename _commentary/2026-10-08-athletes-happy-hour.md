This is an entry from my journal. It is about pacing ourselves and
maintaining a balance in our life.

* * *

There are so many things I have learnt from football in no small part
because I was paying close attention the entire time. Your physical
and mental states must be in harmony, otherwise you are thrown off
balance.

A physically strong person becomes imbalanced easily if they have a
short temper, for example. You can disturb them with just a few words.
Then they do not have the emotional stability to resume what they were
preoccupied with. Governed by uncontrollable anger, they will do
something that will come to haunt them later. Over time, this
imbalance will be ruinous to their life. As such, their strength does
not contribute to their empowerment, at least not to the extent it
otherwise could. It becomes a curse, even when it appears to be a
blessing.

Same principle for those who have mental fortitude, such as a
combination of patience, curiosity, and intelligence, but fail to take
care of their body. Eventually they get tired very easily. They can no
longer sustain their concentration on a given task. Their brain is
foggy and their excitement is snuffed out. What they do becomes sloppy
and disconcerted. Their stress levels are high at all times and reach
critical levels under duress. They thus find themselves in situations
where they feel disoriented, overwhelmed, and defeated.

The gist is that you cannot be in control of yourself in phase one and
of yourself in rapidly evolving states of affairs in phase two while
mind and body are not cooperating with each other. The imbalance will
hinder your potential.

## Imbalance can happen to anyone

You are well positioned onlye when there is concordance of mind and
body.

Balance of this sort describes the baseline of the person. It is about
who they are day-by-day. Yet even a balanced person can be thrown off
balance under certain circumstances. A case of force majeure is not
only excusable but also the kind of scenario that does not necessarily
break one's resolve. Whereas yielding to the temptation of an easy way
out sets in motion a process of moral erosion, of the person's
character gradually disintegrating unless through active intervention,
and of them eventually failing to keep up with the rigours in their
life.

In quotidian affairs this happens when we start having "cheat days"
where we suspend our rules for no principled reason. There is the
appeal of immediate gratification which nevertheless comes at the
expense of longer-term instability. We think we can bargain with
daemons, only to learn that we are puny humans. You cannot have a
night of heavy drinking with the lads and expect to be performing at
your peak the day after. And, depending on the frequency of such cheat
days, you will not be performing well for the entire week and beyond.

Even absent moral degeneracy, imbalance can occur in the wake of
mindless application. To use an example from football, you exert
yourself to chase after every lost ball even though it is clear you
will not reach it. It is passion that drives you, which is a powerful
motive for competitiveness and a key contributor to peak performance.
Though inconsiderate passion becomes wasteful. You cannot keep chasing
lost balls over the course of 90 minutes and still have the tenacity
to be involved in every phase of play. It then is important to pace
yourself. To know when it is not worth doing something which is
otherwise beneficial to you. Let the ball go out of play and conserve
energy for when the time is right, such that you affect the match to
the maximum of your ability.

If you do not pace yourself, then you will be thrown off balance, in
the sense here considered but also literally by being pushed around
and outmuscled more easily. Your tank will be depleted earlier and you
will be running out of breath. This, in turn, will make your brain
more hazy. You will not see clearly. Everything will become a blur as
you will not have the attentiveness to discern patterns in your space.
You will not estimate correctly how far or near the opponents are. You
will not have an accurate model of where your teammates are moving
towards. You will not be able to discern the passing lanes and the
openings for an opportunity at goal. In short, your body will be
sabotaging your mind because your mind, by allowing an excess of
needless action, created conditions for the body to fail in. Such is a
vicious cycle of discord between mind and body that will make you lose
control of the situation.

## Pacing oneself for the long-term

Balance over time is thus a matter of pacing. You cannot sprint your
way to a marathon run. The only way to do it is by committing to a
relatively slow and steady pace. Even then, you have to make that your
normality for a long time, such that your overall life is one of slow
and steady experiences. You cannot be impatient and also work towards
becoming a competent marathon runner. It will not work, because the
moment you are on the road, your impatience will compel you to give up
and to then shift your attention to whatever your immediate desire is.
Competence in running involves skilfulness in taking it slow, and the
other way round, which are both developed one tiny step at a time.

The athlete gives us a model of the person who enjoys gratification
over the long-term. There still are the moment-by-moment ups and down,
but the trajectory is one of controlled satisfaction, of vigour within
constraints. It is not extreme and so it does not oscillate from the
zenith to the nadir. It consists in a state of moderation between the
notional extremes.

One does not need to be an athlete to appreciate the life lessons of
sport. Indeed, sport is so didactic that the Greeks consider it their
holiest of holidays, hence the Olympic Games and other such events. By
reifying the deed in religious terms as respect for the divine, for
that which is transcendent and thus true throughout, we clear the
space in order to embed in the collective conscience important life
lessons, such as perseverance, honesty, life as a struggle (agon), and
the understanding that nice things do not come about easily nor do
they stay with us indefinitely without dedication.

An important aside here. In Greek, we do not use the term "game" but
"agon", which describes a trial that involves struggle: it demands a
high level of effort and skill. By extension, the player is not a
"gamer" but an "agonist" (also found in the words "protagonist" and
"antagonist"), else the person who proceeds through demanding and
skilful effort. The point is that this is not a casual activity that
anybody can engage in on a whimsy, the same way e-sports no longer
represents your generic video game experience: each e-sports event is
an "agon" as well. The athlete has a complete lifestyle that allows
them to engage in their agon. They are not just showing up during the
game while otherwise not practicing the requisite skills: that will
quickly lead to their disqualification.

## Life as sustained effort

We start failing in life when we forget about the lessons that sport
offers us. Pacing is the most common. We sometimes want to get the
nice things too quickly, for example, so we develop what effectively
is a lazy attitude of seeking shortcuts towards every goal. Maybe one
or a few shortcuts represent shrewd choices, but once everything is
done that way we fail to build the underlying skills and, more
importantly, we do not cultivate the ethos of the agonist. The
relevant faculties are left to atrophy. This is exactly why laziness
(sloth) is a sin: you lose what it takes to remain competitive in the
agon of life.

If, for instance, you become conditioned to relying on Artificial
Intelligence for every marginally difficult task, then you will 100%
lose sharpness of mind quite soon, to the point where you will need AI
for basic tasks as well. Absent that support, you will be on the verge
of a panic attack in the face of even a mild challenge, as you will be
clueless on how to proceed, nothing will seem to work, the perceived
friction will be too high, and you will feel you are not in control of
the situation. Hence, imbalance of the sort here considered.

Pacing also concerns the things we otherwise do well. For instance, we
are meticulous in our work and attentive to all of its details. We
effectively are the model professional. Yet we forget that life is
like a football match, in which you have to remain competitive for all
90 minutes. You cannot perform well in the first half and expect that
alone to carry you to a win in the second half. If you are not
competitive in the latter, you will definitely lose whatever you had
gained. Similarly, the model professional has to preserve their
vitality long-term by avoiding burnout.

If life is all about work, or productivity more broadly, then it
reaches a point where it cannot be productive anymore. Such is the
reality of our condition where even the most benign thing becomes
deleterious when taken to an extreme. Too much liberty becomes slavery
to the impulses. Too much security becomes the tyranny of caution. Too
much fun is not engrossing anymore and leads to depressive withdrawal.
And so on. Even water, the source we cannot live without, is literal
poison at exorbitant levels of consumption.

The person who only knows how to do work eventually loses their
ability to cope with the challenge. Think again about sport. The
athlete is fully aware that rest is as important as exercise. Rest is
not optional nor does it take discounts. You cannot improve
performance by training ceaselessly. You have to take breaks and to
singularly commit to the task in equal proportion. Without rest you
will definitely sustain serious injury and, more generally, will
become imbalanced.

## The practice of pausing

Which brings me to the point of the "happy hour". This is not
necessarily a exact hour, but a fair amount of time that we reserve
for doing something that contributes to our wellness; something that
is not work or productivity per se. For example, to enjoy a meal
without sitting in front of the computer. To have a cup of tea without
letting music play in the background. To watch and listen to a friend
without reaching for the phone. To go for a walk and not play any
podcast along the way.

I have long now introduced a happy hour to my mornings, where I will
spend at least 30 minutes sitting at the table outdoors and doing
nothing in particular. I do it even on cold days by wearing extra
layers of clothing. The only exception is when it is rainy, though I
will still do the same indoors, while looking through the window. I
observe the surrounding nature. Not only is it breathtakingly
beautiful. Exposure to it energises me. It is one of the reasons why I
continue from strength to strength, but also why I designed my house
to optimise for openness and sunlight. The open vistas, already
witnessed while standing by my desk, inspire me to remain resolute in
a world of unfathomable possibility.

Here is a picture I took the other day which captures the essence of
the happy hour. The table is one of those generic plastic ones that I
could cover with my budget. I collect the prunes and the figs from my
land. I had rescued their respective trees from the overgrowth of the
wilderness and have since taken care of them. It is sobering how
controlled violence---because it takes originary violence to displace
or otherwise harmonise life forms, whether you like it or not---is a
precondition for sustainable coexistence within a space of safety.

I allow myself the chance to not be productive in the moment, to not
check my emails, to not be preoccupied with anything that does not
form part of my immediate experience. I slow down on purpose. I choose
the pace and thus remain in control of my life. I align myself with
nature and, as such, reinforce the alignment that exists within me as
one of balance between mind and body.

To some, the café frappé, which by the way is a mark of my irreparable
Greekness, is a proxy for negligence and the easygoing life that
teeters on irresponsibility. But once you understand who you are
dealing with, this symbol of pause is a representation of authority,
of structure that is secure in its constitution, of stability throughout.

I have met many Greeks, and Italians for that matter, who feel ashamed
about the stereotypes fastened upon the construct of their
nationality; stereotypes about the casual life («σιγά-σιγά», literally
"slowly-slowly") and the dolce vita. Yes, the extreme form is
undesirable, but knowing how to enjoy life, which is how you pace
yourself for the long-term, means that you will be doing it well in
the moment and throughout the years.

## It is not late to change your defeatist attitude

In a balanced life, vitality will remain your defining feature while
those around you will feel depleted. All the Greeks who have
internalised racism and thus devalue their culture tell me how "we are
old now" even though they are still in their 30s. No, "we" are not in
this together, my friend. You may be a dead man walking because you
have long now lost sight of beauty, due to your imbalance, but I have
not even peaked yet.

Absent the pacing, you quickly turn into a jaded has-been who trades
in nothing but name-calling and virtue-signalling. You rely on those
to obfuscate the fact that you have been wasting your years in a state
of mindlessness, grinding away like an idiot for the benefit of some
project you never really liked to begin with.

I remain at your disposal and will be there for you if you choose the
right path. Though I stand my ground, even if I have to do it against
the rest of the world.

The happy hour empowers me to pursue my interests with a beginner's
enthusiasm and a master's poise. This is the sportsman within who
understands why the sense of agon is a divine matter.

Such is the spirit I embody: a hint of a greater power. It is imposing
like the peak of Olympos, pristine as the shallows at Antipaxos,
awe-inspiring akin to the Santorini caldera, microcosmic yet
inexhaustible just how Cyprus, the island of the goddess of love,
reveals itself incrementally only to those who have the requisite
playfulness. It is the Mediterranean zest I bring with me as a
reminder of what you could have been, had you been paying attention to
beauty.
