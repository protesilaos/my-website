This is an entry from my journal. I comment on the psychosynthesis of
the bully.

* * *

Reading the BBC's article with the title [How to spot a psychopath at work - from a man who worried he could be one](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy8e3vjexj0o)
made me think of my time at the European Parliament and of the human
nature I have been so attentive to all this time. The world of
politics does not have anywhere near as much money as bankers do,
though it has formal power and enjoys great prestige. Those create in
people certain expectations and thus amplify specific aspects of their
personality that can be anti-social.

From the BBC:

> "The city [meaning London's finance sector] rewards this dark triad
> behaviour... being uber confident, being able to control and
> manipulate others, not getting emotionally involved in decisions and
> being decisive," he said.

I think it actually comes down to a single core quality, which is the
lack of integrity throughout. It has to be systematic. This is what
makes a person manipulative. It also is where this "uber confidence"
comes from, because it is the capacity to peddle lies knowingly. From
the systematic lack of integrity also comes emotional detachment in
terms of not caring about consequences. The manipulative person only
serves their own interest. They exhibit no commitment to any statement
or cause beyond their self: that is how they operate. As such, they
necessarily have no commitment towards other people.

There are degrees, of course, though systematicity is necessary. At
its extreme end the lack of integrity speaks of a character who will
do anything to advance their agenda and take no accountability
whatsoever for their actions. This is the person who will give you a
warm hug, followed by a passionate kiss, and then a stab in the back.
They will find what you need, give you enough of it or engender in you
the hope that you will acquire it, and then instrumentalise that to
control you. When you are of no service to them, they will betray you
outright.

The lack of integrity is an anti-social trait even when the person is
not as confident or decisive. Or, to put it differently, confidence
and decisiveness do not make for an anti-social person. Those are
actually the traits you want in a friend, for example, especially if
they are combined with kindness. Granted, a master manipulator who
shows confidence and is skilful is extra dangerous.

## The insecurity of indeterminacy

In modelling the world, we abstract away the details to make sense of
the underlying mechanics. In other words, we simplify. Consider a
simple distribution of competences into three groups: low, medium, and
high. Those at the lower and higher ends will typically be aware of
their strengths or weaknesses and attendant placement in the
distribution. The reason is that their cases are easier to notice. The
person who is somewhere in the middle has a more challenging task to
figure out where they are along the distribution. They could be a 4
but they could also be a 6 out of 10. There is more doubt there.

From awareness comes acceptance, which begets a sense of stability.
Awareness for those in the middle, however, creates an immediate
concern as it brings to the foreground this indeterminacy. They are
effectively asked to resolve it, but they likely do not have the
experience to do so. More so since this happens from an early age. As
such, the person who finds themselves somewhere in the middle
experiences, at least to an extent, insecurity. They can work to
overcome it, but the point is that the insecurity is present.

Every person can find themselves in this situation. It is the
intersection of awareness, inability to find answers with the given
means, and concern about the implications. What does it mean to be
somewhere in the middle? Indeterminacy is unsettling even in
intellectual terms. We have a tendency to have an ending to our
stories. Cliffhangers bother us. But why? I think it is due to said
intersection.

## From common insecurity to systematic belittlement

While feeling unsettled in the face of indeterminacy is common, there
is an extra step before it forms the basis of a bully's patterns of
behaviour. The bully is the average person in the aforementioned
distribution whose solution to indeterminacy is the belittlement of
those who rank lower than they do.

A typical form of bullying that does not involve coercion is to make
fun of someone who relative to the bully is less wealthy, less smart,
less good looking, and so on. It always is directed towards those whom
the person considers as inferior to their own and is systematic in its
application.

The bully wants to invent or identify their inferiors, in whatever
metric is expedient, and will shift the goalposts accordingly, in
order to confirm in their conscience that they are not below average.
They do so as a matter of first priority.

You can identify a bully in how they incorporate information about
another person in the conversation they are having. They introduce
this person and immediately say something about them which counts as a
grave fault or irreparable frailty. For example, "I know Protesilaos:
he is thoughtful, but he is poor". Here the reference to one's poverty
is not relevant to the discussion and need not be stated at the
outset. Yet it comes at the beginning to establish the baseline of
expectations and thus to put the bully in a relatively superior
position.

The bully is signalling a want for reassurance. They are asking to be
told that what they have is worthwhile. It is not evil that drives
them, but fear.

Watch out for this pattern and you will realise how common it is.
Though it is not systematic in all those cases, otherwise everybody
around us would qualify as a bully. Though pay close attention and you
can spot the bully through the systematicity of this pattern.

Fear is not a weakness per se. It is response mechanism. It
communicates a message. How we handle the fear is what speaks to our
character.

The bully is effectively a coward. They are not only governed by fear
but are also trying to externalise it and to weaponise it. They turn
it into fuel for belittling others. They are cowards for not wanting
to face their own insecurity and then to work towards resolving it.

Every person can reach a point of stability through acceptance. They
have to be honest about their own condition and not seek to compare it
to that of others in a positive or negative light. In other words,
they need to be descriptive, without prescriptive undertones.

Cowards can appear confident. They will put up an act, where they are
loud and boastful. By verbally or physically abusing others they will
try to give the impression of toughness. More so if they can make a
show out of it, to advertise to the world just how capable they are.
They are, in effect, saying and doing it in the hope of tricking
themselves into believing it. But they never do, otherwise the remedy
for bullying would be bullying.

## Punching down and kissing up

Their abuse will always be directed towards their perceived inferiors.
To those whom they consider superior to them, even when they dare not
admit as much, they will show a completely different face. They will
be friendly, charming, and even exhibit a willingness for servitude.
It will be exaggerated, as if something is not right about it. We thus
use the expression "to punch down and to kiss up".

Someone punching down is obviously a reason for caution. Though beware
of the person who kisses up, for they always punch down.

Now an example from football to describe a bully in action. There was
a guy in my neighbourhood who fancied himself as the strongest around.
Me and friends of mine were athletes but we never made a big point
about our fitness. One time we played a friendly match (i.e.
off-season) where that guy was on the opposing team. We were
outclassing them but were otherwise casual about it.

At some point a teammate of mine gets the ball off that guy with a
clean challenge. He turns around, pushes my teammate, and then
exclaims exactly this "you also are tough? I fuck you all in here"
(«είσαι και παλικάρι; εγώ σας γαμώ όλους εδώ μέσα»). My teammate
responded calmly and we ignored him.

He then kept trying to hit us instead of playing the ball. At some
point after tolerating his dirty game, I turn around, push him on the
chest with force, and he falls to the ground. I get to his face while
he is still confused and shout aggressively "what do you want,
feeble-minded, what do you want?" («τι θέλεις, μαλακισμένε, τι
θέλεις;»). After that he calmed down and nothing happened.

[ An aside about "feeble-minded". In English this sounds
  intellectualist, but in Greek the word "malaka" (μαλάκα) and
  derivatives are very common. This is a variation of the word for
  soft and is used to describe someone or something that is soft in an
  undesirable way. Though malaka is used more casually to call a
  friend's attention, so it is not negative in such a context. ]

Later he was prince charming to everybody and never again did he play
against us. Maybe that event broke his propensity for bullying, so it
served him well long-term. Or he found other ways to channel his
resentment. But in the moment at least, he exhibited the behaviour of
wanting to punch down to reassure himself that his self-image was
correct, only to realise he should be kissing up instead, which he
resorted to right after.

As for violence, it is neither good nor bad. Those who glorify,
demonise, ignore, or underestimate it will be its victims. Same with
everything in a world of admixture, as I have explained at length many
times before. One must be prepared. It is a matter of how it is used:
the application and the degree will make it abusive or not. Controlled
use can be benign. It is how a safe space is established, after all.

## Advantage at the personal level

Bullies will not be as obvious, however. They may wear nice clothes,
look wonderful, and talk in polite ways with all the trappings of
etiquette. You can still spot them by paying attention to their
patterns of speech, where they keep alluding to specific people whom
they in fact loath for their perceived inferiority, even though they
are not relevant to the topic at hand.

Furthermore, these subtle bullies will default to devaluing what you
do or represent. They operate by shifting the goalposts. One way to
achieve that result is to state something vague, which can then be
redefined willy-nilly. More so if this gives them an immediate
advantage over you.

For example, "Protesilaos is a poet; poetry is lame". By dismissing or
trivialising what you do/are they are again setting the baseline in a
manner that is advantageous to them. What does it even mean for poetry
as such to be lame? And what even is the problem with that, anyway? As
soon as you pause to work within their framework, you are playing
their game.

If you ask for them to elaborate on what exactly they have in mind,
they will refrain from doing so. They will instead claim to have made
a mistake or tell you how this does not matter after all. Remember
systematicity though. A genuine mistake is fine, but they will keep
showing this behaviour over and over again. They seek that advantage
at the personal level, to make you think that there is something to it
after all, which is the key here.

Disapproval is not bullying per se. It can be done with rigour, in the
form of an analysis, to explain how or why something is undesirable in
general. But in the bully's case, there is no rigour, no depth, no
analysis that applies beyond their immediate want. It consists in
name-calling like "this is lame", which itself is malleable to always
fit the rules of the game that the bully chooses to initiate.

For example, I am against clickbait tactics. It is one of the reasons
I produce long-form content and why I avoid knee-jerk reactions. My
rationale is that clickbait forces things to be superficial, which in
turn reinforces the worst traits in people, which is turn destroys
nuance, which in turn makes us less thoughtful, which begets anger,
frustration, and conflict.

Mine is a case of judgement, of understanding the state of affairs,
and of thinking through the implications of any given position. In so
doing, I am not seeking an advantage against any specific person
because I am not shifing the goalposts. I go to great lengths to
explain what I mean exactly so that the goalposts cannot be shifted.
Thus I stand by what I claim and am eager to comment on it further.

Pay attention to the goal, which is control at the personal level.
Look for the tendency to divert attention, to say something flagrant
only to take it back immediately. As always, this has to be done
systematically. A one-off, for whatever reason that may be, is a false
positive.

## Common tricks of emotional manipulation

Which brings me to the experience of life in the world of day-to-day
politics. As I noted above, politics does not have the money that goes
around in financial circles. Though it has power and prestige ex
officio. Those can and do encourage people to adopt anti-social
patterns of behaviour.

Based on what I observed, politicians are less likely to be bullies.
In the aforementioned simplified model, they are the achievers who do
not feel the need to prove that they are achieving something. There
may still be those among them who have great ambition coupled with
deep-seated insecurity, though the average parliamentarian is fine in
this regard.

Where we find lots of bullies are in the assistants to the politicians
and in the wider world of civil society, else lobbying. Those are the
functionaries who are close to power but are not powerful themselves.
They need to remind everybody of their acumen, their academic
credentials, the money they earn, the connections they have, and so
on. This is where we begin to discern the propensity to single out the
ostensibly inferior people at times where that is unwarranted.

In pointing towards their inferiors at the earliest opportunity, those
bullies will use names and have some actual person in mind. For
example, they will not talk about poverty in broad terms as a systemic
phenomenon, but only as it is expressed through the vices of a given
figure that they know. And then their talk about poverty is not for
the sake of doing something about it, but only to then juxtapose it
with their own state of being.

Bullies are the situational psychopaths. If they think they can get
away with abuse, they will employ it, like the tough guy I covered
above. Except in politics they will do it while also professing their
undying devotion to some lofty standard of morality.

European politics is especially suspect in this regard, because in
Europe many decision-makers live in a fantasy land where they believe
to have a sophisticated civilisation while the rest of the world is
backward-minded. No, I am not referring to some extremist ideology,
but to the run-of-the-mill Europeanist and milquetoast centrist.
Whereas, say, politicians in Cyprus know that Cyprus is a small
country with little influence on international affairs.

This, too, by the way, is the figment of the bully's mind: to depict
other cultures as inherently inferior, in order to prop up what they
have.

If outright abuse is not an option, bullies will operate with guile.
Emotional manipulation thus unfolds. One such trick is through
instilling in you a sense of guilt. It is most effectively when you
cannot change what they are pointing towards or you have to do
something extremely demanding to remedy the situation.

By telling you how something unchangeable you have is undesirable,
they pressure you to accept your inferiority. Once you internalise
that thought, they no longer appear to you as bullies but as normal
people who have an apparent concern for your wellness; so normal that
you may even think they are benevolent.

Racism is the most common form of induced guilt, even though we can
think of it in different dimensions as well. Its goal is to make the
person feel bad about their innate characteristics, like their skin
colour, facial features, and cultural background. Racism is inducing
guilt because if it succeeds in convincing its targets to internalise
its message, then the oppressive superstructure it conceals is no
longer seen as oppressive in the eyes of the oppressed, but as the
ordinary form of social organisation.

Guilt from the outside exploits a person's desire to fit in. It also
feeds off a person's want to excel at what they do. Such a person will
thus internalise the message more easily and then use it as the basis
for their own self-criticism. It is no coincidence that many people
will be racist against their own people, for example, or immigrants
will have strong anti-immigrant views. They have internalised racism
as the objectivity of things.

Another common trick of emotional manipulation is mixed signalling.
Its goal is to create uncertainty, much like the shifting of the
goalposts, which eventually makes you rationalise your doubts as your
own shortcomings. That makes you vulnerable and open to being
controlled.

Mixed signalling happens in many interpersonal affairs. A common one
is in what can appear as on and off romance. It consists in a needless
gentle touch that is accompanied by suggestive language that seem to
appear all of a sudden. It then turns into an expression of distance
and apparent professionalism. The objective is to create a hope and an
expectation through which the person becomes controllable. The giving
and the taking away is a kind of entrapment.

There are other tricks, such as denying any wrongdoing or making you
doubt your own recollection of events. Fishing for compliments, asking
for validation, making tacit threats. Though those are easy to catch
and thus not very effective in isolation.

Badmouthing someone is also very common. Though this one is not
directed towards you. It is a form of conspiracy: to make others turn
against you. But you yourself are not effectively caught in the trap
of committing self-harm, so you can notice how you are being the
victim in such a situation.

## Integrity as a strength and a weakness

I entered the world of politics as an outsider. I got invited to work
for a politician who was reading my blog and who thought I would be of
use in their office. I did not have any connections, was not a member
of some party or organisation, did not partake in activism, and was
generally doing exactly what I do now: write with clarity.

I think I did well in all my office tasks, such as to write internal
analyses about a political situation, to table amendments to
legislation, and to contribute to relevant research. Those are not
interpersonal skills. Quotidian politics is more like gossip than
philosophising. You have to connect with people, to give them
something in exchange for something else, and to always maintain that
web of dependencies.

Whereas I am a one-man army who will not try to depend on anybody and
who will do my own thing at all times. I quickly realised this made me
unfit for the world of politics. Fundamentally, I am too honest and
too loyal, and probably too capable for my own good.

This is a blessing and a course. On the positive side, it makes me
speak my mind in earnest using tools I have developed carefully and
which I continue to sharpen (e.g. this article). I am not trying to
mislead anybody. I will take the time to expound on my thoughts
because (i) this is how I can understand them better and (ii) I may
filter out the kind of person who is not willing to engage with my
work in depth.

By filtering out those people I effectively guarantee that I myself
will not fall into the trap of appealing to an audience for the sake
of gaining some material advantage. I am not here to make friends. If
that happens, it is a happy coincidence. I am here to express what I
am working on. I enjoy the process.

It is also why I do not keep track of any analytics on my website and
why I do not share my publications on social media or Reddit. I do not
want to be influenced by external factors. Same for doing videos only
when I feel like it, without tricks and gimmicks. These are my genuine
thoughts, which I have considered carefully, and which I pronounce
responsibly to the best of my abilities. It is why I do it in public
with my real name as well.

I want to communicate in earnest because this is what motivates me. It
is how I became a philosopher, after all, given no formal background
in the field. I am interested in depth of understanding and will
entertain every idea seriously without rejecting it at the outset.
Whatever I do, I do it carefully, giving to it my undivided attention,
out of a sense of wanting to identify in it the qualities it has
beyond what it renders apparent.

This, however, means that I am prone to emotional manipulation. Or, at
least, I used to be before figuring it out and paying closer attention
to it. You would show me a friendly face and I would be willing to run
through a brick wall to defend our friendship. So if you were fooling
me, you were already making me do something that I would not have done
under different circumstances.

It is not the same as a people-pleaser. Such is the person who will
adapt to their social milieu like a chameleon. They are not doing it
maliciously: they just want to fit in. Whereas I am fine in being a
misfit if it means that I remain honest, hence the one-man army
attitude I have. My case is about being loyal and expecting
reciprocity. It is the expectation that would fail me. As such, my
loyalty would be expressed as a kind of naivety, else foolishness.

You are a fool if you are expecting friendship in the world of
politics (and the same for any environment where people can advance
their career by throwing you under the bus). You might make friends,
because there still are kind people working there, though if your
disposition is towards loyalty then you will quickly be taken
advantage of.

The weakness I had is also not the same as being a pushover. I am
effectively the opposite of that because I do not budge from my
position for the sake of sociability or amiability. I only change my
mind in the face of compelling arguments brought forth by another or
discovered through further personal inquiry. Granted, if it is
something I do not care about, I will not argue for it.

## You know them from when they are young

These are identifiable in people from an early age. You will find them
in personal dynamics at school, for instance. I never was the super
popular kid, for example, because I am not handsome and charming. But
I was always well received within each niche I occupied: such as sport
and motorcycles.

I was the defiant boy you find cool exactly because your parents and
teachers are not allowing to be as wild as he is. But you still do not
see at especially appealing. Being at the periphery was fine as I was
doing my own thing with such dedication that you could not make fun of
it: others would call you out for being silly. Whereas being at the
periphery while trying to do what the popular kid does will make you
look desperate.

Change the details and you realise that the same is happening today. I
frequently get feedback along the lines of how inspiring I am to
someone. I understand it at the rational level, though I cannot feel
it because to me this is my normal state of affairs. I keep being
myself then and hope for the best.

Some people, well men to be precise because only men are curious about
this specific topic, even want to live my lifestyle and ask me for
tips. That is the part that your upbringing forbid you from
approaching: the untamed expression to do what you want because you
have concluded that it is right for you to proceed thus.

What I do is not especially challenging in technical terms. Though it
requires steel at the emotional level. You have to set your
expectations accordingly and be patient throughout.

To my mind, I am average in terms of strength and skill. Where I excel
is in the robustness of my character combined with the capacity to
dive deep into something.

To live the way I do, you have to accept that you are not involved in
whatever the centre of attention is. Put differently, you embrace
irrelevance. You liberate yourself from the want for relevance and in
so doing you free up your energy, which you may then direct towards
whatever little thing you are interested in, such as to write.

This naturally means that you will probably be alone as well, but that
you would be experiencing already if you were doing it. Last year I
earned close to 13k EUR, while the minimum wage is 1k EUR. You do not
actually want to live like me. If you like what I do, it is that same
sense of admiration you have for tribes in the rainforest while you
live in some apartment downtown.

You cannot relate and are underestimating how you need to build up the
capacity for frugality if you have been raised in an environment of
abundance. In effect, you have to rewild yourself, which takes time
and effort. Though, yes, anybody can do it, male or female: it is how
people used to live for millenia until very recently.

My life is not harsh in material terms. It is austere, yes, but I am
not suffering. In effect, I have turned "less is more" into a lifestyle.

## The failure of schooling

Which brings me to the point of education. At school, students learn
plenty of tangentially useful technical details through memorisation.
Those are designed to make them "book smart", else to create in them a
misplaced confidence in textbook versions of the given field of inquiry.
They are not interested in cultivating a sense of "street smart"
because that is how you begin to think critically about matters beyond
the narrow confines of your specialisation.

If anything, knowing about emotional manipulation would help with
friendship, dating, marriage, and parenting, which is potentially
beneficial to practically everyone. You will be better prepared to
spot the early signs of a bully and choose to avoid them while you
can. In so doing, you will be giving negative reinforcement to the
bully, to not resort to their usual tricks, which can be a benign
corrective for them long-term.

Actual psychopaths are a different kettle of fish, however. Schooling
will not change them. Though it will prepare others for what they are
capable of. In most cases, what we consider a psycho actually is a
bully in a certain situation, as I have described herein.
