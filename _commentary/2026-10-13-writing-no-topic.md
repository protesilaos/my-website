This is an excerpt from my journal in which I comment on creativity.

* * *

How do you write when you have nothing to convey? What is the first
word that comes to mind? Which phrases follow from there to form a
paragraph? Is there a wider point you are making? Are you effective at
communicating it?

Writing is as much as matter of skill at the given moment as it is a
reflection of one's state of being. A competent writer needs to be in
a certain mood to produce a noteworthy result. They should be feeling
alright to commit to the task. Their environment and overall lifestyle
must be conducive to writing.

To the reader, writing is about the production of a piece of text with
some innate qualities, such as the ideas it communicates, the feelings
it engenders, and the story it describes. The text provides a message
and elicits a response. Readers receive the culmination of a process
whose workings they are otherwise oblivious to. They do not gain
insight into the prerequisite state of being.

Creativity demands focus. One has to collect their thoughts and direct
their vitality towards the given task. In effect, the creative process
is the channelling of energy. In so doing, the creator becomes the
created, ceteris paribus, for they establish an organising principle
around which their perception and ultimately their body revolves. The
consistent creator is partly remade in the image of their creation.

The author cannot hide their selfhood indefinitely. Even when they
write with a pseudonym and even if they never state anything about
their private life, they still reveal information about their state of
being. They are telling us about their ability to focus. They are
giving us a clear signal that they have honed the skill of harnessing
their power. If they are consistent in their output, they are further
informing us that their lifestyle and/or living conditions provide at
least a modicum of stability.

These are not mere reasonable assumptions. One does not become a
creator by accident nor do they maintain their level out of talent
alone.

Talent without application does, ipso facto, speak of a person whose
life is missing something important. Maybe they lack the self-esteem
to put the final touch on their works. Perhaps they have an
aggrandised sense of their achievements, which makes them complacent,
and takes away their drive. They may have taken on too many
responsibilities that they cannot manage while also being creative.
Their overall lifestyle imbalances their body and emotional condition.
We can think of many such explanations. The point, though, is that
talent alone does not take anybody as far as they might think.

Creation consists in convergence. The author qua author is inwardly
and outwardly balanced. There is no disturbance within them, of the
sort that can interrupt their thinking processes. And there is nothing
in their immediate environment that creates distraction or disruption.

The creator effectively travels through mindscapes. Each time they
focus, their immediate environment becomes a distant backdrop of
abstract patterns. It is as if they enter another dimension; a
dimension that is accessible only to those who practise the art of
controlling their vitality.

In traversing the mindscapes, their thoughts become fecund beyond
their own capacity of expression. They simply do not have enough time
to elaborate on the breadth and depth of what they have discovered,
even if their body and mind are in full concordance. What they carry
within is a profound impression, the awe that arises from the
realisation that no matter how prolific they are, they can never
capture the full extent of what they are being exposed to.
