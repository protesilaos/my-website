---
title: "About the ethnic Greek rite I attended"
excerpt: "I joined a group of polytheists and describe my experience."
---

Yesterday I attended an ethnic Greek (i.e. polytheistic) rite. The
event took place at the outskirts of Lemessos (Limassol), on a hill
overlooking the Mediterranean. There were about twenty practitioners,
while I was told that there are many others in different parts of
Cyprus who could not join us for one reason or another. I took photos
for my personal archive, which I will not share because I did not ask
for the consent of those present. There is only a selfie that I have
published: <https://protesilaos.com/selfies/2026-07-26-reaching-seaside/>.

The rite itself was modest. I think this is how rituals ought to be,
to direct people's attention towards the substantive teachings rather
than distract them with the glitter of material riches and the
inevitable greed those engender.

We found a rock on which we placed all the gifts people brought. I
offered samples of the two types of sage I have right in front of my
room (salvia officinalis, salvia fruticosa) as well as some lavender.
Others made offerings of wine and fruits. All gifts are symbolic,
therefore it is appropriate that they are unassuming. Again, gifts
that are spectacular misdirect the attention towards themselves,
cultivate materialism, and ultimately idolatry.

The priests read out Orphic hymns. Going off of memory, those were
addressed to the goddess Hera, the goddess Nike, the goddess Hestia,
the goddess Athena, the goddess Mnemosene, the god Apollon, and the
god Zeus. Most of the hymns were read in ancient Greek. There was also
a modern Greek version. We even had hymns in Italian, to accommodate
the Italiots present there. This, for me, is an important detail
because it shows that we are interested in the substance, not the
surface-level features of the hymns, such as that they supposedly
ought to be read out in their original tongue.

Ethnicity is about the cultural framing of the people. It is a state
of mind. One's bloodline is not a predictor for what kind of person
they will be. There are plenty of Greeks "by blood" that are not
Greeks culturally, for example. And there can be biological non-Greeks
who embed in their quotidian life the values of Greekness. This to me
is what ethnicity is about, as opposed to nationality: the latter is a
legal arrangement, while the former is a worldview that manifests as a
way of living. To this end, I am Greek because I live the Greek life.

I had the chance to talk with everyone and introduce myself. In a
small religious community like this one you get the sense that you are
one among equals. I had in the past tried to join a more established
group, which I do not want to name here, where I felt I was not wanted
because I did not show up with fancy clothes, shiny shoes, and so on.
Those other groups are de facto materialistic and idolatric even
though their claims are to the contrary (and I would say the same for
Greek polytheists who shifted their focus on the magnificence of
temples, the quality of marble statues, and so on, which made them
greedy and distracted them from spirituality). In that established
group, it is a common secret that if you pay more, you enjoy greater
approval. Their rituals are contests where people compete with each
other over prestige and social standing. In this small community I am
accepted for who I am, not rhetorically through virtue signalling but
practically through camaraderie.

This is what I want to get out of such religiosity: the community of
equals. I do not need anyone to tell me about the divine or, indeed,
anything at all. I am the theologian here, I am the philosopher that
will flesh out the relevant corpus of thought. I am the luminous star
that emits light. Thinking things through is the easy part for me. And
I realise that I am mistaken in even calling myself a "philosopher"
because people assume that I am an instructor who presents the ideas
of Platon, Aristotelis, and so on, or that I am interested in talking
about the various schools of thought. No, I am an original thinker and
I only speak for myself without appealing to anybody's authority. I
neither accept nor need such authority. The descriptor I should apply
to myself is not important right now because, anyway, people who have
not dived deep cannot estimate and thus appreciate how deep those
obscure waters are. What matters are the deeds. I always talk through
my actions. And I have a lot to do.

Going forward, I will meet again with those people. It is difficult
for me to travel around the country, but I will do my best whenever I
can. The gist is I am keen on contributing to the common good and I
met people who have the same outlook.
